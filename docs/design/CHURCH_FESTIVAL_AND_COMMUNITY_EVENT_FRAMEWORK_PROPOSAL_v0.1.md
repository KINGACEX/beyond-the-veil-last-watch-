# BTV — Church Festivals, Community Celebrations & Recognition Events
## Design Framework Proposal v0.1

**Status:** PROPOSED / OPEN — design framework only; not locked canon  
**Domain:** Church life, civilian community life, calendar events, character continuity  
**Authority:** Subordinate to the BTV Master Canon and the Church / HCI / Service / Communion Bible  
**Related character reference:** Kay Family Character Bible and Ellen DW Naming Addendum

---

## 1. Purpose

Define a safe, coherent design framework for Christian festivals, church celebrations, community events and recognition ceremonies in *Beyond the Veil: The Last Watch*.

The aim is to make churches and communities feel inhabited throughout the world—not to turn festivals into reward dispensers, faction systems or compulsory activities.

This document does not approve a specific event calendar, service order, named festival programme, award title, recipient or real-world institutional representation.

## 2. Canon boundary

### Established foundations

- Churches are living communities, not factions or Covenant facilities.
- Church life includes worship, teaching, fellowship, service, ordinary work, relationships, celebration, grief, disagreement and care.
- The Church and Covenant remain distinct. The Covenant does not govern the Church.
- First Sunday Communion is established; exact presentation and service order remain open.
- KITFIT is an established pre-preaching segment involving Scripture and a short teaching/message about giving; its acronym expansion remains unknown.
- Giving is voluntary and must never purchase blessing, divine favour, spiritual status or gameplay power.
- Easter, Christmas and other Christian/community festivals are possible world events, but their implementation, frequency, service content and significance remain proposed/open.

### Non-invention requirements

- Do not invent official HCI endorsements, leadership, doctrine, policies or institutional claims.
- Do not invent a locked calendar or exact service order.
- Do not assume every church celebrates every event in the same way.
- Do not fabricate Scripture, Christian doctrine, liturgy or quotations.
- Do not treat a festival as a Covenant operation by default.
- Do not make attendance compulsory or punish players for missing an event.

## 3. Event categories

These are design categories, not a finalized calendar.

1. **Christian calendar observances** — possible examples include Easter and Christmas. Their theological meaning must be handled accurately and respectfully.
2. **Congregational celebrations** — anniversaries, thanksgiving gatherings, milestone celebrations or other locally established occasions.
3. **Community festivals** — regional cultural, civic, seasonal or neighbourhood gatherings, designed with local context and without implying that every culture is identical.
4. **Service and outreach events** — food support, practical assistance, visiting people, community restoration and other forms of service.
5. **Recognition and appreciation gatherings** — occasions where a community may publicly thank people for meaningful contributions.

The existence, names, frequency and exact content of each event remain OPEN until separately approved.

## 4. Event design principles

Every proposed event should answer:

- What is the event's purpose for the people holding it?
- Why is it taking place at this location and at this point in the story?
- Who participates, including ordinary civilians and families?
- What preparation and follow-through happen before and after the gathering?
- What can the player observe, join voluntarily, or respectfully decline?
- What changes in the community afterward?
- Does the event remain believable without the player?

Events should have a life beyond the player character. NPCs may prepare, attend, serve, talk, celebrate, disagree, clean up and return to ordinary routines.

## 5. Recognition and awards — proposal

Recognition ceremonies may be used in some church or community events, but they are not yet established as a universal BTV church practice.

### Appropriate recognition themes

Potential recognition may acknowledge:
- Consistent practical service.
- Community care and support.
- Responsible stewardship.
- Reliability and teamwork.
- Courage or assistance during a specific incident.
- Long-term contribution to a community.

These are illustrative categories only. Exact award names, criteria, presenters and recipients remain OPEN.

### Theological and ethical safeguards

- An award must not claim to measure salvation, holiness, divine favour or a person's closeness to God.
- No award should imply that wealth, donations, public visibility or social status make someone more righteous.
- Recognition should not replace sincere service or turn helping people into a competition.
- A person may decline public recognition.
- Do not make the player grind for church awards or treat them as spiritual progression.
- Recognition should be proportionate, contextual and believable; many meaningful acts may never receive a public award.

## 6. Ellen DW — Dewealth Easter egg integration

Follow the separate **Ellen DW Naming Addendum**.

- **Normal display name:** Ellen DW.
- **Reserved reveal:** “Dewealth” may appear naturally in family-house scenes or church-event scenes.
- A church-event context may include a festival, celebration or recognition ceremony where a host, presenter or participant naturally uses her full family name.
- If she is being introduced or recognized, the name can be spoken as part of that moment.
- This is an optional, occasional Easter egg—not a mandatory beat at every event.
- Do not assume Ellen receives an award. If a future approved story gives her recognition, the reveal may fit that scene.
- Do not use the reveal to imply spiritual superiority, a special divine status or an official HCI position.
- Outside the reserved contexts, use **Ellen DW**.

## 7. Event lifecycle — proposed production model

A reusable event may be authored through these stages:

1. **Preparation:** NPCs organize the space, communicate, arrange supplies and make practical plans.
2. **Arrival:** attendees arrive according to believable routines; some may be late, absent or occupied.
3. **Gathering:** the event begins with context-appropriate activity. Do not assume one universal service sequence.
4. **Participation:** worship, fellowship, service, celebration, conversation or recognition occurs as appropriate to the approved event.
5. **Personal moments:** optional character interactions may occur without forcing exposition or player participation.
6. **Close and cleanup:** attendees depart, volunteers tidy the location and organizers follow up.
7. **After-effects:** relationships, community needs, NPC schedules or local conditions may change in grounded ways.

This lifecycle is a design pattern, not a prescribed church liturgy.

## 8. Gameplay boundaries

- Attendance is optional unless a specific story mission has a justified narrative reason to be present.
- No attendance streaks, guilt prompts, spiritual score, faith meter or divine-favour currency.
- No Communion buff, loot or XP.
- Voluntary giving remains voluntary and is never tied to awards or blessings.
- Any material reward must have a clear, ordinary narrative source and must not be presented as God's payment.
- The event should remain meaningful even if the player does not attend.
- Player actions may affect trust and relationships through credible conduct, not through a hidden holiness score.

## 9. Accessibility and world simulation

Potential implementation considerations:
- Provide readable event information without requiring the player to attend.
- Allow the player to arrive late, leave early or observe quietly where appropriate.
- Avoid blocking essential missions behind calendar events.
- Support NPC attendance variation and persistent routines.
- Use region-appropriate clothing, architecture, language and community customs only after their local context is established.
- Keep crowd sizes and performance requirements appropriate for Android-first development; prioritize meaningful NPC behaviour over sheer crowd count.

These are production recommendations, not existing implemented features.

## 11. Dependencies and review

Before any candidate becomes canon or is implemented, review it against:
- BTV Master Canon.
- Church / HCI / Service / Communion Bible.
- Faith, Prayer and Armor of God reference.
- Story & Lore and Character references.
- Covenant separation and authority boundaries.
- Regional worldbuilding and NPC routine rules.
- Android performance and accessibility requirements.

## 12. Open decisions register

- Which festivals and community events are actually included?
- Which locations host them?
- What is the event calendar and how does it relate to story time?
- Which congregations or community groups hold each event?
- Are recognition ceremonies used, and if so, under what circumstances?
- What are the approved recognition categories and criteria?
- Who presents or receives recognition in any specific story?
- Which family-house scenes should contain the Dewealth Easter egg?
- Which church-event scenes, if any, should contain the Dewealth Easter egg?
- What event content is story-critical versus optional world activity?

## 13. Acceptance checks

A proposed event fails review if it:
- Confuses the Church with the Covenant.
- Claims real-world HCI endorsement or invents official institutional facts.
- Treats attendance, giving or awards as a route to divine favour.
- Turns Communion into a gameplay buff or reward.
- Makes the event mandatory without a justified story reason.
- Presents unapproved festival details as established canon.
- Uses Ellen's Dewealth reveal constantly or outside its approved contexts.
- Makes an award proof of spiritual superiority.

**Final status:** This framework organizes future design work. It does not lock festivals, calendars, service orders, awards, recipients or individual event scenes.
