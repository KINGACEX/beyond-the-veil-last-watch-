# BTV — Annual Community Calendar Framework Proposal v0.1

**Status:** PROPOSED / OPEN — planning framework only; not locked canon  
**Domain:** World time, church life, regional culture, community events and NPC continuity  
**Authority:** Subordinate to Creator decisions, BTV Master Canon, World & NPC Bible, Church / HCI / Service / Communion Bible and the Church Festivals & Community Events Framework  
**Implementation status:** NOT IMPLEMENTED

## 1. Purpose

Create a coherent way to plan recurring and one-off events across BTV's interconnected world without forcing every region or community to follow one identical calendar.

The calendar should make the world feel lived in. It must support Christian observances, congregational life, regional and local cultural events, public/community activity, family occasions and story-driven events while preserving cultural accuracy and player freedom.

This document does not approve specific dates, festivals, congregations, traditions, event programmes or story scenes.

## 2. Calendar layers

Use separate layers so one event system does not flatten the whole world.

### Layer A — Shared world-time framework
A common passage of time may help coordinate travel, NPC routines, weather and story continuity. The game's exact calendar, year length, time scale and date display remain UNKNOWN until approved.

### Layer B — Christian calendar observances
Potential events include Easter and Christmas. Their inclusion, dates, local presentation and service content remain PROPOSED / OPEN. Any implementation must be theologically accurate and reviewed against the Church and Faith references.

### Layer C — Congregational calendars
Individual congregations may have their own anniversaries, service patterns, thanksgiving gatherings, outreach plans or community milestones. Do not assume that every congregation follows the same programme.

**Established boundary:** First-Sunday Communion is an existing BTV church foundation. Preserve the already approved context and safeguards. Do not invent a universal service order or assume every congregation has identical practice.

### Layer D — Regional and local cultural calendars
Regions and communities may hold locally meaningful cultural, civic, seasonal or neighbourhood events. Their inclusion must be grounded in the specific location, people and history.

For Ghanaian settings, research relevant local traditions and community contexts before selecting events. Do not treat Ghana as culturally uniform or use festivals as decorative proof of representation. Apply the same research standard to every international region.

### Layer E — Community and service calendar
Possible events include volunteer days, practical support, rebuilding, local markets, school activities, health/community initiatives and recognition gatherings. Each remains optional design content until separately approved.

### Layer F — Personal and family events
Birthdays, family visits, reunions, milestones, remembrance and other personal events may appear where supported by character biographies and story continuity. Do not invent dates or family histories simply to fill the calendar.

### Layer G — Story-driven events
Mission incidents, Covenant briefings, local emergencies and recovery periods can affect schedules. Story events must not overwrite established observances or make ordinary community life disappear permanently.

## 3. Recurring versus one-off events

Every event record should identify its recurrence type:

- **Recurring:** repeats according to an approved schedule, such as a weekly or monthly routine.
- **Seasonal:** occurs during a defined local or world-time season, once the season model is approved.
- **Annual:** occurs once per approved calendar year.
- **One-off:** tied to a specific story, community need or personal milestone.
- **Reactive:** triggered by a credible world-state change, such as a disruption or recovery effort.

Do not mark an event as annual merely because it is a festival in real life. Confirm its local practice, setting and intended in-game role first.

## 4. Scheduling rules

Before an event receives a date or recurrence, establish:
1. Which calendar and time model the game uses.
2. The event's location and local community.
3. Whether the event is recurring or one-off.
4. The source and reason for its timing.
5. Who organizes it and who plausibly attends.
6. What happens before, during and after it.
7. Whether it affects ordinary NPC routines or story continuity.
8. Whether the event can be missed without penalty.

Exact dates remain OPEN until the BTV time model and relevant local context are established.

## 5. Regional variation and cultural accuracy

- A shared world may contain different regional calendars and observances.
- Not every region celebrates every event.
- Similar event names do not imply identical customs or meanings.
- Local characters should explain or participate in their own community events naturally; avoid using one NPC as the spokesperson for an entire culture.
- Ghanaian and international characters should appear naturally throughout BTV, consistent with the Global Character Naming & Cultural Representation Framework.
- Do not create a Ghanaian event list and an international event list as two isolated halves of the game.
- Do not assign events to a location only to satisfy a representation quota.
- Research local names, language, history, religious context, clothing, music, food and ceremonial practice before authoring culturally specific content.
- Where credible sources or consultation are unavailable, keep details general or mark them UNKNOWN rather than inventing them.

## 6. Event lifecycle and persistent world response

An event may use the following production lifecycle:

1. **Announcement and preparation:** organizers and participants make believable arrangements.
2. **Arrival and participation:** NPC attendance varies according to work, family, travel, health, personal choice and story conditions.
3. **Event activity:** the content follows the approved purpose and local context.
4. **Close and cleanup:** organizers and participants complete practical tasks.
5. **After-effects:** schedules, relationships, local needs or community state may change where justified.

Events must continue to make sense if the player is absent. NPCs should not wait indefinitely for the player to start or finish community life.

## 7. Player agency and safeguards

- Optional events remain optional.
- No attendance streaks, guilt prompts, spiritual score, faith meter or divine-favour currency.
- No rewards for giving, Communion or worship.
- No essential mission should be locked behind an optional festival or community event.
- Do not punish players for missing an event because of travel, mission timing or ordinary gameplay.
- If a story-critical event requires attendance, the narrative reason and player choice must be explicit and proportionate.
- Respectful observation, early departure or non-participation should be supported where the event design allows it.

## 8. Continuity and technical considerations

- Store event timing separately from dialogue and presentation.
- Use stable event identifiers and explicit recurrence metadata.
- Avoid hardcoding real-world dates into unrelated mission scripts.
- Ensure time skips, travel and save/load do not duplicate one-off events or erase completed community state.
- NPC schedules should degrade gracefully on Android; prioritize believable key behaviours over simulating every attendee.
- The calendar must not become a hidden quest checklist or a source of constant notifications.
- Event completion should not create unearned knowledge for NPCs or characters who were not present.

These are proposed production requirements, not implemented systems.

Review each future calendar entry against:
- BTV Master Canon.

- Faith, Prayer and Armor of God reference.

## 11. Open decisions register

- What is the in-game calendar system and year length?
- How closely does in-game time map to real time?
- Which Christian observances are included, and how are they represented?
- Which congregations have distinct recurring schedules?
- Which regions and local communities are established first?
- Which Ghanaian and international cultural events are appropriate for those specific locations?
- Which community events recur, and which are one-off?
- How do travel, time skips and mission states interact with events?
- Which events create persistent world-state changes?
- Which events are story-critical, optional or ambient?

## 12. Acceptance checks

A calendar entry fails review if it:
- assumes one universal culture or event schedule;
- invents a date, tradition, church programme or institutional fact as canon;
- conflicts with the established First-Sunday Communion boundary;
- turns worship, giving or attendance into a reward system;
- uses Ghanaian or international events as quota-filling decoration;
- forces the player to attend optional events;
- breaks mission, NPC or save/load continuity;
- makes the world revolve around the player.

**Final status:** This framework organizes future calendar design. It does not lock an in-game calendar, event dates, festivals, congregational schedules or individual event scenes.
