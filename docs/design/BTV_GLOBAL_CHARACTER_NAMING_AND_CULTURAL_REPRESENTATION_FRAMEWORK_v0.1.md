# BTV — Global Character Naming & Cultural Representation Framework v0.1

**Status:** CREATOR-CONFIRMED DIRECTION / DESIGN FRAMEWORK  
**Scope:** Global character creation, NPC populations, naming, casting and regional representation  
**Authority:** Subordinate to explicit Creator decisions, the BTV Master Canon and specialized character/world references  
**Implementation status:** DESIGN ONLY — this document does not add named characters or alter locked story content.

## 1. Purpose

Establish a consistent approach to Ghanaian and international representation throughout *Beyond the Veil: The Last Watch* (BTV).

BTV should contain a genuinely varied international cast. Ghanaian characters must be included meaningfully, alongside characters from other national and cultural backgrounds. This must emerge naturally from the world's people, places, histories and relationships—not from an arbitrary numerical split.

## 2. Creator-confirmed direction

- Include Ghanaian characters across the wider BTV world.
- Include a diverse range of international characters.
- Do not divide the cast into a Ghanaian half and an international half.
- Do not impose a fixed 50/50 ratio or another arbitrary nationality quota.
- Do not make every character Ghanaian, and do not make Ghanaian characters rare or token appearances.
- Representation should be distributed naturally across the world, with each character's identity grounded in their own context.

## 3. Naming principles

A character's name should be plausible for that individual's family, language, cultural background, personal history and setting.

When creating a name:
1. Establish the character's intended background and family context first.
2. Research spelling, naming conventions, pronunciation and cultural meaning where relevant.
3. Avoid combining unrelated naming traditions merely to make a name sound international.
4. Do not assume that a person's name alone proves nationality, ethnicity, religion or birthplace.
5. Allow for migration, diaspora, adoption, mixed heritage and family naming choices when the character's own biography supports them.
6. Keep names readable and distinguishable in dialogue, subtitles, mission records and UI.
7. Avoid repeatedly assigning one naming pattern to every character from a country or community.

Names are not substitutes for characterization. Characters must have individual goals, relationships, strengths, limitations, occupations and agency.

## 4. Representation across the world

Representation should be considered across:
- ordinary civilian populations;
- families, neighbourhoods and local communities;
- schools, hospitals, workplaces and public services;
- churches and community organisations;
- travel routes, visitors and displaced communities;
- Covenant personnel and support roles;
- specialists, leaders, agents and teammates;
- regional storylines and optional missions.

No one region should carry the entire burden of representing a culture or nationality. Equally, do not insert characters into a location without a credible reason for their presence.

## 5. Natural distribution — no quota system

There is no target percentage for Ghanaian or international characters.

For each region and cast group, writers should ask:
- Who plausibly lives, works, studies, worships, travels or serves here?
- What local and wider connections explain the population?
- Are named characters varied without becoming a checklist?
- Are any backgrounds repeatedly omitted, stereotyped or overused?
- Does the distribution make sense for this particular place and story?

A population review is a quality check, not a quota-enforcement system. Do not change a character's identity solely to satisfy a numerical target.

## 6. Character integrity and cultural accuracy

- Characters must not be defined only by nationality, accent, food, clothing or cultural references.
- Avoid caricature, exoticisation, one-dimensional villains and assumptions about ability, wealth, education, morality or faith based on nationality.
- Do not assume all Ghanaian characters share one language, ethnicity, region, class, worldview or Christian tradition.
- Do not assume international characters are culturally uniform.
- Use local consultation and reliable research before writing culturally specific ceremonies, dialect, history, religious practice or community customs.
- Distinguish a character's personal beliefs from the beliefs of an entire population.
- Cultural identity must never be used as a shorthand for righteousness, corruption, danger or spiritual maturity.

## 7. Ridgeview continuity — protected slice boundary

Ridgeview Outpost remains governed by **BTV Vertical Slice Specification v1.0** and its approved slice-level case design.

The currently established named Ridgeview characters remain:
- **Dorian Pike** — local leader.
- **Wren** — frightened resident who wants to leave.

The Scout, Covenant duty officer, visitor and repairman remain unnamed by design in the locked slice specification. Do not assign them names through this framework.

Do not rename Pike or Wren, add a new named Ridgeview resident, or alter their established roles merely to demonstrate cultural representation. Any additional named Ridgeview character requires a separate, explicit slice review against the locked specification.

The wider BTV world must include Ghanaian and international characters naturally, but the first Ridgeview slice is not to be rewritten to meet a quota.

## 8. Character review checklist

Before approving a new named character, verify:
- [ ] Name and spelling have a credible basis.
- [ ] Background and personal history are internally consistent.
- [ ] The character has a purpose beyond representing a nationality.
- [ ] Their knowledge and dialogue fit their lived experience and role.
- [ ] Cultural details have been researched where needed.
- [ ] The character is not a stereotype or a demographic token.
- [ ] Their presence fits the region and story.
- [ ] Their status is labelled CANON, CREATOR-CONFIRMED DIRECTION, APPROVED SLICE DESIGN, PROPOSED or UNKNOWN as appropriate.
- [ ] Existing locked characters and story boundaries remain unchanged.

## 9. Status boundaries

**CREATOR-CONFIRMED DIRECTION**
- Ghanaian and international representation across BTV.
- Natural distribution rather than a forced half-and-half split.
- No fixed nationality quota.

**DESIGN GUIDANCE**
- The naming, cultural accuracy and review principles in this document.

**UNKNOWN / NOT ESTABLISHED**
- Exact global population percentages.
- A complete country-by-country cast list.
- Every region's demographic composition.
- New named Ridgeview residents beyond the locked slice roster.
- Specific nationalities, biographies or naming histories for characters not yet established.

## 10. Acceptance criteria

This framework is being followed when:
1. Ghanaian characters are present meaningfully across the wider game.
2. International characters are represented across the wider game.
3. No arbitrary 50/50 split or nationality quota is used.
4. Character names and cultural details are grounded in individual context.
5. Regional populations remain coherent and varied.
6. Existing BTV canon and the Ridgeview vertical-slice boundary are preserved.

**End of framework v0.1.**
