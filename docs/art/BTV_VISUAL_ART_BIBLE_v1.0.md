# BTV VISUAL ART BIBLE v1.0
**Project:** Beyond the Veil: The Last Watch  
**Date:** 6 November 2026  
**Status:** APPROVED DESIGN DIRECTION — PRODUCTION ART RULES PROPOSED FOR REVIEW  
**Authority:** Creator decisions and BTV source-of-truth documents take precedence. This bible does not override canon.

## 1. Purpose and status

This document establishes the visual identity and visual-production rules for BTV. It translates the creator-approved direction into guidance for concept artists, character artists, environment artists, animators, lighting artists, technical artists and developers.

The approved direction is **Cinematic Semi-Realism**:
- Hybrid visual language: polished character presentation informed by Wuthering Waves; environmental detail and cinematic composition informed by Black Myth: Wukong; grounded atmosphere and world-building informed by The Witcher 3.
- Semi-realistic characters with natural proportions, readable silhouettes, detailed but controlled materials and expressive performance.
- A living, semi-open world made of interconnected regions and settlements.
- Cinematic atmosphere and lighting.
- Hybrid camera and animation presentation.
- Quality-led graphics with scalable settings, Android-first and PC later.
- High visual ambition pursued through staged, measurable production.

The named games are study references, not asset, character, story, interface or world-design templates. BTV must remain original.

Status vocabulary:
- **APPROVED DESIGN:** creator-approved direction.
- **PROPOSED STANDARD:** production rule defined here; may be refined through prototype evidence.
- **UNKNOWN / BLOCKED:** requires creator decision or target-device evidence.
- **NOT IMPLEMENTED:** documented target not yet present in the game.

## 2. Creative identity

### 2.1 Visual promise
BTV should feel grounded, mysterious, human and cinematic. The world is recognisably modern and lived-in, with spiritual conflict conveyed through narrative context, character response, environmental changes and restrained visual storytelling—not through spectacle in every scene.

The ordinary world must remain credible. Peaceful community spaces, homes, roads, schools, hospitals, settlements and wilderness should feel inhabited and regionally coherent. Supernatural or Veil-related presentation must be purposeful, legible and restrained.

### 2.2 Reference-game study matrix

| Reference | Study for | Do not copy |
|---|---|---|
| Wuthering Waves | Character presentation, stylized realism, animation fluidity, colour and atmospheric landscapes | Character designs, UI, lore, silhouettes or signature effects |
| Black Myth: Wukong | Environmental detail, material richness, cinematic framing and visual scale | Characters, mythology, creature designs, effects or proprietary assets |
| The Witcher 3: Wild Hunt | Grounded atmosphere, regional identity, quest-world coherence and environmental storytelling | Narrative structures, monsters, symbols, UI or distinctive art assets |
| Assassin's Creed Shadows | Seasonal variation, weather, architecture, stealth readability and movement presentation | Historical setting, character identities, symbols or signature systems |
| The Last of Us Part II | Facial performance, grounded movement, emotional staging and environmental detail | Characters, scenes, story beats or protected assets |
| Red Dead Redemption 2 | Persistent-world ambience, settlement life, NPC staging and environmental continuity | Characters, locations, missions or proprietary assets |
| Horizon Forbidden West | Vegetation richness, terrain readability, material layering and world composition | Machines, silhouettes, lore, UI or signature designs |
| Uncharted 4 | Camera composition, cinematic transitions and authored traversal presentation | Set pieces, characters, locations or scenes |

Reference images may be used for internal analysis only when their source and permitted use are recorded. Never trace, extract or ship copyrighted game assets.

## 3. Visual hierarchy and composition

1. Gameplay readability and accessibility.
2. Character identity and emotional clarity.
3. Environmental navigation and landmark readability.
4. Narrative composition and atmosphere.
5. Decorative detail and visual effects.

Detail must not obscure interactable objects, evidence, navigation routes, subtitles, hazards or important character expressions. A cinematic shot may temporarily prioritise composition, but gameplay camera control and player orientation must remain understandable.

Use foreground, midground and background layers to create depth. Use silhouettes and value contrast before adding surface detail. Important points of interest should have a readable approach and recognizable landmark.

## 4. Character art standards

### 4.1 General
Characters use semi-realistic 3D presentation: natural anatomy and proportions, believable clothing construction, expressive but restrained facial features, readable silhouettes and materials that respond consistently to lighting.

Avoid exaggerated anatomy, generic interchangeable faces, ornamental overload and detail that cannot survive the target platform's screen size.

### 4.2 Asher Vance
Canon reference:
- Asher is the sole playable protagonist and Chosen.
- He is presented as 18 years old.
- He is an Agent/Specialist in the Watcher branch.
- He is human, not divine.
- His approved Covenant uniform direction is white with restrained gold and a cross-centred insignia, subject to the relevant uniform reference and source-of-truth documents.

Art rules:
- Preserve approved identity, age presentation, uniform language, insignia and role.
- His silhouette must remain recognizable at gameplay distance.
- Do not invent facial identity, ethnicity, hairstyle, body measurements, scars, equipment loadout or additional costume variants without creator approval.
- Do not visually portray him as superhuman or divine.
- Facial performance should communicate attention, uncertainty, compassion, resolve and fatigue without melodramatic exaggeration.

### 4.3 Ember
Canon reference: Ember is Asher's intelligent animal companion, not a weapon. Exact species and final appearance must be taken from an approved character reference; if no approved reference exists, mark them UNKNOWN.

Art rules:
- Do not guess or silently lock Ember's species, anatomy, coat/skin pattern, size or accessories.
- Companion animation must communicate an animal's body language, not human speech or human gestures.
- Perception is fallible. Visual reactions must not imply omniscience or guaranteed supernatural knowledge.
- Ember is not a divine messenger, worship leader or spiritual authority.
- Avoid turning Ember into a combat gadget or decorative mascot.

### 4.4 NPCs and Covenant agents
- Distinguish civilians, Covenant personnel and other groups through context-appropriate clothing, posture, equipment and behaviour.
- Civilian populations generally outnumber Covenant agents outside operational Covenant zones.
- The Covenant is a hidden organization, not a government or replacement for the Church.
- Do not reveal Covenant affiliation through conspicuous uniforms in ordinary public settings unless a scene's canon explicitly permits it.
- Named slice characters must follow their approved character records. Unnamed characters remain unnamed until approved.

## 5. Materials, surfaces and colour

**PROPOSED STANDARD:** use physically plausible material response where it improves believability, but do not pursue expensive shader complexity without visible benefit.

Material families:
- Skin: controlled roughness and subtle variation; avoid plastic gloss.
- Fabric: visible weave or folds only at useful viewing distances; restrained metallic trim.
- Metal: distinguish painted, worn, polished and oxidized surfaces.
- Stone/concrete: layered roughness and scale-appropriate surface breakup.
- Wood: grain and wear consistent with use and environment.
- Glass: restrained reflections and transparency; preserve readability.
- Foliage: consistent translucency and lighting response without excessive overdraw.

BTV palette:
- Establish regional palettes through colour keys before asset production.
- Covenant white and restrained gold are identity cues, not a universal world palette.
- Ordinary community spaces should feel welcoming and credible, not like Covenant facilities.
- Veil-related visual language must be reviewed for theological accuracy and canon consistency.
- Exact hex values, LUTs and grading curves remain **PROPOSED / NOT LOCKED** until reviewed against approved character and environment references.

Avoid constant teal-orange grading, over-saturation, crushed shadows, excessive bloom and colour-only communication.

## 6. Environments and world art

BTV's approved world structure is semi-open interconnected regions, not an unrestricted seamless open world.

Each region art brief must define:
- Narrative and gameplay purpose.
- Geographic and cultural identity, time of day and likely weather.
- Routes, boundaries, landmarks, points of interest and connective wilderness.
- Settlement function and civilian presence.
- Gameplay collision, navigation and sightline requirements.
- Lighting and audio mood.
- Required asset families and target-device constraints.

Build each region in stages:
1. **Blockout:** test scale, routes, sightlines, elevation, navigation and mission placement.
2. **Presentable:** establish terrain/material palette, landmark silhouettes, architecture language, vegetation families and lighting direction.
3. **Shippable:** refine materials, set dressing, lighting, sound, transitions, accessibility and performance; repeat functional tests after art changes.

Separate authored points of interest from wilderness/interstitial spaces. Do not fill every metre with props, encounters or collectibles. Use negative space and quiet travel deliberately.

### 6.1 Vegetation and terrain
Treat large trees, gameplay shrubs, ground cover, rocks, terrain modules and decorative foliage as different asset classes. State whether each asset is a landmark, occluder, boundary, navigation cue, gameplay object or decoration. Validate collision, visibility, navigation, shadow cost and overdraw.

### 6.2 Weather and time
Weather and seasonal variation are presentation and world-state systems, not automatic promises of full simulation. Start with a small set of authored conditions that serve specific regions and scenes. Ensure weather does not hide critical information or make navigation unfair.

## 7. Lighting, atmosphere and visual effects

Cinematic lighting is approved as an art direction, not as permission to make every scene dark or expensive.

Use:
- Clear key/fill/rim intent for character readability.
- Motivated practical lights in interiors and settlements.
- Controlled fog and atmospheric depth.
- Shadows where they materially improve grounding and composition.
- Baked or simplified lighting where dynamic lighting is not needed.
- Effects that communicate a specific narrative or gameplay state.

Veil-related effects must be reviewed individually. Do not invent metaphysical rules, divine manifestations, power mechanics or visual signs that conflict with canon. No visual effect may turn prayer, Scripture, Communion or the Holy Spirit into a consumable gameplay power.

## 8. Camera and cinematic language

**APPROVED DESIGN:** hybrid first- and third-person presentation, with third-person as the default working baseline unless a specific gameplay design approves otherwise.

- Third-person should preserve spatial awareness, character readability and companion visibility.
- First-person is optional and must have a defined gameplay purpose; it is not a universal requirement for every interaction.
- Avoid camera clipping, motion sickness triggers, abrupt FOV changes and excessive shake.
- Dialogue shots should preserve speaker/listener geography and player comprehension.
- Cinematic camera control must return cleanly to gameplay and must not create unskippable friction without a narrative reason.
- Camera transitions require keyboard, touch and controller consideration as applicable.

## 9. Animation and performance

Use natural, responsive gameplay movement and more carefully authored cinematic animation for story beats. Gameplay input, collision and state changes must not wait for cosmetic animation frames.

Human character baseline test set (not a final animation catalogue):
- idle, walk, run, start/stop, turn, interact/reach, listen, sit/stand and safe context actions.
- Add character-specific clips only when gameplay or narrative requires them.

Ember requires a separate animal locomotion and body-language set. Do not retarget a human animation set onto Ember.

Animation acceptance includes:
- correct skeleton hierarchy and rest pose;
- joint deformation and accessory alignment;
- foot/ground contact and root-motion consistency;
- interruption and recovery;
- camera-distance readability;
- responsive movement at varied frame rates;
- no animation implying unapproved abilities or knowledge.

## 10. UI, readability and accessibility

The interface must be legible over varied backgrounds and remain consistent with BTV's identity. Define safe areas for Android, scalable text, subtitle controls, contrast, focus order, input prompts and audio channels.

Do not rely on colour, sound or animation alone to communicate critical information. Keep subtitles readable and speaker attribution clear. Avoid decorative HUD clutter and persistent objective markers where the design calls for player observation.

## 11. Asset provenance and originality

Every production asset must record:
- stable asset ID and descriptive name;
- asset type and intended use;
- creator/source and license/permission;
- source project and export version;
- target scale, pivot/origin and orientation;
- materials, textures and animation clips;
- collision/navigation assumptions;
- review status and approver;
- known limitations and dependencies.

Do not ship reference-game screenshots, extracted meshes, ripped textures, copied logos, music or other protected assets. Marketplace or generated assets require license and provenance review. AI-generated output must be checked for originality, anatomy, cultural accuracy, theological accuracy and consistency with approved BTV designs.

## 12. Art review gates

An asset is not production-ready merely because it looks attractive in a render.

- **Concept approved:** design fits canon and visual identity.
- **Model approved:** silhouette, proportions, topology and materials reviewed.
- **Rig/animation approved:** deformation and required clips validated.
- **Engine import approved:** scale, materials, skeleton and dependencies verified in pinned Godot version.
- **Gameplay approved:** readability, collision, navigation and interaction verified in context.
- **Platform approved:** visual quality and performance checked on target hardware.
- **Release approved:** provenance, license, source files and final integration reviewed.
