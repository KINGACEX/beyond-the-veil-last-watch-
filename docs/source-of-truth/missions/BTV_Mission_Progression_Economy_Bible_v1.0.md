## 1. Authority & Purpose
Specialized BTV reference for mission structure, objectives, progression, leadership advancement, rewards, currencies, economy principles, Scout Reports, mission consequences, and player growth.

## 2. Authority & Status
Creator decisions and corrections override all references. Master Canon remains primary content authority. Story & Lore governs narrative meaning; Character governs character development; Covenant governs organizational progression; Faith/Prayer/Armor governs spiritual boundaries; Church/HCI governs church activities; Veil/Enemy governs supernatural encounters; World/NPC governs world integration.

## 3. Non-Invention Rule
Do not invent exact XP curves, level caps, prices, named missions, reward tables, progression timelines, economy simulation, rank requirements, or mechanical formulas as established canon. Clearly label all proposals.

## 4. Mission Philosophy
BTV missions should express the world's people, conflicts, responsibilities, mysteries, and consequences. Missions should not reduce the game to a repeated travel-to-location → kill-enemies → collect-item formula.

## 5. Mission Categories
Established/recognized mission categories include Rescue, Investigation, Evangelism/Outreach, Restoration, Battle Operations, Scout Operations, Community Help, and Veil Investigations. Categories may overlap.

## 6. Mission Objectives
Objectives may include protecting civilians, rescuing people, gathering evidence, investigating anomalies, escorting, evacuating, repairing, restoring, communicating, negotiating, scouting, defending, pursuing, containing, or fighting.

## 7. Multiple Objective Types
A mission may change objectives as new information appears. A successful mission can involve investigation before combat, rescue during combat, restoration afterward, or several overlapping goals.

## 8. Mission Context
Every major mission should establish why the objective matters, who is affected, what information is known, what is uncertain, what risks exist, what resources are available, and what consequences may follow.

## 9. Player Agency
Where appropriate, missions should permit meaningful choices such as approach, preparation, route, dialogue, protection priorities, tactical commands, retreat, investigation depth, and resource use.

## 10. Non-Combat Solutions
Combat is important but not universally mandatory. Depending on context, missions may support dialogue, de-escalation, investigation, stealth/avoidance, rescue, evacuation, protection, pursuit, or withdrawal.

## 11. Combat Mission Boundary
When combat is appropriate, it should serve mission context and objectives rather than exist solely to generate XP or loot.

## 12. Civilian Protection
Civilians are meaningful mission participants and responsibilities. Protecting, evacuating, rescuing, or assisting civilians can be a primary objective rather than an optional decoration.

## 13. Mission Failure
Failure should be possible and meaningful. Consequences may involve lost opportunities, injuries, damaged infrastructure, changed relationships, delayed information, altered future missions, or other context-specific effects.

## 14. Mission Consequences
Consequences should be specific to the mission and world rather than a universal morality score. Success does not require every outcome to be perfect.

## 15. Redemption & Consequences
Mercy, forgiveness, surrender, reconciliation, or restoration can produce meaningful outcomes where appropriate, but they do not automatically erase consequences.

## 16. Scout Reports
Scout Reports can reveal threats, anomalies, missing people, suspicious activity, enemy movement, environmental changes, or uncertain reports. Reports should include limitations and confidence rather than magically revealing everything.

## 17. Scout-to-Operation Flow
A Scout Report may lead to investigation, preparation, rescue, organized operations, outreach, continued observation, or no immediate action. The player may decide what to do when the game context permits.

## 18. Investigation Missions
Investigation missions should reward observation, evidence, corroboration, questioning, environmental interpretation, patience, and judgment rather than only combat.

## 19. Veil Investigations
Veil investigations follow the Veil & Enemy Encyclopedia. The player may learn effects and patterns without automatically receiving the Veil's ultimate explanation.

## 20. Outreach & Evangelism
Outreach can involve listening, conversation, service, encouragement, warning, testimony, prayer, and relationship. It must never become a conversion-count minigame.

## 21. Community Help
Community-help missions can involve food, repair, rescue, rebuilding, assistance, protection, volunteering, care, logistics, or other grounded service. Generosity must not become a righteousness score.

## 22. Restoration Missions
Restoration may repair places, relationships, services, trust, or community functions. Restoration is broader than repairing an object.

## 23. Battle Operations
Battle Operations can address serious threats, enemy attacks, rescues under fire, defensive operations, or other conflicts where combat is justified by context.

## 24. Leadership Missions
As leadership responsibility grows, missions may include assigning priorities, coordinating teams, balancing civilian safety, allocating resources, reviewing intelligence, and deciding when to advance or withdraw.

## 25. Tactical Commands
Established examples include Hold, Push, Scout, Protect Civilians, Retreat, and Protect Teammate. This framework is not exhaustive. Exact controls and implementation remain open.

## 26. Rank Progression
Covenant advancement follows Recruits → Agents/Specialists → Team Leaders → Commanders → Generals. Advancement represents responsibility, trust, service, judgment, and leadership rather than body count or ordinary XP alone.

## 27. Rank Is Not Spiritual Worth
Higher Covenant rank never means greater holiness, salvation, divine favor, or spiritual superiority. A General can be mistaken; a Recruit can demonstrate wisdom.

## 28. Progression Philosophy — APPROVED DESIGN
Progression should communicate increasing capability, responsibility, relationships, knowledge, access, leadership, and consequences. It should not make the player morally or spiritually superior to others.

## 29. Asher's Progression
Asher is a Covenant-trained field member whose leadership trajectory can move through Team Leader, Commander, and eventually General responsibilities. Exact timing, missions, promotion criteria, and chronology remain OPEN unless creator decisions establish them.

## 30. Character Progression
Character development should include relationships, trust, skills, equipment, experience, decisions, knowledge, and narrative growth. Mechanical growth must not replace character arcs.

## 31. Teammate Progression
Teammates should develop through relationships, missions, responsibilities, personal arcs, tactical roles, equipment, and experience rather than functioning as interchangeable stat containers.

## 32. Ember Progression — APPROVED DESIGN PRINCIPLE
Keep Ember growth distinct from the player character’s other progression dimensions. Ember has physical, behavioral, bond, and spiritual-development dimensions, with established abilities including Veil Sight, Guardian Instinct, Veil Energy, Tracking Sense, and Spirit Guidance. This does not define species, origin, exact mechanics, or theology-dependent details; those remain OPEN where not already established.

## 33. Armor Progression
The six Armor of God elements and the medieval-modern visual direction are established. Progressive Armor equipment is an established design direction, while acquisition order, upgrades, statistics, visual stages, and milestones remain OPEN.

## 34. Faith Progression Boundary
Faith is never a level, stat, XP track, currency, purchasable resource, or numerical spiritual score. Prayer and spiritual growth are not grindable progression systems.

## 35. Skill & Capability Growth — APPROVED DESIGN PRINCIPLE
Practical capability is a distinct progression dimension. Skills may improve through training, experience, equipment, discovery, team coordination, or authored milestones where later designed. Exact skill trees, levels, and formulas remain OPEN. Capability must not measure spiritual worth.

## 36. Equipment Progression
Equipment may change through discovery, rewards, Covenant resources, crafting or other approved systems. Exact crafting, durability, upgrade, rarity, and loot rules remain OPEN.

## 37. Rewards Philosophy — APPROVED DESIGN
Rewards should reinforce the meaning of the mission and remain in three distinct categories: (1) ordinary money such as Aces, (2) Covenant resources such as Covenant Credits or authorized supplies, and (3) non-monetary outcomes such as information, access, relationships, rescued people, restored services, opportunities, and changed local conditions. Do not add a new currency where one of these categories already expresses the outcome. This distinction is approved design; exact reward tables and values remain OPEN.

## 38. Spiritual Reward Boundary
Faith, salvation, grace, divine favor, holiness, prayer power, Holy Spirit power, or spiritual worth are never rewards that can be purchased, traded, earned as numerical currency, or dropped by enemies.

## 39. Aces
Aces are the general-world currency and may support ordinary economic activity where appropriate. Exact denominations, prices, sources, sinks, and simulation rules remain OPEN.

## 40. Covenant Credits — APPROVED DESIGN PRINCIPLE
Covenant Credits are Covenant-focused currency/resources. They remain distinct from Aces. Covenant logistics should be grounded in the fiction: Keepers support equipment and supply accountability; Scouts report field conditions and needs; Watchers investigate and assess intelligence; and leaders authorize assignments and operational priorities within established rank responsibilities. Exact acquisition, spending, limits, authorization rules, and organizational policies remain OPEN.

## 41. Currency Separation
Aces and Covenant Credits must not be casually merged into one generic currency. Their existence should communicate different economic contexts.

## 42. Faith Is Not for Sale
No system may allow a player to purchase faith, salvation, forgiveness, prayer effectiveness, divine favor, spiritual rank, Holy Spirit abilities, or moral worth.

## 43. Giving & Charity
Church giving follows the Church/HCI/Service/Communion Bible. Offerings are voluntary; giving more does not make a character more righteous, and donations never guarantee blessings, loot, XP, healing, luck, or divine favor.

## 44. Economy Integration
The economy should connect to world occupations, businesses, services, resources, travel, crises, trade, repairs, and community life rather than existing only as a loot-and-shop loop.

## 45. Enemy Rewards
Enemy drops may include resources, clues, materials, artifacts, equipment, information, or access to special opportunities. Spiritual realities never become loot.

## 46. Reward Scaling
Rewards should be proportionate to mission context, risk, contribution, and world logic. Avoid arbitrary reward inflation that undermines the economy.

## 47. Loot Philosophy
Loot should have purpose. Not every enemy or container needs a reward, and rewards should not overwhelm narrative discovery or ordinary commerce.

## 48. Mission Economy
Missions may consume resources, create expenses, affect supplies, or provide legitimate rewards. Exact mission-cost systems remain OPEN.

## 49. Progression Without Grind
Major advancement should not require repetitive low-value grinding when narrative, leadership, relationships, investigation, or service can provide meaningful progression.

## 50. Exploration Rewards
Exploration can yield clues, hidden locations, resources, equipment, relationships, knowledge, or special opportunities. Hidden content should not be reduced to treasure collection.

## 51. Reputation Boundary — APPROVED DESIGN PRINCIPLE
BTV does not establish a universal morality/reputation meter. Relationship and trust states should remain contextual to the relevant person, team, community, or institution; they must reflect relevant actions and consequences rather than a single universal approval score. Exact implementation remains OPEN.

## 52. Mission Availability
Missions may emerge from Scout Reports, NPC needs, relationships, world events, Covenant priorities, investigation, church/community life, or story progression. Not every possible mission needs to be permanently available.

## 53. Dynamic Mission State — APPROVED DESIGN PRINCIPLE
Missions may change because of time, player choices, world events, failures, rescues, enemy actions, restoration, or new evidence. Where appropriate, outcomes can affect future access, NPC reactions, available information, local services/safety, and later mission conditions. Keep consequences legible and proportionate; avoid simulating every world detail. Exact dynamic-mission architecture remains OPEN.

## 54. Mission Chains
Missions may form chains, branches, follow-ups, recurring responsibilities, or independent episodes. A chain should have meaningful narrative or systemic justification.

## 55. Optional Content
Optional missions can deepen characters, communities, lore, exploration, service, and world understanding. Optional does not mean meaningless.

## 56. Main Story vs Side Content
Main and side content should feel part of the same world. Side content should not casually contradict canon or reveal information characters could not plausibly know.

## 57. Leadership & Multi-Team Responsibility
Later leadership may involve multiple teams or regional priorities. Exact team counts, command maps, delegation mechanics, and strategic interfaces remain OPEN.

## 58. Promotion Principles
Promotion should reflect demonstrated responsibility, judgment, service, leadership, and trust. It should not be awarded solely for killing enemies or accumulating currency.

## 59. Failure & Learning
Failed missions can teach the player through consequences, evidence, relationships, tactical feedback, or changed circumstances. Failure should not automatically mean game over.

## 60. Save/Retry Boundary
Exact save, checkpoint, retry, and mission-reset systems are technical/gameplay decisions and remain OPEN.

## 61. Difficulty & Progression
Difficulty may affect challenge, enemy behavior, resources, or mission pressure, but should not rewrite character identity, theological principles, or established world canon.

## 62. Accessibility & Fairness
Progression and mission systems should remain understandable and reasonably fair. Difficulty should challenge judgment and skill rather than hide essential information arbitrarily.

## 70. Creator-approved design addendum — 2026-10-11

**Status: APPROVED DESIGN.** The creator explicitly approved the following principles for development in the existing BTV references. This approval does not claim that code has been implemented, integrated, tested, or accepted in a runnable build.

1. **Three kinds of reward:** ordinary money, Covenant resources, and non-monetary outcomes remain distinct. Do not add unnecessary currencies.
2. **Progression without spiritual scoring:** keep practical capability, Covenant responsibility, Ember growth, and contextual relationships distinct. None measures spiritual worth. Knowledge progression remains a useful additional design dimension; it is not a spiritual score.
3. **World recovery:** rescue, investigation, restoration, and community outcomes may affect future access, NPC reactions, available information, and local conditions. Consequences should be proportionate and understandable.
4. **Lore-grounded Covenant logistics:** Keepers, Scouts, Watchers, and leadership ground supplies, reports, equipment, and operational authorization in the fiction. Exact rules and interfaces remain open.
5. **Evidence-based development gates:** documentation, implementation, integration, verification, and acceptance are separate states; each requires its own evidence.
6. **One source of truth:** update the existing authoritative reference for each decision, cross-reference changes that affect other domains, and reconcile contradictions rather than creating competing specifications.
