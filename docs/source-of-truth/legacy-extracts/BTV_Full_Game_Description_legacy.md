BEYOND THE VEIL: THE LAST WATCH
FULL GAME DESCRIPTION & MASTER DESIGN DOCUMENT
Game 2 • Approved Working Canon • Design Reference
1. Project Identity
Beyond the Veil: The Last Watch is Game 2. It is completely separate from AFTERFALL. Never mix 
their characters, lore, mechanics, code, assets, worlds or files.
Genre: modern Christian action-adventure, survival, RPG and semi-open-world exploration with 
supernatural combat, investigation, leadership, community systems and meaningful choices.
Setting: a fictional near-present/late-2020s world where an unseen supernatural conflict exists 
behind a mysterious boundary called the Veil.
2. Core Vision
The player begins in a recognizable modern world and gradually discovers a hidden supernatural 
war. The game combines exploration, survival, combat, investigation, leadership, teamwork, 
community service, evangelism, faith and choice.
The central journey is not merely becoming powerful. It is learning to stand in truth, serve people, 
lead wisely, grow in faith and endure as the crisis escalates.
Originality is mandatory: study excellent games for principles, but create original characters, lore, 
mechanics, assets, environments and presentation.
3. Era, World & Technology
The game uses a contemporary/near-present setting with modern society and technology.
 Smartphones and communication
 Computers and digital systems
 Modern cities and roads
 Cars and public transport
 Modern medical facilities
 Modern churches and community buildings
 Rural and wilderness regions
 Covenant technology and equipment
It is a fictional world, not a direct recreation of real Earth. Biblical themes inspire the world and 
story without simply retelling the Old or New Testament.

4. The Veil
The Veil separates ordinary human reality from the deeper supernatural conflict. At first the 
player knows little about it. As the story advances, evidence shows that it is weakening.
Mystery is important: environmental clues can reveal that something is wrong before the player 
receives an explanation.
5. WORLD PILLAR — THE WORLD REMEMBERS
The world is not a static backdrop. Weather, wind, terrain, wildlife, NPCs, enemies and player 
actions influence one another.

 Rain can create mud, reduce visibility and alter animal behavior.
 Wind moves vegetation, cloth, smoke, dust and fire.
 Wildlife flees danger and may avoid supernatural activity.
 NPC behavior changes after battles, rescues and neglected threats.
 Enemy activity can change regional danger.
 Player actions can help regions recover.
6. THE VEIL'S BREATH
An original environmental phenomenon linked to supernatural activity.
 Leaves can move against the normal wind.
 Grass may ripple while nearby trees are still.
 Smoke can bend toward an unexplained location.
 Birds may suddenly become silent.
 Wildlife can react before the player sees an enemy.
 Environmental behavior can hint that something is nearby.

Trees, grass, bushes, leaves, smoke, dust, fire, clothing and selected objects react to conditions. 
Wildlife, NPCs and enemies react as well. Systems should be optimized rather than simulated at 
excessive cost.
8. Wildlife & Nature
Wildlife naturally roams the world and helps create a living ecosystem.
 Animals wander, feed, drink, rest and flee threats.
 Different species have different behaviors.
 Weather changes animal activity.
 Supernatural disturbances can drive animals away.
 The player can observe, track and feed animals.
 Certain animals can be rescued or cared for.
 Optional hunting can provide food and materials.
 Overhunting can affect local populations.
 Wildlife can become environmental clues.
The protagonist's special animal companion remains distinct from ordinary wildlife.
9. Transportation
Transportation is meaningful but deliberately manageable.
 Walking/running — detailed exploration and interiors
 Horses — wilderness and rough terrain
 Motorcycles — fast solo travel
 Covenant SUVs/patrol vehicles — teams and supplies
 Team transport — larger operations
 Boats — waterways/coastal regions
 Helicopter transport — selected major missions
Transportation can provide speed, storage, team movement, communication and evacuation. 
Weather and terrain can affect travel.
Horses are an official transportation system. Horses can be approached, cared for, bonded with 
and ridden. The main animal companion is separate.

11. Day/Night & World States
Day/night can influence visibility, wildlife, NPC routines, enemy activity and selected missions.
 CALM — normal activity.
 UNSTABLE — strange activity and increased enemy presence.
 CORRUPTED — supernatural influence becomes visible.
 CONFLICT — active enemy operations and civilian danger.
 RECOVERING — enemies are pushed back and life gradually returns.
Ignored threats can worsen regions, but consequences should create new gameplay rather than 
simply producing game over.
12. Two-Protagonist Choice
The player chooses a male or female protagonist. They have different backgrounds, histories, 
personalities, relationships and opening storylines.
Their stories eventually converge as they discover that their missions are connected.
The selected protagonist becomes the Chosen General later. The other remains extremely 
important, can lead missions and teams, develop independently and play a major role in the final 
conflict.
Neither protagonist is intended to be objectively better.
13. The Covenant
The Covenant is the main fictional Christian organization. It protects people, investigates 
supernatural activity, trains members, coordinates teams, rescues communities, gathers 
intelligence and fights supernatural evil.
Suggested hierarchy: Recruit Field Agent Specialist Team Leader Commander General. → → → → →
Rank represents responsibility and leadership, not spiritual worth.

14. Covenant Agencies
 Guardians — combat and protection.
 Messengers — communication and evangelism.
 Watchers — investigation and intelligence.
 Shepherds — rescue, protection and restoration.
 Keepers — equipment, resources and safehouse support.
 Scouts — reconnaissance and threat reporting.
15. Leadership Progression
Leadership is earned through service, successful operations, wise decisions and responsibility. The 
protagonist becomes Team Leader around early-to-mid game, Commander later, and General 
significantly later.
16. Scout Reports & Dynamic Operations
Scouts discover developing threats and report them.
Example: “Unusual activity detected in the eastern district. Destroyer presence increasing. Civilian 
risk: HIGH.”
The player can personally respond, send a team, investigate, rescue civilians, prepare or delay. 
Major battles are occasional and meaningful.
17. Enemy Factions
 Destroyers — direct physical combat enemies focused on destruction and assaults.
 Persuaders — manipulate people, spread deception and influence communities.
 Deceivers — use intelligence, illusions, misdirection and ambushes.
 Enemy Commanders — powerful supernatural leaders and major bosses.
Enemy variants should evolve through abilities and tactics, not just larger health bars.
18. Enemy Drops & Currency
Enemies can leave materials, equipment components, salvage, clues and special artifacts.
Primary ordinary currency: Covenant Credits. They support equipment, crafting, upgrades, 
transportation, safehouses and resources. Spiritual progression is never a purchasable commodity.

21. Prayer, Divine Intervention & Holy Spirit Guidance
God is central to the game's worldview but is not an ordinary controllable combat character.

Prayer can seek guidance, protection, wisdom, knowledge, strength, blessing and needed

Holy Spirit guidance can appear through warnings, wisdom, conviction, prompting or

32. Choices & Consequences
Choices can affect teammates, communities, missions, resources, Covenant reputation, regional 
states and side stories.
Examples: fight versus investigate; rescue versus pursuit; lead personally versus delegate; trust

33. Canon Story Mode & Player Story Mode
Canon Story Mode follows the intended canonical storyline and major outcomes.
Player Story Mode gives greater freedom over selected choices and side outcomes.

34. Story Progression
 1 — Awakening: introduce protagonist, modern world, Covenant, combat, first supernatural 
incidents and missions.
 2 — The Paths Converge: the two protagonists meet and discover their missions are connected.
 3 — Rising Responsibility: Team Leader stage, scouts, community work, evangelism and Armor 
of God progression.
 4 — The Deepening War: stronger Destroyers, Persuaders, Deceivers and commanders; deeper 
Veil mystery.
 5 — Command: protagonist becomes Commander and coordinates multiple teams/regions.
 6 — General: chosen protagonist becomes General while the other remains a major leader and 
ally.
 7 — The Veil Weakens: supernatural activity accelerates and deeper truths emerge.
 8 — The Last Watch: final major operation combines leadership, combat, prayer, Scripture, 
rescue, evangelism, investigation, teamwork and divine intervention.

43. Final Creative Statement
Beyond the Veil: The Last Watch is a modern Christian semi-open-world action-adventure about a 
Covenant operative chosen for a supernatural mission. The player chooses a male or female 
protagonist with a distinct background and opening story. Their paths converge. The chosen 
protagonist rises through Covenant leadership and eventually becomes General, while the other 
remains essential.
The world is alive: weather, wind, vegetation, wildlife, NPCs, enemies and world states react to one 
another. Scouts report threats. The player can fight, investigate, rescue, evangelize, advise 
teammates, manage operations and travel by foot, horse or modern transport.
Prayer, divine intervention, Holy Spirit guidance and Scripture moments support the Christian 
worldview. The Veil weakens, the conflict escalates, and the story eventually reaches The Last 
Watch.
The goal is an original, meaningful, reactive and manageable game—not a copy of another title.
IMPORTANT PROJECT SEPARATION: AFTERFALL and Beyond the Veil: The Last Watch are 
different games. Do not merge their lore, characters, mechanics, files or code.
