BEYOND THE VEIL: THE LAST WATCH
MASTER AI GAME DEVELOPMENT DOCUMENT
Version 1.0 — Foundation Bible
How This Document Must Be Used
This document is the master reference for recreating or developing the game. Treat every Bible 
below as a separate design layer. Do not merge, delete, or contradict a Bible unless a later 
approved revision explicitly changes it. The game is fictional and modern, Christian-themed, 
action-adventure, survival-oriented, and semi-open-world.
1. GAME IDENTITY BIBLE
 Title: Beyond the Veil: The Last Watch.
 Genre: Christian action-adventure with survival, exploration, combat, leadership, relationship,
investigation, and choice systems.
 Setting: A fictional modern world in which supernatural evil is beginning to influence 
humanity.
 World structure: Semi-open world made of several large connected regions rather than one 
enormous seamless map.
 Core fantasy: The player serves God through The Covenant while confronting supernatural 
evil, protecting people, resisting deception, exploring the hidden world, and eventually taking 
part in The Last Watch.
 Design goal: Make the game feel cinematic and adventurous while keeping faith, service, 
courage, truth, compassion, and leadership central.
 Do not make God a normal playable party member or combat character. God is the ultimate 
authority and source of the protagonist's mission; His presence is represented through 
prayer, guidance, signs, timing, blessings, and story events.
 Do not turn faith into a purchasable commodity. Spiritual progression must remain distinct 
from ordinary currency.
2. WORLD & LORE BIBLE
 The visible world is recognizably modern: cities, roads, homes, businesses, wilderness, 
industrial areas, settlements, churches, abandoned facilities, and hidden locations.
 The supernatural world exists behind ordinary reality. The boundary between the two is called
the Veil.
 The Veil is not simply a portal. It represents the hidden boundary between ordinary human 
life and the supernatural conflict.
 The Veil gradually becomes weaker as the story progresses.
 Biblical end-times themes inspire the larger story, but the game is not a retelling of the Old 
Testament or New Testament.
 The game may use fictional locations, organizations, characters, events, supernatural 
manifestations, and interpretations while respecting the game's Christian foundation.

 Communities can be affected by fear, deception, destruction, and manipulation. The player 
can fight, investigate, rescue, help, advise, and evangelize.
3. THE COVENANT BIBLE
 The Covenant is a hidden Christian organization operating across the modern world.
 The Covenant exists to protect people, resist supernatural evil, investigate unusual events, 
support communities, and serve God's purposes.
 The Covenant is not presented as competing with God. It is an organization of human 
servants.
 Members have different specialties and ranks.
 Core branches include Guardians, Messengers, Watchers, Shepherds, Keepers, and Scouts.
 Guardians specialize in combat and protection.
 Messengers specialize in communication, evangelism, and community work.
 Watchers specialize in investigation, intelligence, tracking, and detecting deception.
 Shepherds specialize in rescue, support, community restoration, and protecting vulnerable 
people.
 Keepers specialize in equipment, resources, crafting, maintenance, and safehouse support.
 Scouts specialize in reconnaissance and reporting enemy activity.
 These branches cooperate rather than becoming rival factions.
4. HIERARCHY & LEADERSHIP BIBLE
 Covenant hierarchy: Recruit → Field Agent → Specialist → Team Leader → Commander → 
General.
 The protagonist begins as an established Covenant field operative, not as a new recruit.
 The protagonist should become a Team Leader during the early-to-mid portion of the story, 
but not immediately.
 Later progression can move the protagonist through Senior Leadership/Commander 
responsibilities and eventually to General.
 Promotions are earned through story achievements, service, successful operations, 
leadership decisions, and responsibility rather than simple XP grinding.
 Rank represents organizational responsibility, not spiritual superiority.
 As the protagonist rises, the game can shift from personal missions toward strategic 
decisions involving teams, regions, resources, and operations.
 Teammates can also grow in rank and responsibility. Characters helped and mentored by the
protagonist may later become leaders themselves.
5. PROTAGONIST BIBLE
 The protagonist is a member of The Covenant before the game begins.
 The protagonist is capable but still developing, especially as a leader.
 The protagonist can fight, explore, investigate, rescue civilians, communicate with 
teammates, give advice, and participate in evangelism.
 The protagonist progressively obtains the Armor of God and additional fictional gameplay 
abilities.
 The protagonist can pray during appropriate story and gameplay moments.

 The protagonist's faith should be portrayed as a lived relationship and source of courage, not 
as a video-game currency.
6. ENEMY BIBLE
 Destroyers are primarily combat-focused supernatural enemies. They create physical danger 
and can appear in groups or major encounters.
 Persuaders specialize in influencing, deceiving, and attempting to turn people away from 
what is good. They are not required to be defeated through combat.
 Deceivers specialize in manipulation, illusions, misinformation, ambushes, and hidden 
operations.
 Powerful enemy commanders can control regions or major operations.
 Enemy groups should move through the world and create believable activity rather than 
simply spawning everywhere.
 Enemies can leave drops when defeated.
 Enemy drops can include salvage, crafting materials, intelligence clues, artifacts, equipment￾related items, and Covenant Credits depending on enemy type and rarity.
 Do not imply that ordinary human beings who struggle with fear, pride, greed, doubt, or other 
flaws are literally supernatural enemies. The supernatural enemy forces are fictional entities.
7. SCOUT & EVENT BIBLE
 Covenant scouts observe enemy movements throughout the world.
 Scouts can return with reports such as unusual activity, enemy concentrations, civilian 
danger, patrol movements, or suspicious locations.
 Reports can trigger occasional combat operations.
 Not every report becomes a battle. The player can engage, investigate, rescue, prepare, or 
sometimes continue another mission.
 Large enemy concentrations should be relatively occasional so combat remains meaningful.
 Example event flow: Scout → Report → Player Decision → Operation → Consequences → 
Rewards/World Change.
 Enemy activity can change the state of a region.
8. COMBAT BIBLE
 Combat is action-focused and includes melee weapons, the Sword of the Spirit, defensive 
equipment, abilities, dodging, combos, teammate assistance, and special encounters.
 Combat should be responsive and readable rather than excessively complicated.
 Normal battles should not constantly force religious speeches.
 Major boss battles can contain cinematic Scripture-based declarations or prayers.
 The protagonist may recite a meaningful biblical passage during a major boss battle while 
continuing to fight.
 Psalm 23 can be used as an exceptional full-battle prayer/recitation scene for a significant 
boss, beginning during combat and continuing as the fight progresses, rather than functioning
as a magical spell.
 Scripture should not be portrayed as a button that mechanically forces God to attack an 
enemy. It represents faith, prayer, courage, and conviction while fictional gameplay abilities 
provide the combat mechanics.

 Signature short declarations can include: 'By truth we stand. By faith we endure. By the Light 
we rise. Darkness will not prevail.'
 Another possible protagonist declaration is: 'The darkness has had its watch. Now we take 
ours.' Use sparingly and only after the game's tone supports it.
9. ARMOR & POWER BIBLE
 The Armor of God is introduced progressively.
 Core pieces: Belt of Truth, Breastplate of Righteousness, Shoes of the Gospel of Peace, 
Shield of Faith, Helmet of Salvation, and Sword of the Spirit.
 Each piece must have gameplay functions in addition to visual identity.
 Armor should unlock progressively through story progression and meaningful events.
 Additional fictional powers may be introduced, but they must fit the Christian fantasy tone and
must not replace the importance of the Armor of God.
 Prayer can occur during gameplay and after battles.
 Possible prayer categories include guidance, knowledge, protection, items, blessings, and 
other needs.
 Answers to prayer should not always be immediate. Timing and outcomes belong to the 
game's depiction of God's will.
 The Holy Spirit can be represented through guidance, conviction, warnings, insight, or other 
respectful narrative/gameplay systems without being reduced to a controllable spell.
10. EVANGELISM & COMMUNITY BIBLE
 Evangelism is a gameplay and story system, not merely background dialogue.
 Some missions involve helping people who have been influenced by Persuaders or other 
forms of deception.
 The player can talk to people, help communities, provide practical assistance, share Christian
messages, and make choices about how to respond.
 Successful community support can change settlements, unlock allies, provide information, 
open missions, and affect later story events.
 Do not make every person automatically agree with the protagonist. Responses can vary 
while remaining respectful.
 Helping people can be as important as defeating enemies.
11. TEAMMATE & RELATIONSHIP BIBLE
 Teammates are active Covenant members with their own personalities, specialties, goals, 
histories, and progression.
 Teammates can accompany the protagonist on missions.
 The player can give tactical advice such as defensive, aggressive, careful, or rescue-focused 
instructions when appropriate.
 Advice influences teammate behavior without making one wrong choice automatically fail the 
game.
 Teammates can reward the protagonist for helping them.
 Rewards may include weapons, equipment, upgrade materials, information, missions, or 
access to special operations.
 Teammates can remember significant actions and develop trust with the protagonist.

 Some teammates can progress through the Covenant hierarchy alongside the protagonist.
12. PET COMPANION BIBLE
 The protagonist has an animal companion.
 The pet assists with exploration, detection, tracking, warnings, selected combat situations, 
and finding useful objects.
 The pet should feel like a companion rather than a disposable gameplay tool.
 Any unusual abilities should be revealed progressively rather than all at once.
13. ECONOMY & REWARD BIBLE
 Primary ordinary currency: Covenant Credits.
 Covenant Credits are earned through missions, enemy drops, rescues, investigations, 
community assistance, and other gameplay activities.
 Credits can be used for equipment, crafting materials, transportation, safehouse 
improvements, and other practical systems.
 Spiritual growth must not be purchasable with ordinary currency.
 Rewards should include both material rewards and narrative rewards such as trust, 
information, access, reputation, and leadership opportunities.
14. EXPLORATION & SURVIVAL BIBLE
 The world should feel explorable without requiring a gigantic map.
 Regions should contain cities, wilderness, ruins, facilities, settlements, caves, hidden 
locations, enemy concentrations, Covenant sites, and story locations.
 Survival elements can include resource management, equipment condition, safe areas, 
crafting, preparation, and environmental hazards.
 Exploration can reveal new routes and information that become useful later.
 Traversal abilities can progressively unlock previously inaccessible areas.
15. MISSION BIBLE
 Mission categories include combat operations, rescue missions, investigations, scouting, 
community restoration, evangelism, equipment recovery, teammate missions, and major 
story missions.
 Mission design should provide meaningful choices rather than forcing every problem into 
combat.
 Some missions should be optional while others advance the central story.
 Operations can have consequences that affect regions, teammates, resources, or future 
missions.
 Major missions should feel cinematic and memorable.
16. STORY & ARC BIBLE
 Arc 1 — The Unseen Signs: The protagonist carries out Covenant operations while strange 
supernatural incidents begin appearing more frequently.

 Arc 2 — Rising Shadows: Destroyer activity increases and Persuaders begin influencing 
communities. The protagonist begins taking greater leadership responsibility.
 Arc 3 — The Fractured Veil: Investigations reveal that multiple incidents are connected to the 
weakening Veil.
 Arc 4 — The Watch Expands: The protagonist commands larger operations, works with 
different Covenant branches, and faces stronger enemy commanders.
 Arc 5 — The Last Watch: The supernatural conflict reaches a critical stage. The protagonist's 
leadership, faith, relationships, and previous choices become important.
 Arc 6 — Beyond the Veil: The story reaches the deepest layer of the supernatural conflict and

events.

identity.
17. BIBLICAL & THEOLOGICAL BIBLE
 The game is Christian in worldview and draws inspiration from Scripture.
 Biblical themes include spiritual warfare, truth, faith, salvation, righteousness, peace, prayer, 
courage, service, redemption, and end-times imagery.
 The Armor of God is inspired by Ephesians 6.
 Philippians 4:13, Psalm 23, Psalm 91, Romans 8:31, and other appropriate passages can 
inspire character declarations and major scenes.
 Full biblical passages should be reserved for carefully chosen scenes rather than repeated 
constantly.
 Do not present the protagonist as divine or equal to God.
 Do not present fictional gameplay powers as literal promises that God must act on command.
 Respectfully distinguish fictional lore from canonical Scripture.
18. AUDIO, CINEMATIC & PRESENTATION BIBLE
 The tone should be cinematic, serious, adventurous, hopeful, suspenseful, and occasionally 
intense.
 Music should support exploration, danger, prayer, discovery, and major battles.
 Scripture declarations should be rare enough to remain powerful.
 Major boss encounters can use silence, environmental sound, music changes, and spoken 
prayer to create memorable moments.
 The Covenant should have recognizable visual identity without making the world look like a 
generic fantasy kingdom.
 Modern clothing, equipment, buildings, vehicles, communication devices, and environments 
should coexist with supernatural elements.
19. PLAYER PROGRESSION BIBLE
 Progression combines equipment, abilities, Armor of God, Covenant rank, relationships, 
reputation/trust, exploration, and story knowledge.
 Do not make progression solely numerical.
 New ranks should unlock responsibilities and opportunities.
 New equipment should unlock or improve ways of interacting with the world.

 Create placeholder assets first where necessary, then replace them with final art and audio.

 Any contradiction should be flagged for approval rather than silently changing established
