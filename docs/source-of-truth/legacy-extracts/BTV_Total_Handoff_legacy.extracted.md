1. PROJECT IDENTITY
Title: Beyond the Veil: The Last Watch (BTV).
BTV is an original fictional modern Christian action-adventure survival RPG with a large connected semi-open
world.
The world combines contemporary/futuristic civilization, Christian themes, human conflict, community life,
supernatural Veil phenomena, exploration, investigation, survival and redemption.
The game should feel like a premium AAA third-person PC RPG, not a mobile game.
Reference principles may draw from high-quality RPGs such as Red Dead Redemption 2, The Witcher 3, Elden
Ring, Cyberpunk 2077, God of War Ragnarök, Baldur's Gate 3, Ghost of Tsushima, Zelda: Breath of the Wild,
Black Myth: Wukong and The Blood of Dawnwalker—but BTV must remain original.
2. CORE WORLD PHILOSOPHY
BTV is not a retelling of the Old or New Testament. It is an original story set in a modern world experiencing an
escalating spiritual conflict.
Christianity is central to the world's moral and spiritual framework, with Christ above the Covenant and above the
Chosen.
The story explores truth, faith, prayer, mercy, courage, service, evangelism, redemption, leadership, sacrifice,
discernment and the consequences of choices.
God is the ultimate authority and is not a playable combat character.
The Holy Spirit is represented through guidance, conviction, warnings, wisdom and discernment—not as a
controllable magic meter.
Prayer is meaningful but is not a guaranteed transaction or button that automatically produces a desired reward.
Divine timing and God's will remain important.
3. ASHER VANCE
Established protagonist: Asher Vance.
Asher is a young Black protagonist and Chosen, approximately 16–19 years old. He must consistently look
youthful, not like a middle-aged or fully adult military protagonist.
Asher is a Covenant field member who grows through service, leadership and responsibility.
His progression is narrative/leadership-based: Team Leader → Commander → General, rather than ordinary XP
alone.
The Chosen status is not a Covenant rank. A Chosen can occupy any Covenant rank.
Future visuals must preserve Asher's recognizable appearance, youthful age, white/gold Covenant identity and
established companion relationship with Ember.
4. THE COVENANT
The Covenant is a hidden Christian organization serving God and communities.
It is not the government and is not the entirety of the Church.
The Covenant protects, serves, investigates, rescues, supports communities and responds to Veil-related threats.

Established equipment concepts: Belt of Truth; Breastplate of Righteousness; Shoes of the Gospel of Peace;
Shield of Faith; Helmet of Salvation; Sword of the Spirit.
The Armor of God should combine biblical inspiration with believable BTV modern/futuristic design.
The Sword of the Spirit may have a medieval-inspired and modernized visual expression, but faith should not be
reduced to generic fantasy magic.
7. EMBER — COMPANION
Ember is Asher's established German Shepherd/wolf-like animal companion.
Ember is a real gameplay companion, not merely cosmetic.
Systems: Physical Growth, Bond Growth, Spiritual Awakening, Veil Sight, Guardian Instinct, Veil Energy, Tracking
Sense and Spirit Guidance.
Commands may include attack, protect, scout, track, retrieve, hold, regroup, follow, guard and stay.
Ember can react to clues or Veil activity before Asher, help track people, warn Asher and participate in
investigation and protection.
8. CURRENCIES
Covenant Credits: used for Covenant systems.
Aces: general currency usable in both Covenant and ordinary-world activities.
Faith itself is not purchasable and should not be represented as a simple currency or reward.

Critical rule: there should not be the same ratio of Covenant personnel to civilians everywhere.
Public/community areas should generally contain substantially more civilians than Covenant agents.

Active crisis zones may have increased Covenant presence because of evacuation/security needs.
Wilderness is usually sparsely populated.
Veil zones vary according to the event and story state.
Demographic diversity should be natural and location-appropriate. Asher is Black and remains so, but the wider
population should not accidentally appear overwhelmingly one ethnicity in every location.
11. HCI — HARVEST CHAPEL INTERNATIONAL
HCI is a Christian church/community identity within BTV, inspired by the user's real-world HCI context but
represented fictionally in the game.
HCI should communicate worship, Christ, Scripture, fellowship, service, generosity, community and hope.
HCI is not simply a Covenant base.
Community members should generally outnumber Covenant agents at services and public chapel activities.
Typical chapel populations include families, children, students, workers, elderly members, visitors, worshippers,
musicians, volunteers, pastors/ministers and community workers.

Architecture, size, materials, seating, landscaping, lighting, surrounding district and cultural details can vary.
Examples: Urban District Chapel; Riverside Chapel; Campus/University Chapel; Mountain Chapel; Industrial-Zone
Chapel; Remote Outpost Chapel; Underground/Protected Chapel; Post-Crisis Chapel.

13. KITFIT & GIVING
Established HCI pre-service activity: KITFIT.
KITFIT can involve Scripture, a short message, voluntary giving/charity teaching and community interaction before

The gameplay focus is reflection, remembrance, faith and community.
15. FAITH & PRAYER SYSTEM
Prayer can occur during or after situations but is not a guaranteed transaction.
Divine timing and God's will remain important.
Faith systems should not become generic spellcasting.
Rare battle declarations or cinematic Scripture moments can be used carefully and meaningfully.
Established references include Philippians 4:13, Psalm 23, Psalm 91, Romans 8:31 and Armor of God passages.
Scripture should be used respectfully and contextually.
16. HUD SYSTEM
The BTV HUD should have a consistent design language: dark blue/black interface panels, restrained gold
Covenant accents, blue energy indicators, clean futuristic lines and cross/Covenant-inspired iconography.
Typical elements: Asher portrait/name/level/status, health/stamina, quest/objectives, compass, minimap, Covenant
Credits, Aces, Ember panel, interaction prompts, abilities, dialogue and contextual information.
The HUD must adapt to context rather than displaying every element equally all the time.
Exploration: cleaner. Combat: denser. Stealth: minimized. Investigation: evidence/analysis emphasized. Dialogue:
conversation prioritized. Chapel/community: interaction/social elements emphasized. Veil Sight: supernatural
traces and signatures emphasized.

23. CANNIBALISM / THE DEVOURERS — NEW STORY
ELEMENT
A new BTV story element has been approved conceptually: a rare group known as the Devourers, associated with
cannibalism in a severe survival/crisis context.
This should be rare, disturbing and morally serious—not a routine enemy faction, joke or glorified lifestyle.
The game should avoid graphic depictions. The horror can be communicated through survivor testimony,
missing-person investigations, dialogue, environmental clues and consequences.
The Devourers should be internally diverse: some violent and unrepentant, some coerced, some frightened
followers, some manipulated by a leader, and some people who want to escape.
This creates room for preaching, rescue, accountability, defense and possible redemption.
24. DEVOURER ENCOUNTER PHILOSOPHY
When Asher encounters the Devourers, the game should not automatically present a single 'kill' solution.
Possible approaches: Speak/Preach, Investigate, Rescue, Defend, Withdraw and—when necessary—protect
civilians from active aggression.
Preaching does not guarantee conversion.
Some people may listen. Some may reject Asher. Some may become more hostile. Some may remember his
words and seek help later.
If people are actively attacking innocent civilians, protecting life takes priority.
25. DEVOURER STORY ARC — EXAMPLE
Act I — Whispers: People disappear around an isolated settlement.
Act II — The Settlement: Asher investigates and discovers disturbing evidence.
Act III — The Invitation: The Devourer leader agrees to speak with Asher.
Act IV — The Choice: The leader rejects the message; some followers become hostile while others hesitate.
Act V — Aftermath: Depending on choices, people may be rescued, followers may leave, the leader may remain
dangerous, evidence may reveal a wider network, or a former follower may later seek the Covenant.

26. EXAMPLE DEVOURER PREACHING ATTEMPTS
Compassion: “You don't have to remain what you've become.”
Truth: “Survival doesn't make another person's life worthless.”
Faith: “There is still a way back.”
Investigation: “Tell me what happened here.”
Rescue: “There are people here who don't want this.”
Defense: “These people are innocent. You will not harm them.”
Dialogue should remain context-sensitive. The player should not be rewarded with an automatic conversion simply
for selecting a Christian line.
27. WHY THE DEVOURERS FIT BTV
The storyline reinforces truth vs deception, mercy vs vengeance, protection vs revenge, redemption vs
condemnation and meaningful choice.
It also tests whether the player's faith-oriented approach remains compassionate under genuine danger.
The Veil can amplify fear, isolation and desperation without becoming an excuse that removes human
responsibility.
The strongest version of the story asks: Can Asher recognize the humanity of people who have done terrible
things while still protecting innocent lives?
28. VISUAL TREATMENT OF THE DEVOURERS
The Devourers should look like survivors shaped by isolation and crisis rather than generic fantasy cannibals.
Clothing can be improvised, weathered and culturally varied according to the settlement.
Their environment can show signs of severe scarcity, fear and social breakdown without graphic gore.
Use environmental storytelling, tense body language, guarded camps, warnings, abandoned belongings,
missing-person boards and dialogue rather than graphic imagery.
Keep the scene cinematic, mature and serious.
29. DIVERSITY RULE
BTV should represent a believable range of people.
Asher's identity remains established and consistent.
Other NPCs should naturally vary by region, city, district, migration patterns, culture, occupation and story
circumstances.
Do not force every location into the same demographic pattern.
Do not make diversity disappear either: public spaces should generally feel socially alive and varied.
