BEYOND THE VEIL: THE LAST WATCH
RECENT ADDITIONS & IMPROVEMENTS — INCREMENTAL CANON ADDENDUM
ONLY additions established after the previous full game-description document are included 
here.
1. Living World Memory / Echoes
Meaningful actions leave traces. Rescued people may remember the protagonist; restored 
communities can visibly recover; survivors can report battles; teammates and Covenant 
personnel can remember important decisions.
2. Discernment & Deception
Deception is not a simple evil meter. Players may combine observation, evidence, Scripture 
knowledge, prayer and teammate advice to recognize deception. The player can sometimes 
misunderstand situations before discovering the truth.
3. Covenant Evidence Board
Larger Covenant facilities can have a physical investigation board where photographs, witness 
reports, maps, documents, enemy information and Scout reports can be connected.
4. Covenant Watch Rotations
Covenant teams actively monitor different regions. Information must travel through believable 
communication and intelligence channels rather than being known everywhere automatically.
5. Personal Quarters
The protagonist can eventually have personal quarters containing persistent objects such as the 
physical Bible, photographs, maps, equipment, letters and mission artifacts.
6. Horse Environmental Awareness
Horses can subtly react to unusual environments, danger or Veil disturbances, but they are not 
magical supernatural detectors.

7. Regional World Identity
Major regions receive distinct identities. Coastal areas can emphasize fishing and boats; forests 
wildlife and tracking; cities crowds and infrastructure; mountains difficult traversal and remote
facilities.
8. Covenant Communication Network
Covenant facilities can connect through a network such as Outpost → Station → Regional HQ → 
Command. Communication is not necessarily instantaneous.
9. Covenant Link
An original Covenant field communication device supports team communication, Scout reports, 
emergencies, mission updates, coordination, distress signals and command instructions.
10. Signal Dead Zones
Mountains, forests, storms, damaged infrastructure, remote areas and some supernatural 
disturbances can interfere with communication.
11. Smartphones & Original Apps
Smartphones are physical items that can be bought, sold, upgraded and repaired. They support 
calls, messages, camera, video, maps, notes, photos and contacts. An original messaging service, 
LinkLine, supports individual and group chats. The Covenant App provides authorized missions,
announcements, alerts, reports, team information and other approved functions.
12. NPC Communication
NPCs can own different levels of phones, exchange contacts, call, message and use cameras. The 
protagonist gradually builds a contact list through actual relationships and encounters.
13. Technology Economy
Phones, cameras, batteries, storage, chargers, headsets and accessories can be bought or sold 
through appropriate merchants. Availability varies by region and economy.
14. Device Repair
Repair shops and Covenant technical facilities can replace batteries and repair screens, charging
ports, cameras, storage/components and other device problems. Battery health can gradually 
decline and be restored by replacement.

15. Covenant Technical Personnel
Every dedicated Covenant Station has technical personnel; small outposts do not necessarily 
have dedicated technicians. Station technicians have a primary specialty plus broad related 
expertise so the protagonist has useful reasons to visit them. They can support communications,
phones, cameras, Covenant Link, equipment diagnostics and technical investigations.
16. Unknown Number Investigation
Station technical personnel can investigate unknown numbers. Results may include known 
contact, previously reported, unregistered, masked, possible spoofing, approximate region, 
insufficient data or a possible connection to another incident. Tracing is not guaranteed.
17. Physical Data Devices
USB drives, memory cards, portable drives and Covenant data modules can contain reports, 
photographs, videos, maps and signal records and can become physical evidence.
18. Lost Communication Events
A broken transmission can become a world event. Causes may include equipment failure, 
storms, damaged infrastructure, enemy activity or Veil disturbances, creating choices about 
whether to investigate.
19. Technical Teammate Role
Some teammates specialize in technology and can improve signal interpretation, device repair, 
evidence recovery and communications support without becoming unrealistic super-hackers.
20. Photography & Evidence
The protagonist can use both a dedicated digital camera and a smartphone camera. Photos can 
reveal overlooked clues and later be reviewed, transferred and connected to the evidence board.
21. Device Damage
Phones and cameras can be damaged by falls, combat, water and harsh environments. Minor 
problems can receive field repairs; major repairs require appropriate facilities.
22. Backpack System
The protagonist has a physical backpack for the Bible, smartphone, camera, batteries, food, 
water, medical supplies, tools, evidence, maps and other supplies. Capacity is practical rather 
than unlimited.

23. Weight & Bulk
Inventory uses simple Light, Moderate, Heavy and Very Heavy categories. Excessive load can 
gradually affect stamina, movement, climbing and combat mobility.
24. Storage Layers
Backpack = immediate equipment; horse saddlebags = expedition supplies; safehouse = larger 
personal storage; Covenant facilities = organizational resources.
25. Mount Logistics
Horses can carry supplies through saddlebags, making them useful for longer expeditions.
26. Mount Needs
Horses need food, water, rest, grooming, health and shelter. Neglect gradually affects stamina, 
temperament, recovery and travel performance without becoming a full horse simulator.
27. Horse Bonding
Repeated care and use can build trust and familiarity, improving responsiveness and reliability 
without magical powers.
28. Mount Management
Appropriate facilities allow stabling, feeding, watering, grooming, resting, saddle management, 
saddlebags and storage. Saddles can favor travel, cargo, combat or exploration.
29. Field Repair Kit
The protagonist can carry a small kit for basic or temporary repairs. Major work still requires 
specialists.
30. Expedition Preparation
Before long journeys, the game can recommend reasonable supplies and let the player adjust the
loadout without excessive micromanagement.
31. Technology Story Events
Unknown numbers, suspicious messages, broken transmissions, missing teammates and 
photographs containing clues can become story events.

32. Regional Technology Availability
Technology ranges from basic phones to smartphones, dedicated cameras and Covenant 
equipment, with availability and prices changing by region.
33. Covenant Operations Room
Larger stations and headquarters can have maps, communications equipment, computers, 
evidence areas and technical personnel for intelligence, repairs, communication and planning.
34. Covenant Network Map
Later leadership gameplay can show facilities as Connected, Limited, Offline or Disturbance 
Detected, allowing deployment of Scouts, technical teams or Guardians.
35. Mercy & Consequence
Mercy does not guarantee a positive outcome. A spared enemy operative could provide 
information or create future uncertainty. Decisions should have meaningful consequences 
without reducing Christianity to a simplistic morality score.
36. Enemy Morale
Enemies may retreat, call for help, protect commanders, surrender, deceive or become more 
aggressive depending on circumstances.
37. Bible Personal History
The physical Bible can become personally meaningful through remembered or marked passages
connected to important moments, without becoming a magical item.
38. New Scripture References
Galatians 6:2 is associated with helping and bearing one another’s burdens. Philippians 2:3–4 is 
associated with humility and considering the interests of others. These should appear 
contextually through dialogue, sermons, the physical Bible, journal reflection, teammate 
interactions or story moments, not arbitrary stat bonuses.
39. Watch Events / No-Marker Discoveries
World events can emerge through observation instead of map markers: observe → speak → 
investigate → discern → decide → act → consequence.

40. General’s War Table
After the protagonist eventually becomes General, a physical war table can show regions, 
Covenant teams, enemy activity, civilian danger, resources and Scout intelligence. The General 
can deploy teams and resources while still personally playing missions.
41. Quiet Faith Moments
Rare optional moments at chapels, churches or appropriate locations can allow sitting, prayer, 
Bible reading and reflection without always giving visible rewards.
42. Covenant Stations as Strategic Hubs
Stations support technical work, intelligence, medical needs, equipment, communication, team 
deployment, planning and training.
43. Facility Distinction
Small Outposts provide field presence, basic supplies and communications but do not 
necessarily have dedicated technicians. Covenant Stations have dedicated technical personnel, 
communications, repair, signal analysis, intelligence and stronger logistics/medical support. 
Regional HQs coordinate multiple facilities and have more specialists.
44. Integrated Design Rule
New systems should reinforce one another: Technology → Communication → Investigation → 
Covenant intelligence → Mission → Consequence; Backpack → Horse → Preparation → 
Exploration; Bible → Scripture → Reflection → Faith context; Technicians → Repairs/Analysis → 
Reliable world support.
