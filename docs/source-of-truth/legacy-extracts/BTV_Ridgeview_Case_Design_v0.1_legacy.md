## 0. Executive decision

**Recommended case direction:** Ridgeview is a community under coercive control and information isolation. Dorian Pike has knowingly helped conceal the outlying camp and the movement of residents because he believes compliance is the only way to keep the settlement from suffering worse consequences. Wren has participated in small acts of concealment under pressure, but she is not the architect and wants to leave. The repairman discovered discrepancies, attempted to leave a trace, and went toward the camp; his present location and fate remain unresolved at the end of the slice.

The immediate case payoff is not “Asher discovers the monster.” It is that he establishes that the settlement is not deserted, the trader's “shut for weeks” claim is wrong, the missing-person situation is real, Pike has withheld material information, and the camp is connected to the settlement's silence. Asher can act meaningfully without receiving a complete explanation.

**Why this is the strongest direction:**
- It preserves the locked mystery around the repairman's fate.
- It gives Pike a coherent human motive without excusing his choices.
- It gives Wren agency and complexity without making her an exposition device.
- It makes investigation, listening, rescue, defense and withdrawal materially distinct.
- It creates a satisfying local resolution while leaving the larger Veil and Devourer questions open.
- It supports BTV's central themes: truth, trust, fear, manipulation, community protection, responsibility and redemption.

This is a design recommendation, not a canon change. If any detail conflicts with a higher-authority source, the higher source wins.

## 1. Canon anchors that this design must preserve

### 1.1 Locked slice facts

- Asher Vance is the sole playable protagonist and Chosen; he is presented as 18, an Agent/Specialist and Watcher.
- Ember accompanies him; no human Shepherd joins the field operation.
- Ridgeview is an isolated settlement with missing people, a local leader, evidence and a decision.
- The local leader is Dorian Pike. Wren is a frightened resident who wants out.
- The repairman is missing and is not confirmed dead. His fate remains unresolved in this slice.
- The Scout Report does not name or hint at the Devourers.
- The trader's claim that the outpost has been shut for weeks is wrong and can be disproved on site.
- The Scout's categories remain Observed, Reported, Inferred and Unconfirmed. Unverified is reserved for player-created Evidence Board connections.
- Ridgeview begins at Disturbed on the approach and reads as Influenced around the settlement/camp. Veil-Touched, Veil Breach and Critical are not reached.
- The Veil's activity is expressed through quiet and unreliable signals/devices, intensifying toward the camp. It creates no monsters in this slice and does not excuse human choices.
- The Devourers are never named or identified to the player in the slice, including dialogue, UI, Covenant classification and Asher's report.
- No children appear at Ridgeview. Content notice appears before arrival. No gore, on-screen remains or graphic descriptions.
- Psalm 23 appears once at the authored threshold moment. It is not a spell, buff, healing action or guaranteed solution.
- The five approaches are Speak/Preach, Investigate, Rescue, Defend and Withdraw. They can combine; Withdraw is legitimate.
- No XP, Aces, Credits, equipment, stat increase, morality score or spiritual score is awarded for the response.
- Asher writes his own final report and may not overstate what the evidence supports.

### 1.2 Established scene elements

- Approach/perimeter: watched road, abandoned belongings, parked work vehicle with toolkit, beginning of repairman's trail.
- Settlement: people are present; residents are wary but may answer if Asher listens first.
- Wren wants to speak away from the others.
- Outlying camp contains the hardest evidence about what became of the repairman, but not a complete explanation.
- Pike can be approached by listening, presenting evidence, preaching or leaving.
- Evidence Board synthesis precedes or accompanies the response decision.

## 2. Private case truth — PROPOSED

This section is for writers and mission design. It is not automatically revealed to the player.

### 2.1 What happened

Ridgeview's isolation began with a real disruption to its ordinary supply and communication routine. The disruption made residents dependent on a small number of people controlling information, movement and access to outside help. A coercive group began using the outlying camp as a place to hold, move or pressure people away from the settlement's centre. The group is associated with the Devourer crisis, but the vertical slice must not identify or name it to Asher or the player.

Pike initially cooperated because he believed he could limit harm by keeping the settlement quiet and complying with demands. He then continued to conceal facts after he had enough evidence that his choices were harming people. His original fear may be understandable; his later decisions remain his responsibility.

Wren helped with minor tasks that concealed movement and discouraged questions. She did so under pressure and fear, not because she shared Pike's aims. She knows some practical facts about routes and timing, but she does not know the group's origin or the repairman's ultimate fate.

The repairman noticed inconsistencies in the supply and maintenance records. He attempted to document what he had seen and left a trace associated with his toolkit/work log. He went toward the outlying camp. The evidence establishes that he reached or approached the camp area, but it does not establish what happened after that. He is not confirmed dead, rescued or held at the end of the slice.

### 2.2 What Pike knows

Pike knows:
- The settlement has not been shut for weeks; people remain.
- The normal supply and communication pattern has been disrupted.
- Some residents have been moved or directed to the outlying camp under pressure.
- The repairman questioned the records and went toward the camp.
- The situation is dangerous and outside his control.

Pike does not know:
- The Veil's ultimate nature.
- The full identity, origin or hierarchy of the group applying pressure.
- The repairman's final fate.
- Whether compliance will keep the settlement safe.

Pike's central self-deception: he tells himself that concealment is temporary protection. The evidence and his decisions show that he has crossed from frightened improvisation into active complicity.

### 2.3 What Wren knows

Wren knows:
- The outlying camp is relevant to the missing people.
- Residents are discouraged from discussing it.
- The repairman asked questions and did not return to the settlement's normal routine.
- Pike has withheld information.
- Leaving is difficult and may expose others to consequences.

Wren does not know:
- The repairman's final fate.
- The group's true identity or origin.
- The Veil's cause or rules.

Wren's contradictions under stress are about timing, sequence and what she personally witnessed. They must not be written as arbitrary lying. If she says she “heard” something, she cannot later be treated as having seen it.

### 2.4 What the residents know

Residents have uneven, local knowledge. Most know only what they personally experienced: missed deliveries, changed routines, warnings not to travel, a neighbour who stopped appearing, or a radio that no longer works reliably. They do not share a single collective explanation. Some believe Pike is protecting them; others distrust him; many are simply trying to get through the day.

No resident is a universal lore source. No civilian is automatically malicious because they are afraid, mistaken, outside the Covenant or not a believer.

### 2.5 What the Covenant knows

The Scout knows only the contents and limits of the Scout Report. The duty officer knows the report and whatever was transmitted through authorised channels. Neither knows the private case truth. The Covenant does not classify the group by its secret internal name in this slice. Asher's final report must preserve the same boundary.

## 5. Clue architecture and evidence chain

The following are proposed authored clue contents that fit the locked clue slots. Every clue must have a provenance record in the final implementation.

| ID | Location/source | What Asher can establish | Supports | Does not prove |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| RV-E01 | Approach road and recent-use traces | The road has seen little or no ordinary vehicle traffic during the Scout's observation window | Scout's direct observation | That nobody entered by another route or on foot |
| RV-E02 | Parked work vehicle and toolkit | A repair worker was present; toolkit ownership/marking can support the visitor connection if that optional scene was heard | Repairman was at Ridgeview; visitor connection becomes Supported only if identity mark matches | Repairman's fate or who took him |
| RV-E03 | Settlement activity | People are present and the settlement is not shut | Contradicts trader's “shut for weeks” claim | That the settlement is safe or operating normally |
| RV-E04 | Building lights / occupancy traces | At least one building was occupied or used recently | Tests the Scout's inference | Identity, number or intentions of occupants |
| RV-E05 | Radio equipment / record | A response occurred; its content and signal quality can be reviewed if recorded | Confirms the Scout's radio observation if the same event is available | Speaker identity, motive or supernatural cause |
| RV-E06 | Wren's private testimony | Wren knows the camp is relevant and can describe one or more events she personally witnessed | Camp connection; selected timeline points | Repairman's final fate; full group identity |
| RV-E07 | Supply/work log | Records contain a discrepancy between expected work/supply activity and what Pike publicly says | Pike's account is incomplete | Who caused every discrepancy or why |
| RV-E08 | Outlying camp: orderly traces and repairman's work record | The repairman reached or approached the camp area and left evidence of an attempt to document what he found | Trail to camp; missing-person concern | Death, captivity, escape or identity of the group |
| RV-E09 | Pike's account under evidence-led questioning | Pike admits or is demonstrably contradicted on one material point | Deliberate concealment | Every allegation against Pike |
| RV-E10 | Optional independent resident account | A second source confirms a specific event from a separate opportunity to observe | Corroboration of that event | A complete shared theory of the incident |

### 5.1 Mandatory versus optional information

**Critical information must have at least two routes where practical:**
- Settlement occupied: direct observation from the public area; a resident account is optional corroboration.
- Camp relevance: Wren's account or the physical trail; both are stronger together.
- Pike's concealment: evidence-led contradiction or voluntary admission; do not require one exact dialogue branch.
- Repairman reached camp area: work trail plus record/toolkit context; if one is missed, Asher may report only that the trail points toward the camp.

Optional evidence may improve confidence, expose a contradiction, make rescue safer or add context. It must not become an invisible checklist that blocks the mission.

### 5.2 Fair clue presentation

- Each critical clue has a readable interaction affordance and an authored description.
- Important evidence is not hidden in a tiny pixel, an obscure sound or a single lighting angle.
- Photographs preserve what is actually visible; they do not add forensic facts the image cannot support.
- The player can revisit collected evidence in the Journal.
- An evidence link is a player hypothesis until the source chain supports it.

## 6. Pike: dialogue and accountability model

Pike should be convincing because much of his account is true. He is not a theatrical villain who confesses everything when challenged.

### 6.1 Conversation phases

1. **Initial contact:** Pike welcomes or cautiously questions Asher. He offers ordinary explanations: supply disruption, radio trouble and residents keeping to themselves.
2. **Listening:** Asher can ask about routines, missing travellers and the repairman without accusation. Pike answers with selective truth and observable gaps.
3. **Evidence-led challenge:** Asher can present one verified contradiction. Pike adjusts his account, minimises or admits a bounded fact. He does not suddenly explain the whole plot.
4. **Moral appeal / Speak-Preach:** Asher can speak truthfully about responsibility, fear, protecting people and the need to let others make informed choices. Scripture may be used only in an authored context. It does not force confession or conversion.
5. **Exit:** Asher can leave the conversation. Pike's state is recorded as unresolved if the evidence is insufficient.

### 6.2 Pike response matrix

| Player conduct | Pike response | System interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| Listens without accusation | More willing to describe routine and pressure | Access to context, not automatic trust |
| Asks about the trader's claim | Confirms people remain; may blame rumours | Contradicts trader without proving Pike honest |
| Presents the work log | Recognises the discrepancy; attempts to narrow responsibility | Material contradiction, not full confession |
| Presents Wren's testimony | Challenges her reliability or claims she misunderstood | Must be evaluated against independent evidence |
| Speaks/Preaches | May listen, resist, deflect or show uncertainty | No conversion score or guaranteed change |
| Threatens or rushes him | Becomes defensive; dialogue may close | Escalation has practical costs; no arbitrary combat trigger |
| Leaves | Conversation remains incomplete | Mission can continue or withdraw honestly |

### 6.3 Accountability principle

The story may explain Pike's fear without treating fear as an excuse. His later choices have consequences. If he repents or cooperates, that does not erase harm or guarantee forgiveness from affected residents. Legal disposition remains outside Asher's unilateral authority and is not represented by arrest/detention flags.

## 7. Wren: testimony and agency model

Wren's role is to make the human cost legible, not to deliver the whole answer.

- She chooses whether and when to speak privately.
- She can leave with Asher if the player chooses Rescue and the authored conditions are met.
- She may decline to repeat details in front of Pike or other residents.
- She distinguishes what she saw, what she heard and what she fears.
- Her account can be incomplete without being malicious.
- If the player does not rescue her, the story records her actual outcome; it must not imply that she was saved.
- Her future trust is earned through consistent actions, not a hidden affinity score.

## 8. The five response approaches and combined paths

The five approaches are not five mutually exclusive endings. They are strategic priorities that may be combined.

### 8.1 Speak/Preach

**Primary objective:** communicate truth, offer help and invite responsible action without coercion.

- Best supported by listening and a respectful conversation.
- Can be attempted before every clue is found.
- May create an opening for Pike or residents to disclose information.
- May be rejected or misunderstood.
- Does not guarantee conversion, repentance, safety or revelation.
- Does not replace rescue or protective action when someone is in immediate danger.

### 8.2 Investigate

**Primary objective:** improve the factual basis for a decision.

- Cross-checks the Scout Report, physical clues, testimony and records.
- More investigation can cost time and increase exposure, as established by the slice.
- The player can stop with partial information and still act.
- No universal “correct deduction” is forced by the Board.

### 8.3 Rescue

**Primary objective:** assist Wren and any other person who freely wants help, and pursue safe leads about the repairman.

- Requires locating the person and planning a feasible route.
- Does not presume the repairman is alive or reachable.
- May sacrifice the chance to collect additional evidence or maintain tactical advantage.
- The game records who actually leaves and who remains.

### 8.4 Defend

**Primary objective:** protect people against an immediate, credible threat.

- Combat is not the default investigation tool.
- A hostile response must have an authored cause; dialogue disagreement alone is not enough.
- Hostiles may retreat or yield as supported by the slice's Enemy Morale concept.
- Mercy is possible; no graphic depiction is needed.
- Do not reward violence or punish restraint with morality points.

### 8.5 Withdraw

**Primary objective:** preserve life, exit a situation that exceeds Asher's capability or risk tolerance, and report accurately.

- Always available as a legitimate choice.
- Can happen before the camp, after Wren's testimony or after confrontation.
- The report records the actual evidence gathered and the unresolved questions.
- Later Covenant follow-up uses the recorded facts; it does not retroactively claim Asher completed tasks he skipped.

### 8.6 Combination examples

| Path | Example sequence | Likely consequence shape |
|---|---|---|
| Listen → Investigate → Speak | Build trust, corroborate, then challenge Pike | Better context and a chance of voluntary cooperation |
| Investigate → Rescue → Withdraw | Establish a safe route, assist Wren, leave before escalation | Civilian protection prioritised; case remains partly unresolved |
| Speak → Investigate → Defend | Attempt de-escalation, discover immediate threat, protect people | Defence is reactive and context-grounded |
| Investigate → Withdraw | Gather enough to warn the Covenant, avoid overreach | Honest incomplete report; follow-up required |
| Rescue → Speak | Help Wren first, then offer Pike a chance to cooperate | Human safety is prioritised; dialogue remains non-coercive |

These are examples, not exclusive branches or moral rankings.

## 9. Outcome model

Outcomes are multidimensional world-state records, not a single success/failure grade.

### 9.1 Required independent dimensions

- **Wren:** stayed / left with Asher / left with another safe party / unknown.
- **Pike:** unresolved / confronted and remained / left settlement / fled / cooperated in a bounded way. Use only values supported by actual slice scripting; do not imply legal detention.
- **Repairman trail:** not found / trail found / trail followed toward camp.
- **Repairman status:** unresolved throughout this slice.
- **Evidence:** list of documented evidence IDs and claim statuses.
- **Approaches:** set of approaches actually used.
- **Combat:** whether combat occurred; no score attached.
- **Covenant follow-up:** rescue support / restoration support / further investigation / return survey / none assigned, selected only if the report supports it.
- **Veil state at exit:** Disturbed or Influenced, consistent with the actual location and observations.

### 9.2 Avoid a binary ending

A player may protect Wren while leaving the repairman unresolved. Another may establish Pike's concealment but be unable to secure cooperation. Another may withdraw with incomplete evidence. These are distinct, legible consequences, not ranked endings.

### 9.3 No false closure

The final report and Journal must preserve:
- What Asher personally observed.
- What another person reported.
- What Asher inferred.
- What was corroborated.
- What was contradicted.
- What remains unknown.

The repairman's status remains unresolved. A possible connection to a wider threat remains a lead, not a confirmed explanation.

## 10. Five-revelation pacing for Ridgeview

This is a pacing proposal and is independent of the six Veil incident stages.

| Revelation | Player experience | Supported understanding | Still unresolved |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. The Signs | Quiet road, unusual absence of wildlife, one lit building, unfamiliar radio response | Something is unusual; the Scout did not know the full situation | Cause, occupancy, threat |
| 2. The Pattern | People are present; routine and communication are disrupted; the camp is avoided | The trader's claim is wrong; isolation is more than an empty settlement | Who controls events and why |
| 3. The Influence | Devices and signals remain unreliable; residents show fear; effects intensify toward camp | The environment and information chain are affected; this is consistent with Influenced | Ultimate Veil mechanism |
| 4. Competing Understandings | Pike's protective explanation, Wren's fear, residents' fragments and physical records conflict | Pike withheld facts; Wren has bounded first-hand knowledge; no single account is complete | Full group identity, repairman's fate |
| 5. Local Arc Revelation | The player establishes that the camp is tied to coercive isolation and the repairman's trail, then chooses a response | Ridgeview is not simply deserted or suffering routine supply failure; human concealment and an anomalous local situation overlap | Repairman's fate, Devourer identity/origin, Veil's ultimate nature, larger network |

**Payoff rule:** The local case must answer what the evidence can support. Do not defer every answer. The player should leave with a better-founded understanding and a consequential decision, even though the repairman's fate and wider mysteries remain open.

| Character | May know | May believe | Must not know by default |
|---|---|---|---|
| Asher | Scout Report; what he personally observes; evidence gathered; testimony heard | Hypotheses supported by his evidence | Hidden case facts not discovered; Veil origin; repairman's fate |

## 12. Environmental and audio storytelling

Use restraint. The atmosphere should build through absence, inconsistency and human behaviour rather than spectacle.

- Approach: normal environmental sound becomes noticeably sparse; the change is gradual and can be noticed visually as well.
- Settlement: signs of ordinary life remain, but routines are constrained and residents watch conversations carefully.
- Camp threshold: sound and radio reliability worsen in a way consistent with the approved slice's Influenced presentation.
- No environmental clue should imply a new universal Veil law.
- No essential clue may depend exclusively on hearing a quiet sound.
- Ember's reactions are local cues, not an answer system.
- Psalm 23's threshold scene remains authored and singular; it is not tied to finding a clue or choosing a response.

## 13. Mission flow and fail-forward checkpoints

1. **Arrival:** player can observe the perimeter before entering.
2. **Public settlement:** direct observation disproves the trader's claim.
3. **Wren opportunity:** she signals privately; player may engage, defer or miss it.
4. **Evidence collection:** at least three inspectable clues are available; additional evidence improves context.
5. **Pike conversation:** available before or after some evidence; evidence changes dialogue but does not unlock all contact.
6. **Camp threshold:** Psalm 23 scene fires once if the authored story-state conditions are met.
7. **Camp investigation:** hardest repairman evidence; exact fate remains unresolved.
8. **Synthesis:** Board distinguishes evidence from player hypotheses.
9. **Decision:** player can combine approaches; Withdraw remains available.
10. **Final report:** Asher reports only what he can support.
11. **Covenant response:** follow-up matches actual state and leaves unresolved matters open.

### 13.1 Missed or failed interactions

- If Wren is missed, physical evidence and Pike's dialogue still support a meaningful investigation; the personal testimony is absent.
- If the toolkit is missed, the visitor connection remains Unverified or not made; do not invent a match.
- If the work log is missed, Asher may still establish that the trail points toward the camp, but cannot claim the documented discrepancy.
- If Pike leaves or refuses conversation, the player can report the evidence gathered; no forced confession scene.
- If the player withdraws early, later follow-up is a possible consequence, not an automatic off-screen success.
- If the player chooses Defend, the triggering threat and outcome must be authored; combat does not magically reveal the case truth.
