1. PROJECT IDENTITY
• Beyond the Veil: The Last Watch (BTV) is an original fictional modern Christian action-adventure survival RPG.
• The world is semi-open: connected cities, settlements, wilderness, facilities, districts, hidden locations and
Veil-influenced areas.
• It explores biblical/end-times spiritual conflict without retelling the Old Testament or New Testament as the game's
plot.
• Core themes include the Armor of God, Scripture, prayer, evangelism, redemption, service, discernment and spiritual
conflict.
• Originality is a priority. Use other games only for high-level design principles, never direct copying.
2. PROTAGONIST + CHOSEN
• Asher Vance is the established protagonist name; the established age range is 16–19.
• The protagonist begins as a trained Covenant field member and can progress Team Leader  Commander  General
through story, service and leadership rather than ordinary XP alone.
• There is a player-choice concept involving Male or Female Chosen options with distinct backstories and personalities.
• Both Chosen remain important. The player-selected Chosen becomes General; the other remains important.
3. THE COVENANT
• The Covenant is a hidden Christian organization serving God and communities; it is not the government and is not
identical to the whole Church.
• Hierarchy: Recruits  Agents/Specialists  Team Leaders  Commanders  Generals.
• Roles include Guardians, Messengers, Watchers, Shepherds, Scouts and Keepers.
• Rank represents responsibility, not spiritual superiority.
• God is the ultimate authority/Master and is not a playable combat character.
• The Holy Spirit provides guidance, conviction, warnings, wisdom and discernment rather than functioning as a
controllable attack/magic resource.
• Oath: By truth we stand. / By faith we endure. / By the Light we rise. / Darkness will not prevail.
4. FAITH + SPIRITUAL SYSTEMS
• Prayer can occur in or after situations, but it is not a guaranteed transaction or loot mechanic; divine timing and God's
will matter.
• Christ is central and above the Covenant/Chosen; the Holy Spirit is above the organization's hierarchy.
• Armor: Belt of Truth, Breastplate of Righteousness, Shoes of Gospel of Peace, Shield of Faith, Helmet of Salvation and
Sword of the Spirit.
• The Sword of the Spirit should have a medieval-meets-modern visual feel.
• Possible cinematic Scripture includes Philippians 4:13, Psalm 23, Psalm 91 and Romans 8:31, plus Armor passages.
• Do not turn Scripture into a purchasable commodity or make faith power simply equivalent to a videogame mana bar.
5. WORLD, ENEMIES + EVENTS
• Destroyers = direct combat threats; Persuaders = influence/manipulation; Deceivers = illusions, ambushes and
misinformation; Leaders/bosses = higher-level threats.
• Scouts can report groups or situations. The player may engage, investigate, rescue or continue.
• Battles should not be nonstop; investigation, travel, rescue, restoration and community activity matter.
• Drops can include resources, clues, materials, artifacts and equipment.
• Potential themes include the Seven Deadly Sins, Antichrist-related conflict and consecrated bread, handled with care
and coherent worldbuilding.
• Restoration, evangelism, charity and Mercy/Samaritan-style missions can create meaningful consequences.

6. ECONOMY + HCI
• Covenant Credits are for Covenant systems; Aces are general currency usable in both Covenant and normal world.
• Faith is not something the player buys. HCI giving/charity should not become pay-to-win.
• In-game HCI chapels can vary by district, with NPCs preparing for and attending services.
• KITFIT before preaching can involve Scripture plus a short giving sermon; giving is voluntary and not pressure-based.
• Giving should not be measured only by amount; wealth is not moral worth.
• Monthly Communion occurs on the first Sunday. Communion Set (wine+bread single item) is reserved for the
CommunionContextZone; outside service context it should communicate that it is reserved rather than granting
combat stats.
• Easter and Christmas can be represented as limited festivals.
7. VISUAL IDENTITY + HUD
• Covenant uniforms use a white base with restrained gold and a cross-centered Christian insignia.
• Variants: modern tactical, formal ceremonial, field stealth, regional weather, specialist and
rank/branch/gender/sleeve/duty variants.
• Rare ancient or God-/Holy-Spirit-guided variants may exist.
• HUD should change contextually rather than remaining identical in every situation.
• Ember is the established animal companion role. Companion systems: Physical Growth, Bond Growth and Spiritual
Awakening.
• Abilities include Veil Sight, Guardian Instinct, Veil Energy, Tracking Sense and Spirit Guidance.
• Commands include attack, protect, scout, track, retrieve, hold and regroup. Companion AI should be adaptive.
8. AI DEVELOPMENT STACK
• ChatGPT — creative director, systems architect, lore/canon, design documentation and reasoning.
• Cursor — primary coding and codebase engineering.
• MiniMax Agent/Code — autonomous implementation, debugging, testing and review.
• Godot — actual game engine, scenes, gameplay and testing.
• GitHub — source control, history and collaboration.
• Blender — 3D modeling, cleanup, rigging and optimization.
• Leonardo AI — concept art and visual exploration.
• Meshy / Tripo3D — 3D asset generation/prototyping.
• GitHub Codespaces — browser/phone development and Git operations.
• Potentially — model experimentation for image/video/audio/creative work.
• Replit Agent / Firebase Studio — supporting tools, especially web/app prototypes.
• Critical: Cursor and MiniMax are complementary; neither may silently redefine BTV canon.

10. MOBILE / REMOTE WORKFLOW
• Phone: ChatGPT for planning/design; GitHub Codespaces for browser coding/Git operations; MiniMax remote control
for supported desktop-agent workflows.
• PC: Cursor for serious codebase work; Godot for game development/testing; Blender for 3D; GitHub for version control.
• A large AAA-scale project should not be treated as something that can realistically be produced entirely from an
Android phone. The phone is a control/planning surface; PC remains important for Godot, Blender and serious
execution.
11. PRIORITY TIERS
• Tier S — Essential: ChatGPT, Cursor, Godot, GitHub, Blender.
• Tier A — Extremely useful: MiniMax Agent, Leonardo AI, Meshy, Tripo3D.
• Tier B — Supporting: GitHub Codespaces, Potentially, Replit Agent, Firebase Studio.
12. ACTION PLAN
• 01 — Lock the canon: maintain a master BTV canon document; AI-generated proposals are non-canon until approved.
• 02 — Establish repository discipline: use GitHub version control, frequent working milestones and isolated experiments.
• 03 — Build a vertical slice: movement, one environment, one companion loop, one enemy family, one mission, one faith
interaction and contextual HUD.
• 04 — Use agents deliberately: explicit tasks, acceptance criteria, boundaries, changed-file summaries and tests.
• 05 — Build the asset pipeline: concept  approved design  3D starting asset  Blender cleanup/optimization  Godot 
device testing.
• 06 — Test on PC + Android: controls, UI scaling, memory, loading, performance and saves.
• 07 — Protect originality: references inspire principles rather than cloning another game's identity.
13. HANDOFF INSTRUCTION TO NEXT AI/DEVELOPER
• You are joining the development of Beyond the Veil: The Last Watch (BTV). Treat this document as a continuity-first
project handoff. Preserve established names, ages, factions, relationships, mechanics, visual identity, currencies, faith
systems, companion role, HUD philosophy and worldbuilding.
• Do not overwrite established canon without explicit approval. When proposing something new, label it PROPOSAL
rather than CANON.
• Prefer modular, testable implementations. Ask for clarification only when a missing decision materially affects
implementation; otherwise make conservative assumptions and clearly record them.
• Success means turning the established BTV vision into a coherent, playable, original and technically maintainable game
while preserving continuity and its Christian/worldbuilding identity.
