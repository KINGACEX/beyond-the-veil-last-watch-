0. Critical Project Identity
This document describes Game 2. It is completely separate from the user's other game, 
AFTERFALL. Do not combine characters, lore, story, mechanics, code, assets, or development 
decisions from AFTERFALL with this project unless explicitly requested.
Official title: Beyond the Veil: The Last Watch
Main organization: The Covenant
Core conflict: The War Beyond the Veil
1. Game Concept
A modern Christian action-adventure/survival game set in a fictional world where a supernatural 
conflict exists behind ordinary reality. A boundary called the Veil separates normal human reality 
from the supernatural conflict, and the Veil is weakening.
 Action and adventure
 Survival elements
 Semi-open-world exploration
 Story-driven RPG/adventure progression
 Combat and boss encounters
 Investigation
 Rescue and community support
 Evangelism
 Teamwork and leadership
 Prayer, faith and divine intervention
 Biblical and end-times-inspired themes
The game is not intended to simply retell the Old Testament or New Testament. It is a modern 
fictional story inspired by Christian beliefs and biblical themes.

2. Two-Protagonist System
The player chooses between a male protagonist and a female protagonist. Both are chosen for the 
mission, but they are not identical characters with different models.
 Different backgrounds
 Different personal histories
 Different personalities and motivations
 Different early experiences
 Different relationships
 Separate introductory storylines
The two protagonists can begin in different locations and experience separate events. Their paths 
eventually cross, forming a major story moment. They discover that their missions are connected 
and increasingly work together against the same supernatural threat.
3. The Chosen General
The protagonist selected by the player is the one who eventually becomes the Chosen General.
 Choose male male becomes the Chosen General; female remains a major protagonist. →
 Choose female female becomes the Chosen General; male remains a major protagonist. →
The unchosen protagonist is not a backup character, lesser character, or disposable NPC. They 
remain extremely important, can lead missions, command teams, develop their own story, receive 
equipment, make independent decisions, and play a critical role in the final conflict.
Their central relationship is two called people working toward the same mission. Do not force a 
romantic relationship unless explicitly established later.
4. Leadership Progression
The selected protagonist does not become General immediately. Rank is earned through story 
achievements, leadership, service, successful missions, wise decisions and responsibility.
Suggested progression: Existing Covenant Member Field Agent Specialist Team Leader → → → →
Senior Leader Commander General. → →
The protagonist should become a Team Leader around the early-to-mid portion of the game and 
General significantly later.
5. The Covenant
The Covenant is a fictional modern Christian organization responsible for protecting people, 
fighting supernatural evil, investigating strange events, rescuing communities, evangelizing, 
gathering intelligence, training members and coordinating operations.

6. Covenant Branches
The Guardians: Combat and protection specialists. Defend communities, fight Destroyers, protect 
civilians and join major battles.
The Messengers: Communication and evangelism specialists. Communicate with communities, 
evangelize and counter deception.
The Watchers: Investigation and intelligence specialists. Track enemies, investigate supernatural 
events and study the Veil.
The Shepherds: Rescue and restoration specialists. Rescue civilians, establish safe areas and 
support affected communities.
The Keepers: Equipment and resource specialists. Maintain weapons, craft and upgrade 
equipment, maintain safehouses and preserve artifacts.
Scouts: Reconnaissance specialists who discover enemy movement, civilian danger and unusual 
activity and report it to Covenant leadership.
7. Scout Report & Dynamic Battle System
Enemy groups should not simply spawn everywhere. Scouts can discover developing threats and 
report them to leadership.
Example report: “Unusual activity detected in the eastern district. Destroyer presence increasing. 
Civilian risk: HIGH.”
The player can decide whether to engage, investigate, rescue civilians, send another team, 
personally lead the operation, or delay the response.
The intended loop is: Scout Report Player decision Combat / Investigation / Rescue / → → →
Evangelism Rewards Consequences. → →
Large battles should be occasional and meaningful rather than constant.
8. Enemy Factions
Destroyers: Primary physical combat enemies focused on strength, destruction and group 
assaults. They can have variants and become stronger as the story progresses.
Persuaders: Enemies focused on manipulating people, spreading deception and influencing 
communities. Some encounters can be resolved without combat.

Deceivers: Intelligent enemies specializing in illusions, manipulation, ambushes, misdirection and 
intelligence gathering.
Enemy Commanders: Powerful supernatural leaders who command enemy groups and serve as 
major bosses with unique abilities, personalities, environments, mechanics and story significance.
9. Enemy Drops & Currency
Defeated enemies can leave drops. Possible drops include Covenant Credits, salvage, materials, 
corrupted fragments, equipment components, investigation clues and special artifacts.
Primary ordinary currency: Covenant Credits.
 Earned through missions, rescues, investigations, recovering supplies, helping Covenant 
members and completing operations.
 Used for equipment, crafting, upgrades, transportation, safehouse improvements and 
resources.
Faith and spiritual progression must not be reduced to a purchasable currency.
10. Combat & Equipment
Combat should be exciting and skill-based rather than constant. Possible systems include sword 
attacks, combos, dodging, blocking, shield abilities, armor abilities, supernatural abilities, team 
commands and environmental advantages.
The Sword of the Spirit is a signature weapon. It can be developed progressively and should feel 
important without being treated as a generic magic sword.
The Armor of God is acquired progressively:
 Belt of Truth
 Breastplate of Righteousness
 Shoes/Feet prepared with the Gospel of Peace
 Shield of Faith
 Helmet of Salvation
 Sword of the Spirit
Armor pieces should eventually have gameplay functions while remaining recognizable as biblical 
references.
11. Prayer, Divine Intervention & Holy Spirit Guidance
God is central to the game's worldview. God is not an ordinary playable combat companion. Divine
involvement is represented through prayer, guidance, divine intervention, blessings, timing, 
wisdom and protection.

Established prayer phrase: “God, Your will be done.”
Prayer requests can include guidance, protection, knowledge, strength, blessing and needed 
resources/items. Results are not guaranteed or necessarily immediate; outcomes remain subject to 
God's will and timing.
Holy Spirit guidance can appear through inner prompting, warnings, wisdom, guidance toward 
locations, conviction about decisions or understanding of a situation. It should not automatically 
give the player every correct answer.
12. Divine Blessings
At appropriate moments, the protagonist may receive temporary divine assistance. Examples 
previously discussed include divine health, increased strength, battle intelligence, 
supernatural/magic power, protection, guidance and special divine items.
These are not guaranteed and should not function like ordinary purchasable spells.
13. Scripture & Battle Declarations
Scripture or Scripture-inspired declarations can be spoken during major combat moments, such as 
major combos, powerful abilities, dangerous encounters and boss fights. They should be used 
sparingly and meaningfully.
 Philippians 4:13
 Psalm 23
 Romans 8:31
 Psalm 91
 Ephesians 6 / Armor of God references
Scripture is not a mechanical spell that forces God to attack enemies. It represents faith, courage, 
trust and conviction.
14. The Psalm 23 Boss Battle
A major early-mid-game Destroyer boss encounter has a specific planned sequence. The 
protagonist begins reciting Psalm 23 while actively fighting the boss. The recitation continues 
dynamically as combat escalates.
The protagonist fights, dodges, takes damage, performs major combos and uses abilities while 
continuing the recitation. Near the end, the Destroyer is defeated and the protagonist completes 
the Psalm.
This should be a memorable cinematic gameplay moment rather than a normal combat voice line. 
When implementing actual biblical text, use an appropriate translation and account for its 
copyright status.

15. Covenant Battle Oath
Established original Covenant oath:
“By truth we stand.
By faith we endure.
By the Light we rise.
Darkness will not prevail.”
This is an original declaration and should not imitate the Green Lantern oath.
16. Signature Leadership Line
“The darkness has had its watch.
Now we take ours.”
This connects the protagonist's leadership journey to the title The Last Watch.
17. Teammate System
Teammates are important characters rather than disposable NPCs.
 Fight alongside the protagonist
 Give and receive advice
 Participate in missions
 Be rescued
 Develop trust and relationships
 Give weapons or equipment
 Provide information
 Develop their own Covenant careers
Teammates can progress through ranks and potentially become leaders themselves.
18. Tactical Advice System
Teammates may ask what to do during missions. The player can give tactical advice such as:
 Defensive: Hold this position.
 Aggressive: Push forward.
 Careful: Scout the area first.
 Rescue: Get the civilians out before engaging.
Advice should influence teammate AI and mission outcomes. Poor decisions can create 
complications rather than instant game-over states.

19. Teammate Rewards & Trust
Helping teammates can produce meaningful rewards such as weapons, armor components, 
equipment, upgrade materials, information, special missions and unique items.
Teammates remember player actions. Saving them, helping them, protecting their teams, giving 
good advice, completing missions with them, or failing them can affect trust.
Trusted teammates may provide better equipment, reveal information, request the protagonist, 
recommend the protagonist, join difficult missions or become leaders.
20. Evangelism & Community Gameplay
Evangelism is a real gameplay system. The player can encounter communities affected by fear, 
deception, enemy influence, loss or confusion.
 Listen
 Give advice
 Help people
 Evangelize
 Protect civilians
 Complete community tasks
Successful community missions can produce allies, resources, information, safer settlements and 
new missions. Helping people should have lasting mechanical meaning.
21. Pet / Animal Companion
The protagonist has an animal companion. It can assist with exploration, tracking, enemy 
detection, finding hidden objects, detecting unusual activity and selected combat situations.
The pet should be introduced naturally, and not all special abilities should be revealed 
immediately. It can become an emotionally important companion.

 Modern city
 Wilderness and forests
 Mountains and caves
 Villages and remote settlements
 Industrial areas
 Corrupted districts
 Mysterious ruins

25. Choices & Consequences
Meaningful choices include fighting or investigating, rescuing civilians or pursuing an enemy, 
personally leading an operation or sending another team, trusting or investigating someone, giving
aggressive or defensive advice, helping a Covenant member, evangelizing, and allocating 
resources.
Consequences can affect relationships, missions, communities, Covenant reputation, rewards and 
team development.
26. Covenant Reputation
Possible reputation progression: Trusted Respected Veteran Leader. → → →
Reputation can influence mission access, equipment, information, teammate trust, leadership 
opportunities and special operations. Rank and reputation remain separate.

27. Seven Deadly Sins Arc
A possible Seven Deadly Sins storyline has been discussed. It must remain separate from divine 
blessing mechanics. The sins should not be portrayed as God's powers. They can form a distinct 
supernatural/thematic enemy arc. Exact structure remains undecided.
28. Story Structure
Phase 1 — Awakening: Introduce the chosen protagonist, modern world, Covenant, hierarchy, 
basic combat, exploration, first supernatural incidents, Destroyers, prayer and early missions.
Phase 2 — The Paths Converge: The two protagonists' separate stories intersect. They meet and 
discover their missions are connected.
Phase 3 — Rising Responsibility: The chosen protagonist becomes a Team Leader, receives scout 
reports, gives advice, helps communities, evangelizes, fights stronger enemies and acquires Armor 
of God pieces.
Phase 4 — The Deepening War: Stronger Destroyers, Persuaders, Deceivers, commanders, larger 
enemy concentrations and deeper Veil mysteries appear.
Phase 5 — Command: The chosen protagonist becomes a Commander and begins making 
decisions affecting multiple teams and regions.
Phase 6 — General: The chosen protagonist becomes General and coordinates major operations 
while the unchosen protagonist remains a major leader and ally.
Phase 7 — The Veil Weakens: The supernatural crisis accelerates and the Covenant discovers 
deeper connections between the Veil, supernatural enemies, biblical themes and the approaching 
crisis.
Phase 8 — The Last Watch: The final major Covenant operation combines combat, leadership, 
prayer, Scripture, rescue, evangelism, investigation, teamwork, difficult choices and divine 
intervention.
29. Character Development Principle
The protagonists should not be perfect. They can be afraid, make mistakes, give bad advice, lose 
battles, question themselves, need teammates and need God's guidance.
Growth should happen in Combat + Character + Leadership + Faith.

31. Target Experience
The game should feel cinematic, mysterious, action-packed, adventurous, emotional, hopeful, 
spiritually meaningful, exploratory, dangerous and rewarding.
Balance intense combat with quiet prayer, team conversations, exploration, community, 
friendship, loss, victory, difficult choices and hope.

38. Master Creative Identity
Beyond the Veil: The Last Watch is a modern Christian semi-open-world action-adventure about a 
Covenant operative who grows into a leader while protecting humanity from supernatural forces, 
helping communities, resisting deception, discovering the mystery behind the Veil, progressively 
receiving the Armor of God, and eventually leading The Covenant during the Last Watch.
