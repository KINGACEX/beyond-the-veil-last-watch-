# 1. CANON PROTECTION SYSTEM

Every piece of BTV information must be treated according to its status.

## [CANON]

Information explicitly established by the project creator.

Do not casually change it.

## [PROPOSED]

A new idea that has been suggested but has not yet been confirmed as permanent canon.

Do not present it as established fact.

## [ALTERNATIVE]

A possible variation being considered.

## [UNKNOWN]

The project has not established the answer.

Do not invent an answer and claim it is canon.

## [CONFLICT]

An idea that contradicts established canon.

Flag it before incorporating it.

---

# 2. DEVELOPMENT RULE

When creating something new:

1. Check established BTV canon.
2. Identify systems or lore that the new feature interacts with.
3. Check for contradictions.
4. Explain important conflicts.
5. Create the feature while preserving established canon.
6. Mark new material as PROPOSED unless explicitly approved.
7. Never silently overwrite established information.

If information is missing:

DO NOT automatically fill the gap with invented canon.

Instead use:

> UNKNOWN — requires project decision.

or provide a clearly labeled proposal.

# 3. PROJECT IDENTITY

BTV is an original modern Christian action-adventure survival RPG.

It is inspired by the themes of:

- Christian faith
- spiritual warfare
- Scripture
- prayer
- redemption
- courage
- sacrifice
- truth
- hope
- service
- evangelism
- community
- temptation
- good versus evil
- perseverance
- approaching the unknown

However:

BTV is NOT a direct retelling of the Bible.

It is an original fictional world using Christian themes and concepts within an original modern setting.

The game's originality is essential.

The Bible provides thematic and spiritual foundations, while BTV creates its own:

- characters
- organizations
- locations
- conflicts
- enemies
- mythology
- gameplay
- narrative arcs
- world history
- systems

Do not turn BTV into a simple biblical reenactment.

---

# 4. CORE THEMATIC PRINCIPLE

BTV should communicate that:

- truth matters
- faith requires perseverance
- prayer is not a vending machine
- courage does not mean absence of fear
- people can fall and recover
- redemption is possible
- service matters
- power does not equal spiritual superiority
- leadership is responsibility
- Christians are called to serve others
- evil can deceive as well as attack
- hope can survive darkness

The game should avoid reducing Christianity to superficial aesthetic decoration.

Christian themes must influence the world's philosophy, characters, choices, missions, and consequences.

---

# 5. GOD, CHRIST, AND THE HOLY SPIRIT

## God

God is the ultimate authority.

God is not a normal playable combat character.

The Covenant, Chosen, and all human characters remain subordinate to God.

## Christ

Christ is above the Covenant and above the Chosen.

The Covenant does not replace Christ.

The Chosen does not become a divine figure.

Do not design mechanics that imply the protagonist becomes spiritually superior to Christ or replaces Christian authority.

## Holy Spirit

The Holy Spirit is associated with:

- guidance
- conviction
- warnings
- wisdom
- discernment
- direction

The Holy Spirit should NOT be treated as a conventional player-controlled attack resource.

Avoid:

> "Press button to activate Holy Spirit super attack."

Instead, manifestations of guidance should feel contextual, meaningful, and spiritually consistent.

---

# 6. PRAYER SYSTEM

Prayer exists as an important BTV activity.

Prayer is not guaranteed to produce the player's requested result.

Prayer is not a purchasable buff.

Prayer should communicate:

- dependence
- reflection
- trust
- discernment
- surrender
- hope
- perseverance

Possible outcomes should respect divine timing and will.

Do not create a system where:

> More prayer points = stronger God powers.

Faith must not become an ordinary RPG currency.

Prayer can occur:

- during situations
- after missions
- at meaningful locations
- during personal reflection
- before difficult decisions
- in moments of uncertainty
