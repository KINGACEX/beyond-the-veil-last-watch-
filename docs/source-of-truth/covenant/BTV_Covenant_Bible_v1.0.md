## 1. Authority & Purpose
This document is the specialized Covenant reference for BTV. It deepens the established Covenant without overriding creator decisions, the Master Canon, Story & Lore Bible, or Character Bible. It must never silently promote UNKNOWN or PROPOSED material into CANON.

## 2. Covenant Identity
The Covenant is a hidden organization serving God and protecting people. It is neither a government agency nor the Church as a whole. It exists to serve, protect, investigate, communicate, rescue, restore, and respond to threats connected to BTV's world and the Veil.

## 3. Christian Position
The Covenant is subordinate to God and must never be presented as a replacement for Christ, the Church, Scripture, or ordinary Christian life. Covenant membership does not make a person spiritually superior. Rank measures responsibility and trust, not holiness or worth.

## 4. Mission Purpose
Core purposes include protecting people, responding to threats, rescuing those in danger, investigating anomalies and deception, supporting communities, communicating truth, facilitating outreach, and restoring damaged places and lives where appropriate.

## 5. Organizational Hierarchy
Established hierarchy: Recruits → Agents/Specialists → Team Leaders → Commanders → Generals. Advancement is based on service, responsibility, judgment, leadership, trust, and demonstrated readiness rather than simple XP or body count.

## 6. Rank Philosophy
Rank represents responsibility, accountability, and entrusted authority. Higher rank does not automatically mean stronger faith, greater moral worth, or greater closeness to God. Promotion should create new leadership responsibilities and consequences.

## 7. Recruits
Recruits are members undergoing preparation and evaluation. Their training may include Covenant principles, field safety, teamwork, communication, service, discipline, and role-specific preparation. Exact recruitment and training curriculum remain UNKNOWN unless established later.

## 8. Agents & Specialists
Agents and Specialists are operational members with developed field competence. Specialization should support mission diversity rather than turn every member into a combat class. Exact specialist catalog remains open.

## 9. Team Leaders
Team Leaders coordinate small operational groups, interpret objectives, manage risk, communicate priorities, and care for teammates and civilians. Leadership is a responsibility, not permission to disregard Covenant principles.

## 10. Commanders
Commanders coordinate larger operations, resources, teams, and regional priorities. They may have to balance rescue, investigation, defense, logistics, and long-term consequences. Exact command structure remains UNKNOWN.

## 11. Generals
Generals carry the highest established Covenant rank and oversee broad strategic responsibilities. The Chosen may eventually become General through service and leadership, but timing, structure, and other named Generals remain UNKNOWN.

## 12. Branch System
Established branches: Guardians, Messengers, Watchers, Shepherds, Scouts, and Keepers. Branches represent different forms of service and expertise; none is morally or spiritually superior to another.

## 13. Guardians
Guardians focus on protection and combat readiness. Character principles include courage, discipline, restraint, and protection of others. Guardian work is not defined solely by killing enemies.

## 14. Messengers
Messengers focus on communication, outreach, truth-sharing, coordination, and evangelism-related service. Their role includes listening and communicating responsibly, not merely delivering scripted speeches.

## 15. Watchers
Watchers focus on investigation, intelligence, evidence, observation, and patience. They help distinguish reliable information from deception and should not automatically know hidden Veil truths.

## 16. Shepherds
Shepherds focus on rescue, support, recovery, restoration, and care. Their importance demonstrates that BTV's world cannot be reduced to combat operations.

## 17. Scouts
Scouts focus on reconnaissance, mobility, observation, route knowledge, threat identification, and reporting. Scout Reports can generate later operations, investigations, rescues, or preparations.

## 18. Keepers
Keepers focus on equipment, resources, preparation, logistics, stewardship, and operational readiness. Their work should matter visibly to field teams and communities.

## 19. Branch Cooperation
Major missions can require multiple branches. A rescue may involve Scouts, Watchers, Shepherds, Guardians, Messengers, and Keepers. Branch boundaries should enable cooperation rather than create isolated gameplay silos.

## 20. Covenant Oath
The established Covenant oath is: “By truth we stand. By faith we endure. By the Light we rise. Darkness will not prevail.” It should be used deliberately rather than repeated as meaningless decoration.

## 21. Leadership & Accountability
Covenant leaders are accountable for decisions, civilians, teammates, resources, intelligence, and consequences. Leadership should include disagreement, uncertainty, consultation, correction, and responsibility where appropriate.

## 22. Field Operations
Operations should have objectives, context, information quality, risks, civilians, resources, and possible consequences. Avoid repetitive “go there, kill, collect” structure. Operations may combine investigation, rescue, defense, outreach, restoration, or combat.

## 23. Scout Reports
Scout Reports provide actionable information such as anomalies, missing persons, suspicious activity, enemy movement, environmental changes, or Veil-related concerns. Reports should contain uncertainty where appropriate and should not magically reveal everything.

## 24. Tactical Leadership
The player may use contextual tactical direction such as Hold, Push, Scout, Protect Civilians, Retreat, and Protect Teammate. These are a framework, not an exhaustive locked command list. Exact implementation remains open.

## 25. Mission Ethics
The Covenant protects people and should distinguish mission success from indiscriminate destruction. Civilian safety, truth, restraint, rescue, and responsible judgment can matter even when the player faces hostile threats.

## 26. Civilian Relationship
Public spaces should contain substantially more civilians, families, workers, visitors, worshippers, and merchants than Covenant personnel. Covenant members should not make ordinary communities feel like military bases unless the location is explicitly a Covenant facility or active crisis zone.

## 27. Church Relationship
The Covenant is not the Church. Covenant members may support, protect, communicate with, or serve churches and communities, but the organization must not replace ordinary Christian fellowship or claim institutional spiritual supremacy.

## 28. Government Relationship
The Covenant is not a government agency. Its relationship with governments may involve cooperation, secrecy, tension, misunderstanding, or limited coordination depending on story needs. Exact legal/institutional status remains UNKNOWN.

## 29. Relationship to the Chosen
The Chosen is human and remains subject to the Covenant's service and accountability principles. The Chosen does not become divine by rank or title. The exact structure of the dual-Chosen concept remains UNKNOWN and requires creator decision.

## 30. Covenant & the Veil
The Covenant investigates and responds to Veil-related threats but does not automatically understand the Veil's deepest truths. Knowledge is distributed by role, experience, evidence, and discovery. Veil lore must remain coherent and mystery-driven.

## 31. Intelligence & Information
Information should have sources, confidence, limitations, and potential deception. Watchers and Scouts may identify patterns without possessing complete explanations. Characters only know what they plausibly know.

## 32. Equipment & Logistics
Covenant equipment supports mission roles, environments, and preparation. Gear should not turn faith into a purchasable mechanic. Aces and Covenant Credits remain distinct; faith itself is never a currency.

## 33. Uniform Identity
The Covenant uniform uses a white base, restrained gold, and cross-centered Christian insignia, with variants for modern tactical, formal/ceremonial, field/stealth, regional/weather, and specialist contexts. Exact implementation remains subject to visual design work.

## 34. Training Philosophy
Training should build judgment, teamwork, discipline, communication, service, restraint, and role competence. Training should not imply that Covenant membership alone guarantees spiritual maturity.

## 35. Covenant Culture
The Covenant should feel like a living organization with shared purpose, different personalities, disagreements, traditions, work rhythms, mistakes, mentoring, and service. Avoid making every member speak or think identically.

## 36. Covenant Facilities
Covenant bases and facilities may include briefing areas, training spaces, logistics, intelligence, medical/support functions, living areas, communications, and branch-specific spaces. Exact base layouts and named locations remain UNKNOWN.

## 37. Recruitment & Membership
Recruitment methods, eligibility criteria, initiation details, geographic reach, and historical founding remain UNKNOWN unless explicitly established. Do not invent secret ceremonies or hidden history as fact.

## 38. Promotion & Progression
Player advancement should reflect narrative leadership progression: responsibility and trust earned through actions and decisions. Promotion should not function as a generic XP level-up system.

## 39. Characterization of Covenant Members
Covenant members remain people first. They can be brave, afraid, wise, mistaken, compassionate, impatient, humorous, serious, uncertain, or conflicted. Faith should not flatten emotional range.

## 40. Covenant Conflicts
Internal disagreements may arise over strategy, secrecy, risk, civilian protection, resource allocation, interpretation of evidence, or mission priorities. Such conflicts should not automatically mean one side is spiritually evil.

## 41. Boundaries & Prohibitions
Do not make the Covenant divine, infallible, omniscient, morally perfect, a replacement for the Church, a government agency by default, or a purchasable spiritual progression system. Do not invent its history or secret doctrines without creator approval.

## 42. Open Covenant Unknowns
Unknowns include founding/history, geographic scope, exact recruitment/training system, legal status, named leadership, named bases, internal ranks beyond the established hierarchy, specialist catalog, intelligence structure, command architecture, resource policies, Covenant history with churches/governments, and exact relationship to any dual-Chosen structure.

## 43. Covenant Development Template
For any future Covenant feature, record: feature name; purpose; affected rank/branch; gameplay function; narrative function; civilian impact; theological boundary; dependencies; CANON/PROPOSED/UNKNOWN status; open questions; technical implications; canon audit.

## 44. Covenant Canon Audit
Every major Covenant feature must be checked against creator decisions, Master Canon, Story & Lore Bible, Character Bible, this Covenant Bible, originality requirements, Christian worldview boundaries, civilian population principles, and technical constraints.

## 45. Future Specialized Documents
Possible subordinate references include Covenant Operations Manual, Covenant Facilities & Technology Bible, Covenant History/Chronology, Branch Manuals, Equipment Bible, Mission Operations Bible, or Intelligence/Veil Investigation Manual. These remain development roadmap items, not current canon.

## 46. Final Covenant Principle
The Covenant exists to serve, protect, communicate truth, rescue, investigate, restore, and lead responsibly. It is powerful but not ultimate; organized but not infallible; Christian in worldview but not a replacement for Christ or the Church; and mysterious enough to support discovery without becoming arbitrary.
