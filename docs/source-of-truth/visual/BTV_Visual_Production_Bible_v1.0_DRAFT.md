## Visual Production Bible v1.0

**Document status:** DRAFT — production workflow proposal  
**Authority:** Supplementary to Foundation #12 — Visual / UI / HUD Bible v1.1 Consistency Lock  
**Canon effect:** NONE. This document does not create, revise, or approve content canon or visual canon.

## 1. Purpose and authority

This document turns BTV's existing visual direction into a repeatable production workflow for concept art, cinematic scenes, animation, trailers, YouTube Shorts, micro-episodes, and future game cinematics.

It is subordinate to:
1. The Creator's latest explicit decisions.
2. BTV Master Canon as reconciled by accepted creator decisions.
3. Specialized source-of-truth Bibles, including the Character Bible and Foundation #12 Visual/UI/HUD Bible v1.1.
4. The controlling Vertical Slice Specification for slice-specific details.
5. This production workflow and its proposals.
6. Older mockups, prompts, generated assets, and unverified reference images.

**Foundation #12 v1.1 remains the controlling visual authority.** Its core rule is: **IMPROVE, DON'T REINVENT.** This document must not become a competing art bible.

### Status vocabulary

- **CONFIRMED / CANON:** directly supported by controlling source material.
- **APPROVED DESIGN / LOCK:** approved direction, but not proof of implementation.
- **PROPOSED:** production recommendation awaiting approval.
- **UNKNOWN:** not established; do not invent.
- **CONFLICT:** conflicts with a higher-authority source.
- **REFERENCE CANDIDATE:** useful visual material whose approval/provenance is not established.
- **DOCUMENTED / IMPLEMENTED / VERIFIED / ACCEPTED:** separate production evidence states; never conflate them.

## 2. Creative identity

### Controlling visual direction — APPROVED DESIGN / LOCK

BTV's visual presentation is:
- grounded cinematic realism;
- premium third-person RPG presentation;
- contemporary tactical and civilian environments;
- practical equipment and believable materials;
- restrained Christian visual language;
- recognizable Asher and Ember continuity;
- cinematic lighting and environmental storytelling;
- contextual, readable HUD;
- Android-first technical discipline with a path to PC.

The goal is to make each scene feel like it belongs to the same original game. Do not imitate another studio's protected characters, distinctive assets, maps, dialogue, or signature compositions.

### Quality target — DIRECTION

Improve lighting, composition, materials, animation, environmental density, camera language, facial readability, and technical polish over time. Higher fidelity must not cause canon drift or make the Android target infeasible.

## 3. Canon and theology safeguards

Every visual and narrative production decision must preserve the controlling canon.

- God is the ultimate authority and is not a playable character or controllable game mechanic.
- Christ remains above the Covenant and Asher.
- Asher is human, the sole playable protagonist and the Chosen; he is aged 16–19 and presented as 18 in the controlling slice.
- The Holy Spirit is never a controllable combat ability, resource, scanner, or certainty engine.
- Prayer is meaningful and non-transactional.
- Faith, salvation, grace, divine favour, holiness, worship, and spiritual worth are never currencies, purchasable upgrades, loot, or morality scores.
- Communion is contextual and provides no buffs, XP, health restoration, or combat advantages.
- Logos Rhema Temple is the fictional in-game church. HCI is real-world inspiration/reference only; do not reproduce real HCI people, identities, branding, or institutional representation.
- The Veil remains governed by the operational laws in the controlling reconciliation document. Do not turn it into a generic fantasy portal or unrestricted magic system.
- Ember does not replace God, Christ, Scripture, prayer, or the Holy Spirit.

If a shot, line, visual effect, or sound cue risks violating these boundaries, stop and request review.

- Exact face, skin tone, hair, height, build, clothing details, and equipment remain UNKNOWN unless established by an approved reference.
- Existing Asher images in the Library are conflicting REFERENCE CANDIDATES; do not merge them or treat any one as the master model sheet without creator approval.
- Do not age him up, redesign him as a generic fantasy warrior, superhero, medieval knight, or futuristic sci-fi soldier.
- Exact biography and some visual details remain open unless established by an approved reference or creator decision.

Its printed “Early 20s” conflicts with age 18; its approximate height, build, accessories, and loadout are not canon. The separate visual-reference audit records this discrepancy.

### 4.2 Ember — CONFIRMED / LOCK

- Asher's intelligent animal companion and field partner.
- Rare original naturally occurring canid lineage; exact appearance and breed characteristics remain OPEN.
- Believable canid anatomy and natural animal behavior.
- No wings, demonic anatomy, cybernetic body, angelic transformation, or human speech.
- Ember may be perceptive but is not omniscient and does not replace investigation or spiritual authority.
- Preserve the official commands and abilities in the current character/canon sources; do not copy older mockup controls as authority.

Do not use an Afterfall-branded Ember profile as BTV reference without explicit provenance confirmation.

### 4.4 Character reference package — PROPOSED WORKFLOW

For each approved character:
1. Canon fact sheet (locked facts, slice-only facts, unknowns, conflicts).
2. Approved reference-image manifest and provenance.
3. Neutral front / side / back turnaround, if an authoritative reference is approved.
4. Face and expression sheet.
5. Silhouette and scale reference.
6. Outfit variants justified by role, scene, weather, or story.
7. Movement and gesture reference.
8. Voice and dialogue notes, only where established.
9. Continuity QA checklist.

A generated image is not automatically a canon reference. Never overwrite an original reference; store derivatives as new versions.

## 5. Environment production

BTV locations should feel contemporary, inhabited, and specific. Build visual packages only from established locations and scene requirements.

### Required environment reference set — PROPOSED

- wide establishing view;
- medium view for scene blocking;
- close detail/material references;
- day/night or weather variants where story-relevant;
- human scale references;
- entry/exit and camera blocking;
- ambient activity and population notes;
- lighting and colour continuity;
- accessibility and readability checks.

### Environment guardrails

- **Logos Rhema Temple:** fictional in-game church and living community; not a copy of HCI or a Covenant base.
- **Covenant facilities:** functional operational spaces with the established Christian identity; not generic copied military bases or fantasy castles.
- **Ridgeview:** use the controlling slice specification; do not reveal restricted enemy labels or introduce a Ridgeview–Concordance connection.
- **Public/community spaces:** normally show more civilians, families, workers, visitors, worshippers, and merchants than Covenant agents.
- **Wilderness and Veil locations:** follow the current world and Veil rules; do not invent ultimate metaphysics.

## 6. Covenant, clothing, props, and equipment

### Covenant visual identity — LOCK / DIRECTION

- white base;
- restrained gold;
- cross-centered Christian insignia;
- practical tactical equipment;
- professional field organization presentation.

Branch, rank, duty, climate, and mission may justify functional variations. They must not imply spiritual superiority.

### Props and equipment — PROPOSED WORKFLOW

For every recurring prop, record:
- Asset ID and name;
- source/canon dependency;
- owner/user;
- physical description and scale;
- materials and wear;
- allowed variants;
- scene/function;
- provenance and rights;
- approval status.

Do not invent a sacred artefact, Covenant technology, weapon, or supernatural object and silently make it canon.

## 7. Visual effects and the Veil

Effects should communicate observed phenomena, not replace the story's evidence and uncertainty.

Production may use restrained environmental anomalies, spatial inconsistencies, altered perception, sound perspective, light behaviour, and environmental disturbances where supported by the scene.

Every effect requires:
- a source-of-truth reason;
- an intensity and duration limit;
- a readable player-facing interpretation;
- an accessibility-safe version;
- a note distinguishing observed evidence from character inference.

No effect may imply that prayer, Scripture, faith, or the Holy Spirit is a controllable magic ability.

## 8. Cinematography

### Camera language — PROPOSED

- Establish geography before rapid cutting.
- Use wide shots for location and scale.
- Use medium shots for character interaction and blocking.
- Use close-ups for meaningful expressions, evidence, and story details.
- Keep character eyelines and screen direction consistent within a sequence.
- Use camera movement to communicate attention, discovery, tension, or transition—not as decoration.
- Preserve spatial continuity when moving between generated clips.
- Avoid excessive camera shake, rapid cuts, and artificial lens effects that reduce clarity on mobile screens.

### Shot record

Each shot should specify:
- sequence and shot ID;
- story purpose;
- location/time/weather;
- characters and approved references;
- framing and lens intention;
- camera movement;
- lighting and colour continuity;
- action and performance;
- dialogue/voiceover;
- sound and ambience;
- transition;
- generation/editing tool and settings;
- known continuity risks.

## 9. Animation and performance

### Performance priorities — PROPOSED

1. Clear intention and readable body language.
2. Stable character identity and anatomy.
3. Natural weight, timing, and foot contact.
4. Consistent scale and contact with the environment.
5. Facial expression appropriate to the scene.
6. Controlled secondary motion.
7. No unexplained changes to clothing, props, markings, or body proportions between shots.

For Ember, preserve natural canid movement and animal behaviour. Do not humanize his speech or anatomy.

Generated motion must be reviewed frame-by-frame or shot-by-shot for identity drift, object deformation, temporal discontinuity, and accidental visual artefacts. A visually impressive single frame does not prove animation quality.

## 10. Video formats and editorial standards

These are initial channel-production proposals, not canon or fixed publishing commitments.

| Format | Proposed duration | Primary purpose |
|---|---:|---|
| Short | 20–45 seconds | One compelling visual/story beat |
| Micro-episode | 1–3 minutes | One focused narrative event |
| Extended episode | 3–8 minutes | Character/world/story development |
| Trailer | 30–90 seconds | Project identity and release communication |

Each video must have an original purpose, authored script or shot plan, meaningful variation, and a clear relationship to BTV. Avoid mass-produced template repetition.

A short should not be a random sequence of unrelated AI clips. It should have a hook, a coherent development, and a deliberate ending.

## 11. Audio identity

Audio is part of continuity.

Track:
- original score and sound-effect provenance;
- voice casting or synthetic voice provider and usage terms;
- pronunciation guide for established names;
- dialogue and ambience levels;
- music rights and attribution;
- subtitles/captions;
- accessibility and intelligibility.

Do not imitate a real actor's or public figure's voice without authorization. Do not use copyrighted music or sound effects without appropriate rights. Preserve silence and natural ambience where they serve the scene.

## 12. Branding and channel identity

Logo, typography, title cards, thumbnails, and end cards must be original and consistent. Their exact design remains OPEN unless an approved asset exists.

Do not publish mockups as official branding. Record ownership, source files, font licences, image rights, and version history.

## 13. Production pipeline

1. **Brief:** define audience, platform, format, purpose, duration, and intended release state.
2. **Canon check:** identify source documents and locked/unknown elements.
3. **Reference retrieval:** use only verified approved references; mark candidates clearly.
4. **Script and beat sheet:** write original story content and verify Scripture/context where relevant.
5. **Storyboard:** define sequence, shots, staging, and transitions.
6. **Animatic:** test timing and clarity before expensive generation.
7. **Asset preparation:** prepare characters, environments, props, and audio references.
8. **Generation/animation:** use the smallest necessary number of generations; log tools, models, settings, and credits.
9. **Editorial:** assemble, sound design, subtitle, and colour-match.
10. **QA:** run canon, identity, continuity, technical, rights, and platform checks.
11. **Review:** mark REVIEW until a human approval decision is recorded.
12. **Release:** publish only after explicit approval and rights verification.

## 14. Tool-neutral production architecture

Tools are replaceable; source assets and production records must remain portable.

Possible tool roles:
- image generation: concept exploration and approved still assets;
- image editing: correction of a specific approved reference;
- video generation: shot/clip generation from an approved storyboard;
- character/reference systems: identity consistency;
- editing: timeline, audio, captions, colour, and export;
- channel analytics: packaging and performance review.

Do not lock BTV to one vendor or assume free credits, trial balances, feature limits, or commercial rights will remain unchanged. Verify current terms before use.

### Tool record

For each generation, record:
- provider and product;
- model/version if exposed;
- date;
- prompt/reference IDs;
- aspect ratio and output settings;
- credits consumed;
- terms/licence review status;
- output file path;
- review result;
- whether the output is approved for reuse.

## 15. File and version control

Use the repository's source-of-truth and production documentation structure. Keep original source assets unchanged.

Suggested asset filename:
`BTV_[ASSET-ID]_[SHORT-NAME]_vNN_[STATUS].[ext]`

Suggested folder categories:
- characters;
- environments;
- props-equipment;
- visual-effects;
- storyboards;
- animation;
- audio;
- exports;
- approved-releases;
- provenance.

Do not place unapproved reference candidates in runtime asset directories. Do not commit credentials, private account details, or restricted third-party assets.

## 16. Quality gates

A visual production item passes only when evidence supports all applicable gates:

1. **Canon:** no conflict with controlling source.
2. **Identity:** character/location continuity is preserved.
3. **Performance:** animation and acting are coherent.
4. **Cinematography:** shot purpose, geography, and readability are clear.
5. **Technical:** export, resolution, aspect ratio, audio, and platform requirements pass.
6. **Originality/rights:** original or appropriately licensed.
7. **Accessibility:** captions and readable presentation are provided where required.
8. **Approval:** responsible human reviewer accepts the exact deliverable.

Record PASS / FAIL / NOT VERIFIED per gate. Never infer acceptance from generation completion.

## 17. Credit and spending governance

- Free-first and local-first where practical.
- Do not activate trials, subscriptions, paid credits, or paid plans without explicit approval.
- Do not spend credits on speculative character redesigns.
- Reuse approved references and plan shots before generation.
- Prototype the workflow with low-cost/free methods where possible.
- Record actual credit consumption; do not claim a tool is free or unlimited without current evidence.
- Do not publish, schedule, or distribute content without explicit approval.

## 18. Pilot production gate

The first BTV channel pilot should not enter final generation until:
- the featured cast is confirmed from the current canon;
- exact approved reference images are identified;
- the story beat and script are reviewed;
- the location is grounded in an established setting;
- the storyboard and animatic are coherent;
- tool rights and costs are checked;
- the pilot has a defined QA and approval owner.

**Current state:** DOCUMENTED workflow only. No character sheet, final environment concept, storyboard, animatic, animation, or video is approved by this document.

## 19. Change control

Any proposed change to a locked visual identity must be labelled:

**PROPOSED VISUAL CHANGE — REQUIRES CREATOR APPROVAL**

When sources conflict:
- cite the exact sources;
- identify the higher-authority source;
- preserve the lower-authority source as history;
- record the correction;
- do not blend incompatible details.

## 20. Final production principle

**BTV must look better over time, but it must always still look like BTV.**

**IMPROVE, DON'T REINVENT.**
