# BTV Foundation #12 — Visual / UI / HUD Bible v1.0

**Project:** Beyond the Veil: The Last Watch (BTV)  
**Foundation:** #12  
**Status:** Canonical design foundation  
**Purpose:** Define BTV's visual identity, UI/HUD philosophy, presentation rules, accessibility, and platform-scaling principles.

---

## 1. Overall Visual Identity

BTV uses grounded contemporary realism with cinematic environmental storytelling, restrained Christian visual language, believable human communities, mysterious supernatural phenomena, and an immersive interface.

The world should feel like a real place first and a game world second.

### Core visual principle

> **The world comes first; the interface serves the world.**

---

## 2. Quality References

BTV may study the quality and design principles of:

- Red Dead Redemption 2
- The Witcher 3
- Elden Ring
- Cyberpunk 2077
- God of War Ragnarök
- Baldur's Gate 3
- Ghost of Tsushima
- The Legend of Zelda: Breath of the Wild
- Black Myth: Wukong
- Blood of Dawnwalker

These are **quality references only**.

BTV must not copy their characters, assets, interfaces, environments, story structures, terminology, or distinctive protected visual identities.

---

## 3. Visual Hierarchy

The visual hierarchy is:

1. Characters and human action
2. Environment and sense of place
3. Gameplay-critical information
4. Atmosphere and Christian/symbolic language
5. UI decoration

UI must never overpower the world unnecessarily.

---

## 4. Color and Visual Language

The world uses believable regional palettes appropriate to climate, architecture, time of day, and atmosphere.

### Covenant

- White base
- Restrained gold
- Cross-centered Christian insignia
- Functional tactical materials
- Regional and environmental adaptation

### Veil

The Veil uses distinctive but restrained visual language.

Avoid turning the Veil into generic glowing fantasy energy.

Its visual language may include:

- subtle inconsistencies
- altered perception
- spatial unease
- environmental anomalies
- impossible patterns
- escalating disruption

Color must never be the only way information is communicated.

---

## 5. Character Continuity

Character appearance must remain consistent across:

- gameplay
- cinematics
- dialogue
- promotional material
- UI portraits
- equipment screens
- environmental scenes

### Asher Vance

Asher is the established protagonist and Chosen.

Age: **16–19**.

His appearance should communicate:

- youth
- field experience
- responsibility
- humanity
- Covenant training
- believable physical development

Avoid making Asher look unrealistically mature or like a generic adult action hero.

---

## 6. Covenant Uniforms

The Covenant uniform uses:

- white as the base
- restrained gold
- cross-centered Christian insignia

### Major variants

- Modern Tactical
- Formal / Ceremonial
- Field / Stealth
- Regional / Weather
- Specialist

Uniform variation may communicate:

- branch
- rank
- duty
- environment
- weather
- operational requirements

### Prohibited direction

Avoid:

- luxury fashion
- glowing armor for no reason
- random fashion loot
- excessive ornamentation
- designs implying rank equals spiritual superiority

Rank represents responsibility and trust.

---

## 7. Armor of God Visual Direction

The six pieces are:

1. Belt of Truth
2. Breastplate of Righteousness
3. Shoes of the Gospel of Peace
4. Shield of Faith
5. Helmet of Salvation
6. Sword of the Spirit

Visual direction:

**Medieval-inspired, but modern and tactical.**

The Armor should feel integrated into BTV's contemporary world rather than looking like disconnected fantasy equipment.

Progressive visual development is established as a design direction.

Exact acquisition order, upgrades, statistics, milestones, and detailed implementation remain separate design decisions.

---

## 8. Church / HCI Visuals

Churches are living community spaces, not menu screens.

Visual presentation should include believable:

- worshippers
- families
- workers
- children where appropriate
- musicians
- ministers
- visitors
- community activity

Chapels may vary by region, architecture, climate, and local culture.

Do not invent unauthorized real-world HCI identities, leadership, doctrine, endorsement, or partnerships.

---

## 9. Church Service Presentation

Church service should feel like an actual world activity.

### KITFIT

The pre-preaching segment may include:

- Scripture
- a short message
- teaching concerning giving
- voluntary offerings
- charity/community support

The system must not imply:

> More money = greater righteousness.

Giving is voluntary and generosity is not reduced to an amount.

---

## 10. Communion Presentation

Communion is contextual and reverent.

### Communion Set

Bread and wine are represented as one contextual item.

It may only be used inside the appropriate **CommunionContextZone**.

Communion must not provide:

- buffs
- XP
- health restoration
- stat increases
- equipment upgrades
- currency
- combat advantages

The presentation should emphasize reflection and context rather than gamification.

---

## 11. World Visual Language

Major environments should communicate their identity through:

- architecture
- infrastructure
- climate
- vegetation
- signage
- traffic
- commerce
- population density
- local activity
- lighting
- environmental storytelling

World categories include:

- cities
- settlements
- wilderness
- facilities
- districts
- hidden locations
- chapels
- Covenant locations
- Veil-influenced areas

---

## 12. NPC Population Rules

Public/community spaces should contain substantially more:

- civilians
- families
- workers
- visitors
- worshippers
- merchants
- ordinary residents

than Covenant personnel.

Covenant bases may naturally contain more Covenant personnel.

A peaceful settlement should not visually behave like a permanent war zone.

Population density and demographics should vary naturally according to location, time, occupation, and crisis level.

---

## 13. Veil Visual Language

The Veil is not a generic:

- fantasy portal
- magical dimension
- glowing fog world
- interchangeable supernatural realm

Instead, the player should notice increasingly strange inconsistencies.

### Escalation

1. Anomaly
2. Suspicion
3. Recognition
4. Instability
5. Major event

The player should often question what is happening before the game fully explains it.

---

## 14. Enemy Visual Language

### Destroyers

Communicate direct physical aggression and immediate threat.

### Persuaders

Communicate influence, manipulation, temptation, and social pressure.

### Deceivers

Communicate false perception, misinformation, ambiguity, and ambush.

### Commanders / Bosses

Must have narrative significance and coordinate threats.

Human antagonists remain visually human rather than automatically becoming fantasy monsters.

---

## 15. Ember Visual Continuity

Ember is a believable rare wild-dog/canid lineage.

His unusual nature should primarily be communicated through:

- behavior
- attention
- movement
- expression
- tracking
- Veil sensitivity

Avoid unnecessary fantasy anatomy.

Ember should remain recognizably animal-like.

---

## 16. Cinematic Language

Cinematics should prioritize:

- character performance
- environmental context
- restrained camera movement
- meaningful framing
- believable lighting
- atmosphere
- facial/body-language continuity

Avoid turning every Christian moment into a spectacle.

Scripture should be presented deliberately and appropriately.

---

# UI / HUD

## 17. HUD Philosophy

The BTV HUD is:

- minimal
- contextual
- consistent
- responsive
- accessible
- non-intrusive
- immersive

Avoid:

- permanent screen clutter
- giant objective arrows
- excessive floating numbers
- excessive outlines
- unnecessary reward spam

---

## 18. HUD States

The interface supports distinct contextual states:

- Exploration
- Combat
- Investigation
- Dialogue
- Tactical Leadership
- Church / Service
- Communion
- Veil Event
- Inventory / Equipment
- Map
- Pause / Settings

Each state should show only information relevant to the player's current activity.

---

## 19. Exploration HUD

The exploration HUD may provide:

- subtle health/status information
- compact objective/context cues
- interaction prompts
- optional accessibility indicators
- restrained navigation information

It should not dominate exploration.

---

## 20. Combat HUD

Combat may show:

- player health/status
- stamina/resource systems if later established
- contextual abilities/commands
- teammate status
- immediate threats

Combat information should be readable without overwhelming the screen.

---

## 21. Investigation HUD

Investigation UI should support:

- clues
- evidence
- uncertain leads
- contextual interactions
- relevant observations

It must not automatically solve mysteries for the player.

---

## 22. Dialogue HUD

Dialogue presentation should emphasize:

- faces
- body language
- environment
- emotional context
- meaningful choices

Avoid making every conversation look like a conventional dialogue-wheel spectacle.

---

## 23. Tactical Leadership HUD

The Tactical Leadership HUD may show:

- context-valid commands
- teammate states
- civilian protection
- mission objectives
- risks
- relevant tactical information

It should feel like leadership rather than an RTS control panel.

---

## 24. Church / Service HUD

During church-related activities, combat and mission overlays should be minimized.

The interface should communicate that the player has entered a different kind of world activity.

---

## 25. Communion HUD

Communion UI should be extremely restrained.

Avoid:

- XP notifications
- reward popups
- stat increases
- achievement-style spiritual rewards
- gamified score presentation

---

## 26. Veil HUD

During Veil events, the UI may subtly reflect uncertainty through:

- controlled distortion
- incomplete information
- restrained interface instability
- altered perception cues

However, accessibility and basic usability must never be sacrificed.

---

## 27. Notifications

Notifications should be prioritized.

High priority:

- immediate danger
- critical mission information
- important contextual changes

Lower priority:

- optional discoveries
- routine inventory updates
- minor rewards

Avoid notification spam.

---

## 28. Maps and Navigation

Maps should reveal information progressively.

They should support:

- known regions
- discovered locations
- player knowledge
- Scout Reports
- uncertainty

The map should not automatically reveal everything in the world.

Scout Reports may contain incomplete or uncertain information.

---

## 29. Inventory and Equipment

Inventory should feel practical and field-oriented.

It should not resemble an arbitrary magical loot grid.

Faith is **not** an inventory resource.

Spiritual concepts must not be converted into collectible currencies.

---

## 30. Currency Presentation

### Aces

General-world currency.

### Covenant Credits

Covenant-focused currency.

They remain distinct.

Faith cannot be purchased.

---

## 31. Accessibility

BTV should support:

- scalable text
- high-contrast options
- non-color information indicators
- adjustable UI size
- readable icons
- subtitle controls
- reduced motion
- adjustable subtitle presentation
- appropriate audio/visual accessibility options

Accessibility should be designed from the beginning rather than added at the end.

---

## 32. Android and PC Scalability

The visual/UI architecture must support:

### Android

- touch controls
- smaller displays
- scalable UI
- performance-conscious rendering
- configurable graphics quality

### PC

- mouse
- keyboard
- controller
- high-resolution displays
- scalable graphics
- configurable UI

Quality scaling must not change:

- canon
- theology
- character identity
- story meaning
- core gameplay rules

---

## 33. Performance Principles

Visual quality must be balanced against:

- memory
- loading
- rendering cost
- animation complexity
- NPC density
- world streaming
- device capability

Android must receive serious performance consideration rather than being treated as an afterthought.

---

## 34. Animation

Animation should prioritize:

- believable movement
- physical weight
- character personality
- environmental interaction
- contextual reactions
- readable combat
- natural animal behavior for Ember

Animation should reinforce characterization rather than simply display mechanics.

---

## 35. Camera

Camera behavior should support:

- exploration
- combat
- investigation
- dialogue
- cinematic moments
- tactical leadership

Avoid excessive camera effects that reduce readability or accessibility.

---

## 36. Audio-Visual Integration

Visual events should work with sound and music.

Important combinations may include:

- environmental silence
- distant activity
- subtle Veil audio/visual anomalies
- church ambience
- Covenant communication
- combat sound
- animal reactions
- environmental changes

---

## 37. Technical UI Principles

UI should be:

- data-driven
- modular
- reusable
- testable
- resolution-aware
- state-aware
- accessibility-aware

Each major UI widget should have defined:

- purpose
- state
- inputs
- visibility rules
- accessibility behavior
- fallback behavior

Avoid monolithic UI systems.

---

## 38. Visual Canon Audit

Before approving major visual/UI work, verify:

### BTV Identity
Does it look like BTV?

### Canon Continuity
Does it preserve established characters, Covenant identity, Ember, Church/HCI, world rules, and Veil logic?

### Christian Integrity
Does it treat Christian elements respectfully and avoid turning faith into a commodity?

### World Consistency
Does the visual design fit the location and context?

### Readability
Can the player understand what matters?

### Accessibility
Can different players use the system effectively?

### Technical Scalability
Can the design work across Android and PC?

### Originality
Does it remain distinct from reference games?

---

## 39. Sacred Context Anti-Gamification Rule

Sacred activities must not be reduced to conventional reward loops.

Do not create:

- Faith Coins
- Prayer Points
- Grace Currency
- Salvation Consumables
- Spiritual Loot
- Purchasable Faith
- Worship Buffs
- Communion Buffs
- Spiritual Stat Farming

Christian practices should remain meaningful within the world rather than being treated as monetizable or grindable resources.

## 41. Final Visual Principle

BTV's interface should never make the player forget that they are inside a living world.

The goal is not to display more information.

The goal is to display the **right information at the right time**, while preserving immersion, readability, accessibility, Christian integrity, and BTV's original identity.
