# BTV Foundation #12 — Visual / UI / HUD Bible v1.1
## Consistency Lock & Visual Reference Update

**Project:** Beyond the Veil: The Last Watch (BTV)  
**Foundation:** #12  
**Version:** 1.1  
**Status:** Canonical design foundation + consistency lock  
**Relationship to v1.0:** v1.1 preserves the v1.0 visual/UI/HUD direction and adds an explicit visual consistency rule based on the approved Asher gameplay references.

---

# 1. Purpose

This document defines the visual identity, UI/HUD philosophy, presentation rules, accessibility requirements, platform-scaling principles, and—most importantly—the **visual consistency standard** for BTV.

The approved gameplay references demonstrate the intended presentation:

- grounded cinematic realism
- premium third-person RPG presentation
- contemporary tactical environments
- restrained Christian visual language
- contextual HUD
- believable Covenant facilities and personnel
- consistent Asher presentation
- consistent Ember presentation
- cinematic lighting and environmental storytelling
- clear but immersive gameplay information

These references are a **visual baseline**, not a license to redesign canon.

---

# 2. Core Rule — IMPROVE, DON'T REINVENT

> **BTV visual development must improve established designs without casually changing them.**

Future BTV images, mockups, gameplay concepts, cinematics, UI screens, promotional visuals, and environment concepts should preserve established visual continuity.

Improvements are encouraged when they make the presentation:

- more realistic
- more cinematic
- more readable
- more polished
- more immersive
- more technically believable
- more accessible
- more consistent with BTV's established identity

However, an improvement must **not silently become a canon change**.

If a proposed visual improvement changes an established canon element, it must be explicitly identified as:

**PROPOSED CHANGE — REQUIRES CREATOR APPROVAL**

# 3. Approved Visual Baseline

The supplied Asher gameplay references establish the current visual presentation baseline.

The baseline includes:

### Character Presentation
- Asher Vance as the established protagonist
- young field-operative presentation
- believable proportions and anatomy
- grounded facial features
- practical tactical equipment
- consistent hairstyle, facial structure, age presentation, and overall identity

### Covenant Presentation
- recognizable Covenant insignia
- white-based tactical/field uniform direction
- restrained gold detailing
- practical black/dark tactical equipment
- Christian cross-centered visual identity
- professional organization presentation
- functional facilities rather than fantasy castles or generic military bases

### Ember
- believable canine/canid appearance
- recognizable recurring appearance
- practical field companion presentation
- natural animal behavior
- companion UI integrated into the main HUD

### Environment
- realistic contemporary infrastructure
- cinematic lighting
- strong environmental storytelling
- believable weather
- realistic materials
- populated spaces
- practical industrial, urban, wilderness, and facility architecture

### HUD
- premium contextual RPG interface
- restrained blue/white/gold visual language
- Asher status panel
- mission/objective panel
- compass/navigation
- waypoint
- minimap when appropriate
- currencies
- Ember companion status
- contextual interaction prompts
- weapon/ammunition display
- contextual ability/command controls
- dialogue/subtitle presentation

The exact placement may evolve for different gameplay states, but the overall visual language must remain recognizable as BTV.

# 5. Asher Vance — Visual Continuity Lock

Asher must remain visually recognizable across scenes.

Do not casually change:

- apparent age
- facial identity
- hairstyle
- core facial proportions
- body proportions
- ethnicity/appearance
- established Covenant identity
- equipment philosophy

Clothing and equipment may change when justified by:

- mission
- weather
- region
- rank
- specialist role
- tactical requirement
- Armor of God progression
- story progression

Such changes must still clearly read as **Asher**.

### Important

Do not redesign Asher as:

- an older adult action hero
- a generic fantasy warrior
- a superhero
- a heavily armored medieval knight
- a futuristic sci-fi soldier

unless a later creator-approved canon decision explicitly establishes such a direction.

---

# 6. Covenant Visual Continuity Lock

The Covenant must remain recognizable across locations.

Core identity:

- white base
- restrained gold
- cross-centered Christian insignia
- practical tactical equipment
- professional field organization

Different branches, ranks, duties, climates, and operational contexts may produce legitimate variations.

Examples:

- Guardians
- Messengers
- Watchers
- Shepherds
- Scouts
- Keepers

Variations should communicate **function**, not random fashion.

### Do not casually introduce

- unrelated military insignia
- fantasy armor
- excessive glowing equipment
- luxury fashion
- arbitrary neon tactical designs
- symbols that contradict the Covenant's Christian identity

---

# 7. Covenant Facilities

Covenant facilities should feel like functioning operational environments.

They may contain:

- personnel
- security
- research
- logistics
- communications
- training
- vehicles
- equipment
- work areas
- civilian interaction where appropriate
- practical infrastructure

They should not become generic military bases copied from other games.

The Covenant is a unique organization with its own Christian mission and organizational culture.

---

# 8. Ember Visual Continuity Lock

Ember must remain visually consistent with Foundation #11.

Established direction:

- rare natural canid lineage
- believable animal anatomy
- no fantasy wings
- no demonic anatomy
- no artificial cybernetic body
- no angelic transformation
- no human speech

His unusual nature should be communicated primarily through:

- behavior
- perception
- attention
- movement
- tracking
- Veil sensitivity
- bond with Asher

His appearance should remain recognizable across gameplay and cinematics.

# 9. HUD Consistency Lock

The BTV HUD must remain part of the same visual system.

Core language:

- restrained blue
- white information
- controlled gold emphasis
- dark/translucent panels
- clean typography
- contextual presentation
- cinematic integration

The HUD should communicate information without overwhelming the world.

### Core recurring elements

Where appropriate:

- Asher status
- objective
- compass
- waypoint
- minimap
- currency
- Ember status
- contextual interaction
- weapon/ammunition
- abilities
- commands
- dialogue

Not every element should appear at once.

---

# 10. Contextual HUD Principle

The HUD changes according to what Asher is doing.

### Exploration

Minimal information.

### Investigation

Clues, evidence, uncertainty, and contextual interaction.

### Combat

Immediate threats, health, relevant abilities, teammates, and weapon information.

### Stealth

Detection/awareness information and contextual cover/interactions.

### Tactical Leadership

Teammate states, civilian protection, objectives, risks, and valid commands.

### Church / Service

Combat UI largely disappears or becomes extremely restrained.

### Communion

Minimal contextual interface.

### Veil Event

Subtle uncertainty/distortion without destroying accessibility.

The interface may change state, but it must still look like **the same BTV interface system**.

# 11. Christian Visual Identity

Christian elements must be recognizable without becoming visual clutter.

Use:

- cross-centered Covenant insignia
- restrained symbolism
- appropriate Scripture presentation
- believable church architecture
- community worship
- contextual Christian environments

Avoid turning every scene into a collection of glowing religious symbols.

Christianity should shape the world's identity naturally.

# 14. Veil Visual Consistency

The Veil must not suddenly become a generic fantasy portal or magical realm.

Maintain:

- subtle anomalies
- altered perception
- spatial inconsistency
- environmental disturbances
- impossible patterns
- escalating instability

### Escalation

1. Anomaly
2. Suspicion
3. Recognition
4. Instability
5. Major Event

A Veil scene may become visually spectacular, but it should still feel like **BTV's Veil**.

# 15. World and NPC Consistency

Public spaces should normally contain substantially more:

- civilians
- families
- workers
- visitors
- worshippers
- merchants
- ordinary residents

than Covenant agents.

Covenant bases may naturally contain more Covenant personnel.

Population density should match:

- location
- time
- occupation
- crisis level
- weather
- local culture

A peaceful settlement should not suddenly look like a military battlefield without narrative justification.

# 18. Improvement vs Change

## Allowed Improvement

Examples:

- sharper facial rendering
- better lighting
- more realistic fabric
- improved tactical equipment detail
- better rain/reflections
- improved NPC density
- better environmental detail
- clearer HUD typography
- better composition
- stronger cinematic framing
- improved animation posing
- better Android/PC readability

These preserve the underlying design.

## Canon Change

Examples:

- changing Asher's established identity
- changing Ember into another species
- replacing Covenant insignia
- changing the Covenant's core uniform identity
- changing currencies
- changing established HUD philosophy
- turning the Veil into a conventional fantasy dimension
- changing Christian mechanics or sacred-context rules

These require explicit creator approval.

# 19. Reference Image Rule

Approved reference images should be treated as **continuity references**, not isolated one-off artwork.

When generating a new BTV visual, compare against the established references for:

- Asher
- Ember
- Covenant uniforms
- insignia
- HUD
- equipment
- lighting
- environmental realism
- UI language
- overall game identity

The objective is not to reproduce the exact image.

The objective is to produce another scene that convincingly belongs to the same game.

---

# 20. Image Generation Instruction

When generating BTV visuals, use the following internal rule:

> **Create a new BTV scene using the established visual continuity baseline. Improve realism, cinematic quality, lighting, materials, composition, readability, and technical polish while preserving established character identity, Covenant identity, Ember identity, HUD language, currencies, Christian visual language, Veil rules, and worldbuilding. Do not introduce unapproved canon changes.**

If a visual change is necessary for a new design proposal, clearly label it:

**PROPOSED VISUAL CHANGE — REQUIRES CANON REVIEW**

---

# 21. Quality Without Canon Drift

BTV should progressively look better over time.

The visual quality target can rise from:

**Good → Excellent → AAA-level → Highly polished**

without changing the identity of the game.

Quality improvement is expected.

Canon drift is not.

---

# 22. Visual Canon Audit

Before approving a major visual:

### Character
- Is Asher still recognizably Asher?
- Is Ember still recognizably Ember?

### Covenant
- Is the Covenant identity preserved?
- Is the insignia consistent?
- Is the uniform direction consistent?

### World
- Does the environment fit its location?
- Are NPC populations believable?

### Christian Integrity
- Are Christian elements respectful and contextual?
- Is faith avoided as a game commodity?

### Veil
- Does the supernatural presentation remain consistent with BTV's Veil?

### HUD
- Does the interface belong to the established BTV UI family?
- Is information contextual rather than cluttered?

### Technical
- Can the presentation scale appropriately for Android and PC?

### Originality
- Does it remain BTV rather than becoming a copy of a reference game?
