# BEYOND THE VEIL: THE LAST WATCH
## WORLD & NPC BIBLE v1.0

**Document Type:** Approved Specialized BTV Reference (pending integration audit)

**Authority:** Subordinate to Creator decisions/corrections and Master Canon; domain-specialized alongside Story & Lore, Character, Covenant, Faith/Prayer/Armor, Church/HCI/Service/Communion, and Veil/Enemy references.

**Status:** v1.0 — Initial world and NPC reference. Named locations, map structure, regional histories, government structures, and detailed simulation systems remain open.

### Non-Invention Rule
Do not invent named places, maps, regions, governments, named civilians, demographic facts, institutional histories, or technical simulation systems as established canon. Clearly label all proposals.

## 1. Authority & Purpose
Specialized reference for BTV's world structure, settlements, districts, facilities, wilderness, public spaces, population composition, NPC routines, regional variation, civilian life, commerce, travel, environmental storytelling, and world-state presentation.

## 2. Authority & Status
Creator decisions and corrections override all references. Master Canon remains primary content authority. Story & Lore governs narrative interpretation; Character governs individual character knowledge and behavior; Covenant governs organizational spaces and personnel; Church/HCI governs church spaces; Veil & Enemy governs supernatural influence.

## 3. Non-Invention Rule
Do not invent named cities, countries, districts, settlements, Covenant bases, chapels, landmarks, named civilians, demographic facts, political structures, maps, or regional history as established canon. Clearly label all proposals.

## 4. World Identity
BTV is a large interconnected/semi-open world containing cities, settlements, wilderness, facilities, districts, hidden locations, Covenant locations, chapels, and Veil-influenced areas. The world should feel inhabited rather than constructed only around missions.

## 5. Interconnected World
Regions should connect through believable geography, travel routes, infrastructure, social relationships, supply movement, and information networks. The world need not be seamless to be interconnected.

## 6. Regional Variation
Different regions may vary in architecture, climate, vegetation, density, occupations, clothing, transport, worship patterns, commerce, risks, and Veil influence. Variation must remain grounded rather than arbitrary.

## 7. Public-Space Population Rule
Public and community spaces should contain substantially more civilians, families, workers, visitors, worshippers, merchants, and ordinary residents than Covenant agents.

## 8. Covenant-Space Population Rule
Covenant facilities may contain more Covenant personnel, specialists, trainees, support staff, and operational activity. They should still feel like functional workplaces rather than unexplained military mazes.

## 9. Population Diversity
NPC populations should show natural variation in age, appearance, occupation, clothing, family structure, mobility, and background. Avoid visually repetitive populations or over-representation of one group.

## 10. Ordinary Life
People should work, trade, travel, worship, study, rest, socialize, care for families, volunteer, maintain spaces, seek help, celebrate, disagree, and respond to events. Ordinary life is part of the world, not filler.

## 11. NPCs as People
NPCs are people before they are quest dispensers. Their presence, dialogue, routines, relationships, needs, and knowledge should make sense independently of the player's objectives.

## 12. NPC Knowledge Boundaries
NPCs know only what their roles, experiences, location, relationships, and available information plausibly allow. No NPC automatically knows Covenant secrets, Veil truths, player history, or unseen events.

## 13. Rumors & Information
Rumors can be incomplete, biased, outdated, mistaken, or deliberately deceptive. Important information should have identifiable sources and opportunities for corroboration.

## 14. Families & Social Networks
Families, friendships, coworkers, congregations, teams, neighborhoods, and other relationships can create believable social networks. Do not generate arbitrary relationship webs solely to increase quest count.

## 15. NPC Routines
Routines should reflect occupation, time, location, responsibilities, safety conditions, worship schedules, weather, travel, and world events. Routines can be simplified for performance while preserving the appearance of life.

## 16. Schedule Flexibility
NPC behavior should allow reasonable variation rather than rigid loops. Emergencies, festivals, work changes, weather, services, investigations, and Veil incidents may alter routines.

## 17. World-State Awareness
NPC behavior can respond to meaningful world states such as danger, displacement, infrastructure disruption, shortages, public events, rescue operations, or local recovery. Responses should be specific rather than a universal morality score.

## 18. Crisis Population Behavior
During danger, civilians may flee, shelter, assist others, seek Covenant or community help, become separated from groups, communicate warnings, or remain unable to leave. Behavior should reflect context and individual circumstances.

## 19. No Constant War-Zone Rule
Peaceful settlements should not behave like permanent combat arenas. Threat density, civilian activity, commerce, worship, and daily life should change with actual world conditions.

## 20. Settlements
Settlements can include homes, businesses, gathering spaces, worship spaces, services, food, storage, work areas, public facilities, and local leadership. Exact settlement types and named locations remain OPEN.

## 21. Cities & Districts
Cities may contain distinct districts with different functions, populations, architecture, risks, economies, and rhythms. District identity should emerge from place and people rather than only UI labels.

## 22. Wilderness
Wilderness areas can contain travel routes, natural resources, wildlife, hidden locations, ruins, isolated communities, Covenant activity, and Veil influence. Wilderness should not exist solely as empty combat space.

## 23. Facilities
Facilities may include industrial, medical, educational, logistical, governmental, commercial, religious, research, residential, or Covenant-related spaces. Exact named facilities and layouts remain UNKNOWN unless established.

## 24. Hidden Locations
Hidden locations can reward observation, investigation, exploration, relationships, evidence, or persistence. They should not all be treasure rooms.

## 25. Infrastructure
Roads, paths, bridges, utilities, communications, transport, markets, water, power, and public services help make regions believable. Exact infrastructure systems remain open for technical design.

## 26. Travel
Travel should communicate distance, regional identity, changing conditions, and opportunities for discovery. Exact vehicles, fast-travel rules, and traversal systems remain OPEN.

## 27. Commerce
Markets and businesses should serve believable needs. Aces are general-world currency; Covenant Credits are Covenant-focused. Faith, salvation, prayer, divine favor, or spiritual worth are never commodities.

## 28. Services
NPCs may provide ordinary services such as food, repairs, transport, information, medical assistance, lodging, education, worship, or community support where appropriate. Specific vendors and mechanics remain OPEN.

## 29. Economy & Scarcity
World economies may reflect supply, demand, disruption, geography, occupation, and crisis conditions. Avoid arbitrary inflation or reward systems that exist only for gameplay convenience.

## 30. Community Support
Communities may organize aid, shelters, meals, volunteering, rescue, rebuilding, worship, or local protection. Generosity should not be reduced to a numerical righteousness score.

## 31. Church Spaces
Churches are living communities, not menus or passive scenery. Apply the Church/HCI/Service/Communion Bible for church-specific rules, service, giving, Communion, and HCI boundaries.

## 32. Covenant Presence
Covenant agents should appear where their responsibilities logically require them. Their presence should not overwhelm ordinary public life or make every community feel like a Covenant base.

## 33. Government & Institutions
Governmental and institutional structures may exist in the world, but their exact forms, names, jurisdictions, and relationships remain UNKNOWN unless established. Do not invent political canon.

## 34. Law & Public Order
Different places may have different levels of public order, emergency response, trust, or institutional capacity. Do not equate institutional weakness with moral inferiority.

## 35. World & Veil
Apply the Veil & Enemy Encyclopedia to supernatural influence. Most ordinary world spaces should remain understandable as places where people live, even when Veil events occur.

## 36. Environmental Storytelling
Architecture, objects, signage, wear, repairs, abandoned spaces, personal belongings, public notices, sounds, and routines can communicate history without exposition dumps.

## 37. Location Identity
A strong location should have a reason to exist, a visual identity, people who use it, activities, resources, risks, history or context, and relationships to nearby places. Named locations require canon status.

## 38. Dynamic Events
World events may include community gatherings, markets, services, accidents, rescues, disruptions, investigations, festivals, shortages, enemy incidents, or Veil anomalies. Events should have context and consequences.

## 39. Mission Integration
Missions should emerge from the world rather than making the world disappear when a mission ends. Rescue, investigation, outreach, restoration, community help, scouting, and battle operations can interact with locations and people.

## 40. Quest-Giver Boundary
Not every NPC needs a quest. Some people exist to inhabit the world, provide context, maintain relationships, or pursue their own lives.

## 41. Dialogue & Local Voice
Dialogue can reflect occupation, education, location, personality, stress, relationships, and knowledge. Regional distinction should not rely on caricature.

## 42. Children & Vulnerable Civilians
Children and vulnerable people may exist as ordinary members of communities, but their presentation and gameplay treatment must remain age-appropriate and non-exploitative. Avoid using vulnerable people merely as shock devices.

## 43. NPC Safety & Teen-Safe Presentation
Danger, grief, displacement, fear, and conflict may be meaningful parts of the world, but presentation should remain consistent with BTV's established teen-safe standard and avoid graphic gore or excessively gruesome depiction.

## 44. Population Density
Population density should vary naturally by location, time, function, and crisis level. Busy public spaces, quiet neighborhoods, workplaces, chapels, facilities, and wilderness should not all share the same density.

## 45. Performance-Aware Population
NPC simulation should be scalable for Android and PC. High-level routines, simulation zones, pooling, simplified distant behavior, and streaming may be considered, but technical implementation remains OPEN.

## 46. World Streaming & Loading
Large-world presentation may use streaming, regional loading, interiors, transitions, or other technical methods. Exact engine and architecture remain UNKNOWN.

## 47. Weather & Time
Time of day, weather, seasons, and environmental conditions may influence NPC routines, travel, visibility, commerce, worship, safety, and atmosphere. Exact calendar and weather systems remain OPEN.

## 48. Festivals & Public Gatherings
Festivals and major gatherings, including possible Easter or Christmas events, remain PROPOSED/OPEN. Do not establish schedules, rituals, or institutional details without creator approval.

## 49. Regional Chapel Variation
Church architecture and presentation can vary by region while preserving recognizable Christian purpose and avoiding imitation of one real institution's exact facilities.

## 50. World Population Composition Audit
Before finalizing a location, verify that its civilian-to-Covenant balance, occupations, family presence, services, routines, and environmental activity fit the location's function and crisis state.

## 51. World Proposal Template
For proposed locations/world systems record: name; type; region; purpose; population; occupations; architecture; services; routines; economy; travel links; threats; Veil influence; narrative role; missions; consequences; technical considerations; status; unknowns; canon audit.

## 52. NPC Proposal Template
For a proposed NPC record: name; role; location; age category; relationships; occupation; personality; goals; knowledge boundaries; routine; dialogue voice; story function; possible missions; consequences; visual continuity; status; unknowns; canon audit.

## 53. World Canon Audit
Major world, location, population, NPC, economy, travel, settlement, or regional features must be audited against creator decisions, Master Canon, Story & Lore, Character, Covenant, Faith/Prayer/Armor, Church/HCI, Veil/Enemy, originality, and no-invention rules.

## 54. Current World & NPC Unknown Register
UNKNOWN/open areas include world map, countries/cities, named districts and settlements, named chapels, Covenant bases, regional populations, named civilians, government structures, laws, economy simulation, transport, vehicles, weather/calendar, wildlife catalog, exact NPC simulation architecture, and regional histories.

## 55. Future Specialized Documents
Possible subordinate references include World Map & Regions Bible, Location Design Manual, NPC Simulation Specification, Economy & Commerce Bible, Travel & Transportation Bible, Wildlife & Environment Bible, Dynamic World Events Bible, and Architecture/Visual Identity Bible. These are roadmap possibilities, not current canon.

## 56. Final World Principle
BTV's world should feel inhabited even when the player is not present. People should have lives, places should have purpose, regions should have identity, and supernatural conflict should disturb an existing world rather than replace it.
