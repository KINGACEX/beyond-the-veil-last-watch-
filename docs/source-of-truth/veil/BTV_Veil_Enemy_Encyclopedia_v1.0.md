# BEYOND THE VEIL: THE LAST WATCH
## VEIL & ENEMY ENCYCLOPEDIA v1.0

**Document Type:** Approved Specialized BTV Reference (pending integration audit)

**Authority:** Subordinate to Creator decisions/corrections and Master Canon; domain-specialized alongside Story & Lore, Character, Covenant, Faith/Prayer/Armor, and Church/HCI/Service/Communion references.

**Status:** v1.0 — Initial Veil and Enemy reference. Major metaphysical, enemy-origin, hierarchy, and boss questions remain open.

### Non-Invention Rule
Do not invent the Veil's ultimate origin, final cosmology, named supernatural entities, definitive enemy origins, hidden history, boss identities, or supernatural rules. Clearly label all proposals.

## 1. Authority & Purpose
Specialized BTV reference for the Veil, Veil-influenced phenomena, enemy taxonomy, supernatural threat presentation, enemy intelligence, deception, commanders, bosses, and related investigation principles. It does not replace the Master Canon or other higher-authority documents.

## 2. Authority & Status
Creator decisions and corrections override all references. The Master Canon remains primary content authority. Story & Lore governs narrative interpretation; Character governs character-specific knowledge and behavior; Covenant governs organizational response; Faith/Prayer/Armor governs spiritual boundaries.

## 3. Non-Invention Rule
Do not invent the Veil's ultimate origin, cosmology, final metaphysical explanation, named supernatural entities, secret history, boss identities, or definitive supernatural rules unless explicitly established or clearly labeled PROPOSED.

## 4. Veil Core Identity
The Veil is a central supernatural/worldbuilding concept that influences locations, perception, enemies, information, environments, investigations, and events. It must remain distinct from a generic fantasy portal, interchangeable magic realm, or simple alternate dimension.

## 5. Veil Mystery
The Veil should support discovery without arbitrary mystery. The player may understand some effects while deeper causes remain uncertain. Evidence, experience, Scripture, counsel, investigation, and time can contribute to understanding.

## 6. Veil Rules
Veil phenomena should exhibit internally coherent rules. A mysterious event may be unexplained, but it should not behave randomly merely to force a plot outcome.

## 7. Veil Knowledge Layers
Knowledge may be divided among civilians, Covenant branches, specialists, researchers, enemies, witnesses, and the player. No group automatically understands the Veil completely.

## 8. Evidence & Uncertainty
Reports, observations, recordings, testimony, environmental clues, enemy behavior, and Scout Reports may contain incomplete or deceptive information. Confidence and uncertainty matter.

## 9. Veil Investigations
Veil investigations should involve observation, evidence gathering, questioning, environmental interpretation, pattern recognition, preparation, risk assessment, and decisions rather than simply locating and destroying an anomaly.

## 10. Veil-influenced Areas
Veil influence can alter atmosphere, perception, information reliability, enemy behavior, environmental hazards, or social conditions. Effects should serve the location and narrative context.

## 11. Veil & Ordinary World
Veil influence should coexist with ordinary life. Most public spaces should remain populated by civilians, families, workers, visitors, worshippers, and merchants rather than constantly feeling like supernatural battlefields.

## 12. Veil Escalation
Veil incidents may range from subtle anomalies and misinformation to dangerous manifestations and major crises. Escalation should have narrative and environmental logic.

## 13. Veil & Faith
BTV's Christian worldview shapes how characters interpret spiritual conflict, but the Veil must not become a replacement for God, Christ, Scripture, prayer, or the Holy Spirit.

## 14. Holy Spirit Boundary
The Holy Spirit is not a Veil detection device, combat resource, controllable power, or omniscient navigation system. Spiritual guidance remains distinct from game-system certainty.

## 15. Prayer Boundary
Prayer can occur during uncertainty and danger but is not a guaranteed mechanism for revealing Veil truths or solving anomalies.

## 16. Scripture Boundary
Scripture may inform character interpretation, dialogue, reflection, or narrative meaning. Do not fabricate Scripture or imply that every Veil event has an automatically revealed verse-based solution.

## 17. Veil & Ember
Ember has established abilities including Veil Sight, Guardian Instinct, Veil Energy, Tracking Sense, and Spirit Guidance. These do not establish the Veil's origin or Ember's exact nature, which remain UNKNOWN.

## 18. Ember Knowledge Limit
Ember should not become an omniscient Veil encyclopedia. Its abilities can reveal useful information while preserving uncertainty and discovery.

## 19. Enemy Taxonomy
The established primary enemy functions are Destroyers, Persuaders, Deceivers, and Commanders/Bosses. These describe story/game function and do not automatically define complete origin or metaphysical nature.

## 20. Destroyers
Destroyers are primarily direct-aggression threats. They create physical danger and may pressure the player, civilians, teammates, or objectives. Their exact species, origin, hierarchy, and variants remain open unless established.

## 21. Persuaders
Persuaders focus on influence, manipulation, temptation, pressure, corruption, or social/spiritual exploitation. They should not be reduced to a simple 'evil dialogue enemy' formula.

## 22. Deceivers
Deceivers focus on illusions, misinformation, false perception, ambiguity, misdirection, or ambush. Their deception should follow coherent rules and provide learnable clues.

## 23. Commanders & Bosses
Commanders coordinate threats and bosses should have narrative significance. A boss should not exist merely as a high-health enemy with larger numbers.

## 24. Enemy Intelligence
Enemies may possess different levels of awareness, planning, communication, adaptation, and knowledge. Do not give enemies information they could not plausibly possess.

## 25. Enemy Knowledge Boundaries
Enemy knowledge should have sources and limitations. Veil influence does not automatically grant omniscience.

## 26. Enemy Behavior
Enemy behavior should respond to objectives, environment, player actions, civilians, teammates, and threat conditions where appropriate. Enemies should not always choose the most convenient action for the plot.

## 27. Enemy Communication
Enemy communication may reveal intent, misinformation, hierarchy, fear, confidence, or internal disagreement. Exact languages, signals, and command systems remain OPEN.

## 28. Human Antagonism
Human antagonists may participate in BTV conflicts. Human opposition must not imply that every non-Covenant person or every unbeliever is evil. Motivations should be specific and human.

## 29. Spiritual Conflict
Spiritual conflict can involve deception, temptation, fear, corruption, despair, falsehood, pressure, and moral compromise. It is not simply a good-stat versus evil-stat system.

## 30. Physical / Psychological / Social Threats
Enemies may challenge the player physically, psychologically, socially, or spiritually. These dimensions can overlap but should remain appropriate to the game's teen-safe presentation.

## 31. No Graphic Horror Requirement
BTV can communicate danger, fear, loss, and supernatural threat without relying on graphic gore or excessively gruesome presentation.

## 32. Enemy Encounters
Encounters may support combat, dialogue, investigation, escape, protection, rescue, de-escalation, or tactical withdrawal depending on context. Not every threat must become a mandatory kill sequence.

## 33. Preaching / Dialogue Encounters
Some difficult encounters may allow dialogue, preaching, warning, de-escalation, or defense. These should not reduce people to conversion percentages or guarantee conversion.

## 34. Combat Boundary
Combat remains an important BTV activity, but combat should serve mission context, character stakes, and world logic rather than becoming the sole answer to every problem.

## 35. Civilian Protection
Enemy encounters should account for civilians where appropriate. Protecting people, evacuating areas, rescuing victims, and controlling escalation can be meaningful objectives.

## 36. Mission Objectives
Veil/enemy missions can involve rescue, investigation, evacuation, evidence collection, protection, scouting, outreach, restoration, containment, pursuit, or battle operations.

## 37. Scout Reports
Scout Reports may identify anomalies, missing people, suspicious activity, enemy movement, environmental changes, or uncertain reports. They should communicate confidence and limitations rather than magically revealing everything.

## 38. Deception & Counter-Deception
Countering deception can involve observation, corroboration, teammate input, evidence, patience, Scripture, counsel, environmental clues, or learning enemy patterns. No single universal detector should solve every deception.

## 39. Enemy Weaknesses
Weaknesses should emerge from enemy identity, behavior, context, evidence, or narrative discovery. Avoid arbitrary elemental weaknesses unless justified by established design.

## 40. Enemy Resistances
Resistances and immunities should have coherent reasons and should not simply be used to inflate difficulty.

## 41. Enemy Rewards
Enemy drops may include resources, clues, materials, artifacts, equipment, information, or access to special opportunities. Faith, salvation, divine favor, or spiritual worth must never become loot.

## 42. Enemy Scaling
Difficulty should not automatically rewrite enemy lore or grant arbitrary supernatural powers. Scaling systems, if used, must preserve enemy identity and narrative logic.

## 43. Enemy Variants
Variants may change tactics, environment, behavior, or presentation, but should remain recognizable within their established function. Named variants require appropriate status labeling.

## 44. Enemy Factions & Hierarchy
Enemy organizations, ranks, commanders, alliances, and internal structures remain OPEN unless explicitly established. Do not infer a universal hierarchy from the four functional categories.

## 45. Antichrist-Related Material
Antichrist-related themes may be considered as future story material, but no identity, historical role, timeline, organization, or theology beyond established canon is currently locked.

## 46. Seven Deadly Sins
The Seven Deadly Sins remain a proposed thematic direction, not seven established enemy entities. If developed, consequences and human/spiritual meaning should matter more than a simple monster roster.

## 47. Corruption & Influence
Corruption should be portrayed as a meaningful narrative/spiritual condition rather than a generic status effect that automatically determines morality or salvation.

## 48. Redemption & Enemy Change
Some human conflicts may allow repentance, reconciliation, escape, surrender, or restoration where story-appropriate. Enemy change should not be automatic or consequence-free.

## 49. Boss Design Principle
A major boss should connect to location, conflict, character stakes, enemy function, Veil behavior, or mission consequences. Its mechanics should express identity rather than only difficulty.

## 50. Veil Boss Unknowns
No named Veil bosses, final antagonist, ultimate Veil entity, or master hierarchy is currently canon. These remain UNKNOWN/OPEN.

## 51. Investigation & Discovery Loop
A strong Veil investigation can move through report → observation → evidence → interpretation → uncertainty → preparation → decision → consequence → updated understanding. This is a design framework, not a mandatory identical mission formula.

## 52. World Consequences
Veil and enemy activity may affect communities, infrastructure, trust, movement, information, resources, injuries, displacement, and future missions. Consequences should be specific rather than a universal morality score.

## 53. No Universal Morality Meter
BTV has no universal morality meter that numerically summarizes spiritual worth or goodness.

## 54. Enemy / Veil Proposal Template
For proposed content record: name; category; function; origin status; behavior; knowledge sources; abilities; weaknesses; environment; narrative purpose; mission use; civilian impact; theological boundary; visual/audio direction; rewards; status; open questions; canon audit.

## 55. Veil Canon Audit
Major Veil, enemy, boss, supernatural, or spiritual-conflict features must be audited against creator decisions, Master Canon, Story & Lore, Character, Covenant, Faith/Prayer/Armor, Church/HCI, originality, and no-invention rules.

## 56. Current Veil & Enemy Unknown Register
UNKNOWN/open areas include Veil origin and ultimate metaphysics; detailed rules; history; named entities; Ember's exact relationship to the Veil; enemy origins; enemy hierarchy; languages/communication; named commanders/bosses; Antichrist-related implementation; Seven Deadly Sins implementation; exact supernatural mechanics.

## 57. Future Specialized Documents
Possible subordinate references include Veil Rules & Metaphysics Bible, Enemy Combat Design Manual, Boss Design Bible, Investigation & Evidence Bible, Veil Visual/Audio Bible, Human Antagonist Bible, and Enemy AI/Behavior Specification. These are roadmap possibilities, not current canon.

## 58. Final Veil & Enemy Principle
The Veil should create mystery governed by coherent rules, while enemies should feel purposeful, intelligent within their limits, and narratively meaningful. BTV should challenge the player without turning spiritual truth into a numerical system or reducing every conflict to combat.
