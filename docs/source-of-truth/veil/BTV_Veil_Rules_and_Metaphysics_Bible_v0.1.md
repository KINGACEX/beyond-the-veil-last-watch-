## 0. Executive summary

The Veil is a central supernatural/worldbuilding concept that can influence locations, perception, enemies, information, environments, events and investigations. It must have a distinctive BTV identity and must not be treated as a generic portal, interchangeable magical dimension or convenient explanation for every unexplained event.

BTV already records six Veil incident stages:

1. Normal
2. Disturbed
3. Influenced
4. Veil-Touched
5. Veil Breach
6. Critical / Endgame-Level

The stages are an established framework in the current Changelog and vertical-slice specification. Their detailed thresholds, causes, mechanics and reversibility are not fully defined. This Bible therefore proposes classification and production rules without inventing those missing mechanics.

The design target is **coherent uncertainty**: characters and players can understand observable effects while deeper causes remain unresolved. A mystery may be difficult; it must not be arbitrary.

## 2. Confirmed foundation

The following are supported by existing BTV references:

- The Veil is central to BTV's supernatural/worldbuilding identity.
- It can influence locations, perception, enemies, information, environments, events and investigations.
- It should not be reduced to a generic portal or ordinary alternate dimension.
- Veil phenomena should exhibit internally coherent rules.
- Different groups may possess different knowledge; no group is automatically omniscient.
- Reports, observations, recordings, testimony, environmental clues and enemy behaviour may be incomplete or deceptive.
- Investigation should involve observation, evidence, questioning, environmental interpretation, pattern recognition, preparation, risk assessment and decisions.
- Veil influence can coexist with ordinary life; most public spaces should remain recognisably inhabited.
- Escalation should have narrative and environmental logic.
- Ember has established ability concepts: Veil Sight, Guardian Instinct, Veil Energy, Tracking Sense and Spirit Guidance. These do not establish Ember's origin or the Veil's origin.
- Primary enemy functions include Destroyers, Persuaders, Deceivers and Commanders/Bosses. These are functional categories, not a complete species or cosmological hierarchy.

## 3. Metaphysical boundaries and unresolved questions

### 3.1 Explicitly UNKNOWN

Do not decide without creator approval and source reconciliation:

- What the Veil ultimately is in metaphysical terms.
- Whether it is a boundary, condition, phenomenon, domain, created order, spiritual reality or another concept.
- Its origin, creator, first appearance or ultimate purpose.
- Its complete relationship to the physical world and spiritual conflict.
- Whether it has a single centre, source, governing intelligence or hierarchy.
- Whether named supernatural beings originate from it, use it, influence it or merely interact with it.
- Its complete limits, laws, failure conditions and end-state.
- Its complete chronology and hidden history.
- Ember's species, origin and exact relationship to it.
- The identity of any ultimate antagonist or final Veil entity.

The game may present competing in-world hypotheses, but must label them as hypotheses and avoid implying that the game has confirmed them.

### 3.2 Christian worldview boundaries — CONFIRMED

- God is the ultimate authority.
- Christ is above the Covenant and the Chosen.
- The Chosen is human and does not replace Christ.
- The Holy Spirit is not a player-controlled attack resource, Veil detector, omniscient navigation system or controllable mechanic.
- Prayer is meaningful but is not a transaction, guaranteed buff or guaranteed method of solving a Veil incident.
- Scripture must not be fabricated or treated as a universal code that automatically solves every supernatural event.
- The Covenant does not command God, control God or replace the Church.

The Veil must not be elevated into a rival deity, an equal opposite to God, or a replacement explanation for Christian theology. BTV must distinguish fictional supernatural phenomena from claims about the nature of God.

## 4. Operational model: describe effects before causes

**PROPOSED DESIGN RULE:** For every Veil incident, designers must first document what can be observed, then document possible explanations separately.

Every incident record should contain:

1. **Observed event:** What can a player or witness directly perceive?
2. **Evidence:** What physical, recorded, environmental or testimonial evidence exists?
3. **Reliability:** How reliable is each source and why?
4. **Repeatability:** Has the effect occurred more than once under comparable conditions?
5. **Context:** What was happening in the location before and during the incident?
6. **Impact:** Who or what was affected?
7. **Known limitations:** What does the evidence not establish?
8. **Interpretations:** Which explanations are proposed by which characters?
9. **Verified conclusion:** What can the player reasonably conclude by the end of this mission or story arc?
10. **Unresolved questions:** What remains genuinely unknown?

Never write an omniscient lore statement merely because one character believes it.

## 5. Six-stage incident framework

The six stages are recorded in existing BTV references. This section proposes how production teams should use the labels without claiming to have established their underlying metaphysics.

| Stage | Existing label | Safe production interpretation | What must not be assumed |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | Normal | No verified active Veil incident is currently classified at this location. Ordinary life and ordinary explanations remain the baseline. | “Normal” does not prove that no hidden event has ever occurred. |
| 1 | Disturbed | A credible anomaly or irregularity has been reported or observed, but its nature and cause are unresolved. | Do not assume a breach, hostile presence or supernatural cause from one report. |
| 2 | Influenced | Evidence supports a continuing effect on some aspect of the location, information, perception, behaviour or environment. | Do not assume all people or systems are affected equally. |
| 3 | Veil-Touched | A more direct or persistent Veil-related manifestation is suspected or evidenced. | Do not invent a universal symptom, mark, mutation or ability. |
| 4 | Veil Breach | The incident is classified as a major boundary-related crisis in the existing terminology. | Do not define the boundary's precise nature or claim every breach behaves identically. |
| 5 | Critical / Endgame-Level | A highest-severity story classification reserved for major crises. | Do not make this a routine enemy level, player power level or automatic apocalypse switch. |

### 5.1 Classification discipline

- A stage is an incident/location classification, not a moral judgement.
- It is not a measure of a person's spiritual condition.
- It is not a player level, XP tier or reward multiplier.
- It should be assigned using documented evidence and authorised in-world assessment.
- The player-facing UI may communicate confidence and uncertainty rather than presenting a disputed classification as fact.
- A stage change requires a documented cause in the mission design, even if the player does not yet know that cause.
- Do not assume stages are always linear, irreversible or universally measurable. These behaviours remain UNKNOWN until approved.

### 5.2 Stage transition register — OPEN

The following must be defined in a future approved technical/narrative ruleset:

- What evidence is sufficient to move from Normal to Disturbed?
- Who can classify an incident and what authority do they have?
- Can an incident be reclassified downward?
- Can two observers reasonably disagree about a stage?
- Can separate zones within one location have different stages?
- What separates Influenced from Veil-Touched in observable terms?
- What exactly constitutes a Veil Breach?
- What makes Critical / Endgame-Level distinct from a severe local incident?

Until answered, use stages as editorial classifications, not hard gameplay mechanics.

## 6. Proposed rules for coherent Veil phenomena

The following are **PROPOSED design constraints**, not established supernatural laws.

### Rule P1 — Causal consistency
A phenomenon must have a defined cause in the writer's private incident design, even if characters cannot identify it. Do not change the cause to rescue a plot scene.

### Rule P2 — Observable footprint
A significant phenomenon should leave at least one interpretable trace: environmental, testimonial, recorded, behavioural or otherwise discoverable. A trace need not be obvious or conclusive.

### Rule P3 — Bounded effect
For each incident, define what is affected, where, when, for how long, and what is not affected. Avoid unlimited powers that can do anything whenever the plot needs them.

### Rule P4 — Information provenance
Every important claim must have a source. A witness may be mistaken; a recording may be incomplete; an official report may contain an inference. The source and its limits should be traceable.

### Rule P5 — No universal detector
No ability, character, device or dialogue option should reveal every Veil phenomenon or explain every deception. This is consistent with the existing counter-deception principle.

### Rule P6 — No arbitrary escalation
Escalation must follow established incident logic, character action, environmental conditions or a disclosed story cause. It must not happen solely because the player has reached a convenient dramatic moment.

### Rule P7 — Ordinary explanations remain possible
Not every strange event is Veil-related. Ordinary causes must remain available when evidence supports them.

### Rule P8 — Consequences persist appropriately
A resolved incident may leave social, infrastructural, emotional, investigative or mission consequences. Resolution must not automatically reset the community to an unchanged state.

### Rule P9 — Human agency remains meaningful
Veil influence must not erase all human responsibility. Characters retain motives, choices and limitations unless a specific, approved story establishes otherwise.

### Rule P10 — No lore by convenience
Do not create a new Veil power, entity or rule solely to explain a clue after the fact. Seed it earlier or mark it as a genuinely new discovery with supporting evidence.

## 7. Five-revelation narrative architecture

This is a proposed story pacing framework. It is independent of the six incident stages.

1. **The Signs:** The player encounters apparently unrelated irregularities and learns to distinguish reports from verified observations.
2. **The Pattern:** Investigations reveal recurring characteristics. The player learns to compare evidence without assuming identical causes.
3. **The Influence:** The player encounters credible effects on perception, information, environments or behaviour. The exact mechanism remains unresolved.
4. **The Competing Understandings:** Characters and organisations offer incomplete or conflicting interpretations. The player tests claims against evidence.
5. **The Arc Revelation:** A specific mystery is resolved with a defensible answer and consequences. This does not automatically disclose the Veil's ultimate origin.

Every major story arc should specify:
- The question introduced.
- The answer promised to the player.
- The evidence available at each revelation.
- The intended player inference.
- The confirmed answer for the writers.
- What characters know at each point.
- What remains unresolved after the payoff.

Do not indefinitely defer an answer that the story has clearly promised.

## 8. Knowledge and epistemic model

Every clue, report and dialogue claim should use one of these categories:

- **Verified fact:** supported by authoritative, corroborated evidence within the story.
- **Direct observation:** what a character personally perceived; not automatically an accurate interpretation.
- **Reported testimony:** what another person claims happened.
- **Inference:** a conclusion drawn from available evidence.
- **Belief:** a character's sincerely held interpretation.
- **Speculation:** a possibility without sufficient evidence.
- **Unknown:** not established.
- **Deliberate deception:** a claim knowingly presented as false; use only when the writer has established motive and source.

Characters must have plausible access to the information they possess. Knowledge must not spread magically between NPCs without a communication or observation path.

### 8.1 Character knowledge record

For each important NPC, record:
- What they know.
- How they know it.
- When they learned it.
- What they believe.
- What they misunderstand.
- What they have not been told.
- What they are willing to disclose.
- Whether their account is reliable and why.

### 8.2 NLI safety and integrity

The NLI model may interpret player language and propose typed dialogue or action intents. It must not:
- invent evidence or lore;
- fabricate Scripture or citations;
- claim an NPC observed an event they did not observe;
- reveal information outside that NPC's knowledge permissions;
- directly mutate authoritative mission or world state;
- execute arbitrary code or unrestricted engine commands;
- claim an action succeeded before the deterministic game system confirms the result.

When uncertain, the NPC should ask for clarification, state what they do not know, or offer a clearly labelled opinion.

## 9. Ember boundaries

**CONFIRMED:** Ember has the established ability concepts Veil Sight, Guardian Instinct, Veil Energy, Tracking Sense and Spirit Guidance. Ember is not an omniscient encyclopedia. Its species and origin remain UNKNOWN.

**PROPOSED implementation constraints:**
- Each ability should have a defined scope, activation context, information output and limitation before implementation.
- An ability may reveal an observation without revealing its cause.
- Different abilities should not all return the same generic “Veil detected” answer.
- Ember's response should be grounded in what Ember can plausibly perceive or infer.
- The player should retain responsibility for interpreting evidence.
- Spirit Guidance must not be presented as a controllable Holy Spirit mechanic or guaranteed divine instruction.

Ability-level mechanics remain OPEN and require separate design approval.

## 10. Enemy relationship boundaries

The established categories describe function:

- **Destroyers:** direct-aggression threats.
- **Persuaders:** influence, manipulation, temptation, pressure or exploitation.
- **Deceivers:** illusions, misinformation, false perception, confusion, misdirection or ambush.
- **Commanders/Bosses:** coordination and major narrative threats.

Do not infer that these categories are species, ranks, a universal hierarchy or the complete metaphysical structure of the Veil. Enemy origins, named entities, languages, factions and ultimate hierarchy remain UNKNOWN unless separately established.

Enemies must have knowledge sources and limitations. Veil influence does not automatically grant omniscience.

## 11. Environmental, sensory and presentation rules

**PROPOSED production standards:**

- Use ordinary-world baseline presentation before adding anomalies. Contrast makes changes legible.
- Each location should have a specific identity; do not reuse the same fog, static, red lighting and distorted audio package everywhere.
- Visual, audio and interaction clues should reinforce one another without requiring every clue to be obvious.
- Avoid visual effects that imply an unapproved metaphysical explanation.
- Use environmental details to communicate local human consequences, not merely supernatural spectacle.
- Accessibility options should support subtitle clarity, audio cue alternatives, contrast, text scaling and non-colour-only clue communication.
- The game should communicate danger and uncertainty without requiring graphic gore or excessively gruesome imagery.

A future Veil Visual/Audio Bible should define palettes, motifs, sound signatures, camera rules, accessibility equivalents and performance budgets. None are locked here.

## 12. Investigation and player-facing UI

Recommended evidence journal fields:
- Item or testimony name.
- Source and discovery location.
- Time/context if known.
- Direct observation.
- Reliability notes.
- Related evidence.
- Contradictions.
- Player interpretation (if supported by the authored system).
- Current confidence label.
- Essential/optional designation.

Recommended confidence labels: **Unverified**, **Supported**, **Corroborated**, **Disputed**, **Disconfirmed**. These are proposed UI labels, not canon terms.

Do not display false numerical precision such as “93% supernatural certainty” unless the game has a defensible, diegetic reason and the user experience genuinely benefits. Prefer plain-language confidence and source attribution.

Essential progress should not be gated by one obscure optional clue. If the player misses an optional clue, the main story should remain understandable, though less richly informed.

## 14. Acceptance criteria

A Veil incident is ready for implementation only when:

1. Its writer-only underlying facts are documented.
2. Its observable effects are separated from interpretation.
3. Its incident stage has a defensible classification or is explicitly unclassified.
4. Its boundaries and limitations are documented.
5. Every important claim has a plausible source.
6. Character knowledge permissions are recorded.
7. Clues support the intended inference without requiring arbitrary leaps.
8. Contradictions are intentional and explainable.
9. The player can make progress without one obscure optional clue.
10. The incident does not turn the Veil into a universal explanation or morality detector.
11. It respects the Christian worldview boundaries in the Master Canon and Faith/Prayer/Armor Bible.
12. It does not establish UNKNOWN metaphysics or enemy origins by accident.
13. NLI dialogue cannot invent facts or bypass deterministic validation.
14. Visual/audio clues have accessibility alternatives where required.
15. Consequences are documented and testable.
