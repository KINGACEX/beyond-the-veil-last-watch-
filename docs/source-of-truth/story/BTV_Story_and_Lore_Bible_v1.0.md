# 0. PURPOSE

This document defines the current narrative and lore framework of BTV.

It does **not** invent missing history, characters, cities, Veil metaphysics, chronology, or endings.

Where the Master Canon establishes a principle but not a specific story fact, this document preserves that distinction.

The purpose is to give future story development a stable foundation while leaving deliberately unresolved material available for creator decisions.

---

# 1. STORY IDENTITY

**CANON**

Beyond the Veil: The Last Watch is an original modern Christian action-adventure survival RPG.

Its narrative combines:
- personal stakes,
- community consequences,
- spiritual conflict,
- mystery,
- action,
- investigation,
- leadership,
- service,
- survival,
- and redemption.

The Christian worldview is integrated into the story rather than appearing only as decoration.

BTV is not a direct retelling of the Bible.

It is an original fictional world operating within a Christian worldview.

---

# 2. NARRATIVE PROMISE

BTV should make the player feel that they are participating in a living struggle in which ordinary human life continues alongside a deeper spiritual conflict.

The story should be able to move naturally between:
- ordinary community life,
- Covenant operations,
- investigation,
- danger,
- spiritual uncertainty,
- human relationships,
- church/community spaces,
- Veil phenomena,
- rescue,
- confrontation,
- and restoration.

The narrative should not make every scene equally intense.

Quiet moments are important because they give meaning to danger.

---

# 3. CORE NARRATIVE THEMES

**CANON THEMES**

The story may repeatedly explore:

### Truth vs. deception
People may struggle to distinguish what is true from what appears true.

### Faith vs. fear
Characters may be forced to act without possessing complete certainty.

### Light vs. darkness
A central thematic contrast in BTV.

### Spiritual warfare
The conflict extends beyond ordinary physical threats.

### Service
Strength should be connected to responsibility and helping others.

### Sacrifice
Important choices may involve personal cost.

### Compassion
People should not be reduced to objectives, enemies, or statistics.

### Redemption
People can change, while consequences still matter.

### Restoration
Repairing people and communities can be as important as defeating threats.

### Discernment
The player should sometimes need to investigate before acting.

### Responsibility
Greater authority should create greater responsibility rather than merely greater power.

---

# 4. CHRISTIAN WORLDVIEW BOUNDARY

**CANON**

God is the ultimate authority.

Christ is above the Covenant and the Chosen.

The Chosen is human.

The Holy Spirit is not a player-controlled combat resource.

Prayer is not a transaction.

Faith is not a purchasable commodity.

The story must preserve these distinctions.

BTV should not accidentally imply that:
- the Covenant is equivalent to the Church,
- the Chosen is equivalent to Christ,
- spiritual authority can be purchased,
- divine action can be mechanically forced,
- or human rank equals spiritual superiority.

# 6. THE COVENANT AS A STORY FORCE

**CANON**

The Covenant is a hidden organization serving God and protecting people.

It is not:
- a government agency,
- the entirety of Christianity,
- the Church,
- Christ,
- or God.

The Covenant gives BTV a framework for:
- field operations,
- rescue,
- investigation,
- intelligence,
- outreach,
- protection,
- logistics,
- and leadership.

Its presence should never erase ordinary society.

# 11. VEIL STORYTELLING PRINCIPLE

The Veil should create uncertainty without making the story meaningless.

When a strange event occurs, the player should have reason to:
- investigate,
- observe,
- compare evidence,
- seek information,
- use discernment,
- consult teammates,
- or act cautiously.

The narrative should avoid treating every unexplained event as automatically understood.

# 12. ENEMY STORY FUNCTIONS

## Destroyers

**CANON**

Primarily direct physical threats.

Their narrative role may involve:
- violence,
- destruction,
- danger,
- territorial threats,
- or attacks on people and communities.

Specific origins and individual identities remain open.

## Persuaders

**CANON**

Associated with:
- manipulation,
- influence,
- temptation,
- persuasion,
- and deceptive social pressure.

Their conflicts can occur through people and communities rather than only combat arenas.

## Deceivers

**CANON**

Associated with:
- illusions,
- misinformation,
- false perception,
- confusion,
- and ambush.

Their story function can make information itself part of the danger.

## Commanders / Bosses

**CANON**

Higher-level threats may coordinate other enemies.

Major bosses should have narrative significance.

A boss should ideally represent or advance something meaningful in the story rather than simply provide a difficulty spike.

# 13. HUMAN ANTAGONISM

**CANON DIRECTION**

BTV can contain conflicts involving ordinary people, communities, institutions, or corrupted leadership.

However, the game should avoid automatically equating:
- disagreement with evil,
- weakness with wickedness,
- poverty with moral failure,
- wealth with righteousness,
- or a person's mistakes with permanent condemnation.

Specific human antagonists are not yet canonized.

# 14. SOCIAL AND SPIRITUAL CONFLICT

**CANON DIRECTION**

- dialogue,
- preaching,
- de-escalation,
- investigation,
- defense,
- retreat,

Before creating major story content:

**Canon → Story Design → System/Quest Dependencies → Implementation → Testing → Canon Audit → Documentation**

Do not create large amounts of lore first and attempt to retrofit it into the game later.
