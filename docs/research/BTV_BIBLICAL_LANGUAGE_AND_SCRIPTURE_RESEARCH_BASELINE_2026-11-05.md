# Biblical Language and Scripture Research Baseline

**Date:** 5 November 2026  
**Status:** RESEARCH BASELINE — not canon, not gameplay implementation  
**Separation rule:** Language learning and textual research remain optional learning/research. They do not become supernatural abilities, spells, passwords, prayer shortcuts, progression gates or measures of faith.

## 1. Language boundaries

- **Biblical Hebrew:** A Northwest Semitic language represented across the Hebrew Bible, with historical and stylistic variation. Study morphology, syntax, lexicon and textual context rather than treating a one-word gloss as a complete interpretation.
- **Biblical Aramaic:** A distinct but closely related Northwest Semitic language, appearing in limited portions of the Hebrew Bible (notably sections of Ezra and Daniel). Do not label every Old Testament passage as Hebrew.
- **Koine Greek:** The common Greek of the Hellenistic and Roman periods; the New Testament is predominantly composed in Greek. Distinguish New Testament Koine from Classical Greek and later Byzantine/Modern Greek.

## 2. Research method

For each passage or term, record:
1. Canonical passage and literary context.
2. Original-language form and morphology.
3. Lexical range supported by a recognized lexicon.
4. Syntax and discourse role.
5. Textual variants where relevant.
6. Translation comparison and translator's note.
7. Confidence and unresolved scholarly disagreement.

Do not derive doctrine from etymology alone, isolate a single gloss from context, or claim that a Greek/Hebrew word has one hidden English equivalent. Compare lexicons, grammar and passage context.

## 3. Resource baseline

- Yale Divinity Library's Biblical Studies guide catalogs standard Hebrew lexicons (BDB, HALOT), grammars (Joüon–Muraoka; Waltke–O'Connor), Greek reference grammars and the Comprehensive Aramaic Lexicon.
- Yale's New Testament language guide points to Greek lexicons and describes the value of lexical evidence and grammatical forms.
- University of Michigan's guide identifies HALOT, BDB, DCH and related Hebrew/Aramaic reference works.
- United Bible Societies provides a Semantic Dictionary of Biblical Hebrew-derived resource with glosses, references and semantic domains; it states that the dictionary is ongoing and currently covers around 90% of Old Testament vocabulary.
- United Bible Societies provides a Greek New Testament dictionary derived from the Semantic Dictionary of the Greek New Testament and Louw–Nida, with lexical meanings and Scripture references.

## 5. Initial research questions (separate lesson tracks)

### Biblical Hebrew
- Alphabet, vowel points and reading direction.
- Noun gender/number, construct relationships and article.
- Verb roots, stems (binyanim), aspect and clause syntax.
- Psalm poetry, parallelism and semantic range.

### Biblical Aramaic
- Script and relationship to Imperial Aramaic.
- Basic morphology and syntax.
- Reading samples from Ezra and Daniel with passage-level notes.
- Distinguishing Aramaic forms from Hebrew cognates.

### Koine Greek
- Alphabet and pronunciation conventions.
- Noun cases, article, gender and number.
- Present/aorist/perfect systems and aspect-sensitive interpretation.
- Participles, infinitives, clauses and discourse.
- Reading Philippians in context rather than building word studies from isolated slogans.
